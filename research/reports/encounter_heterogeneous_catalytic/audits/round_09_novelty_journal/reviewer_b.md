## 3. B1 findings

### B1-01 — the closest fixed-total-reactivity spatial-design prior art is absent

The present abstract and novelty section say that the increment is the
"fixed-transport connection at a spatial-reactivity fold," but they do not
acknowledge that static spatial killing and its redistribution at fixed total
reactivity/absorbance have already been studied directly.

Two omissions materially change the novelty framing:

1. **Nguyen & Grebenkov 2010** formulate a bounded diffusion-reaction
   equation with a heterogeneous bulk rate \(B(\mathbf r)\), obtain the full
   survival-time law, and explicitly ask which shape and arrangement is best
   for a fixed total amount or strength of reactive regions. Their reported
   optimization focuses on the long-time rate, not density modality.
2. **Scott et al. 2021** analyze partially reactive edges scattered through a
   network and explicitly study how the spatial distribution of a fixed total
   absorbance affects reaction kinetics. They optimize mean reaction times,
   not the number of modes.

These papers do not destroy the modality-fold contribution, but they mean
that neither "static spatial reactivity under a fixed total budget" nor its
optimization can be presented as new. Before submission, both must be added
to the introduction and the relation-to-prior-work section. The manuscript
must state the difference in observable and theorem:

- prior work: survival law, asymptotic rate, MFPT, and spatial optimization;
- present work: a density critical-point bifurcation under the matched
  two-mobile-reactant finite-radius Doi experiment.

**Required disposition:** add both citations and rewrite the novelty paragraph.
Without this comparison, Round 09 fails even though the underlying narrow
claim survives.

### B1-02 — reactivity-generated multimodality is broader than the one Grebenkov example currently cited

The manuscript correctly cites Grebenkov 2020 for encounter-history-induced
mono-to-bimodal reaction times. It nevertheless presents the landscape too
narrowly when the abstract contrasts transport structure only with
"encounter-history-dependent reactivity," and when the introduction uses
Grebenkov as the sole reactivity-law example.

Scher & Reuveni provide a distinct, direct precedent:

- the continuous-time network theory states that internal-state gating can
  make the reaction-time distribution multimodal
  ([PRL DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.018301),
  [full arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05124));
- the discrete-time JCP paper explicitly reports a **bi-modal reaction-time
  distribution** and attributes the two peaks to particles that reach and
  react before switching versus those delayed by a nonreactive period
  ([JCP DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0072393),
  [full arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05593)).

This is not encounter-local-time activation and is not static spatial
patterning. It therefore strengthens, rather than eliminates, the paper's
mechanism contrast. But omitting it leaves a central novelty statement
incomplete.

**Required disposition:** cite both gated-reaction papers and replace the
two-way taxonomy by at least three mechanisms:

1. transport geometry, bias, persistence, disorder, or competing routes;
2. temporal/internal-state or encounter-history-dependent reaction laws; and
3. the present time-independent spatial redistribution at fixed transport and
   fixed integrated killing.

## 4. B2 findings

### B2-01 — the Luca/Giuggioli relationship is directionally correct but two mechanisms are grouped too broadly

It is accurate to call this paper a direct extension of Luca Giuggioli's
encounter and heterogeneous-transmission program. That program already
supplies:

- encounter times for two walkers in overlapping domains:
  [10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.058103](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.058103);
- exact propagators in arbitrary dimensions:
  [10.1103/PhysRevX.10.021045](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.10.021045);
- heterogeneous, site-dependent encounter transmission and partial
  absorption:
  [10.1088/1751-8121/ac8587](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ac8587);
- transport in inert spatial heterogeneity:
  [10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.043281](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.5.043281);
- biased/periodic bimodal first-passage probabilities:
  [10.1103/PhysRevE.102.062124](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.102.062124);
- persistence-generated multimodality:
  [10.1088/1367-2630/ad5d85](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/ad5d85);
- multiple first-passage peaks from biased target routes:
  [10.1007/978-3-031-67802-8_5](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-67802-8_5);
  and
- small-world disorder/direct-intermediate-indirect routes in the current
  preprint [arXiv:2508.10140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10140).

Two current phrases need precision:

- In Das & Giuggioli 2022
  ([10.1088/1751-8121/ac9765](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ac9765)),
  the periodic biased reset-free law already has a bimodal structure;
  resetting changes the relative contributions and creates non-monotonic
  dependence. "Resetting-generated multiple peaks" is therefore too strong.
- Barbini & Giuggioli 2024
  ([10.1088/1751-8121/ad7ca2](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ad7ca2))
  establishes a non-monotonic dependence of the first-passage probability
  **mode location** on bias in heterogeneous space. It is not, on the evidence
  checked, a theorem that inert heterogeneity creates or destroys density
  modes.

A safe replacement for the grouped sentence is:

> Bias in periodic domains, persistence, competing target routes, and
> small-world disorder generate multiple first-passage peaks, while resetting
> and inert heterogeneity reshape peak weights or the location of the
> first-passage mode.

The core relationship to Luca should then be stated as follows: Luca's work
provides the encounter propagator, heterogeneous-transmission, and
transport-generated clock structure; the present paper adds a fixed-budget
**static reaction-field modality fold**, not heterogeneous encounters or
Green reduction themselves.

### B2-02 — the Godec 2016/2017 attribution conflates two different channel notions

The sentence "Direct/indirect two-channel first-passage kinetics is explicit
in the work of Godec and Metzler" cites both 2016 and 2017 as if they were the
same construction.

- Godec & Metzler 2016
  ([10.1103/PhysRevX.6.041037](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.6.041037))
  explicitly separates direct and indirect target-search trajectories.
- Godec & Metzler 2017
  ([10.1088/1751-8121/aa5204](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/aa5204))
  is a two-channel recognition/nonrecognition diffusion model.
- Grebenkov, Metzler & Oshanin 2018
  ([10.1038/s42004-018-0096-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-018-0096-x))
  is the closest source for a partial-reaction full density with a fast
  geometry-controlled hump and late reaction/equilibration-controlled
  plateau/tail.

**Required caveat:** distinguish trajectory channels from internal-state
channels and add the 2018 paper to the discussion of early/late clocks.

### B2-04 — the journal note's domestic-partition section is now outdated

The journal-target note says the current CAS partition remains to be queried.
That is no longer the correct 2026 administrative framing. On 2026-03-27, the
Chinese Academy of Sciences National Science Library stated officially that,
starting in 2026, it will no longer update or publish the journal partition
table, and that partition tables published by other institutions are
unrelated to it:

- official statement:
  <https://www.las.cas.cn/news/tzgg/202603/t20260327_8178738.html>

Therefore:

1. there is no official "2026 中科院期刊分区表" to report;
2. any "新锐分区" or successor issued elsewhere must not be labelled as a
   CAS National Science Library partition;
3. a historical statement must name the exact edition, normally the
   institutionally archived 2025 final annual edition, rather than saying
   merely "中科院几区"; and
4. the user's institution must decide whether 2026+ outputs carry forward
   the 2025 record or use another scheme.

I did **not** verify exact historical zones for JCP, PRE, or PRResearch because
the official historical records require an authenticated query. Third-party
zone labels are not acceptable evidence.

## 5. B3 metadata and terminology audit

### B3-01 — optional: identify the finite CTMC law as phase-type

All DOI entries presently in `references.bib` resolved to titles, journals,
years, and authors consistent with the bibliography. The DOI
[10.48550/arXiv.2508.10140](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.10140)
still resolves to a preprint rather than a journal article as of the audit
date, so the manuscript's "currently a preprint" wording is correct.

For optional hardening, call the finite killed-CTMC absorption law a
**phase-type distribution**. Generic finite CTMCs can approximate highly
varied positive-time densities, so finite-state trimodality is not itself a
novel class of probability law. The manuscript already limits the result to a
bounded mechanism certificate; adding the standard term would make that
boundary even clearer.

## 6. Submission-safe novelty wording

The current narrow claim survives, but it should explicitly exclude the two
newly identified prior-art classes. A safe English replacement is:

> Spatially heterogeneous bulk killing, optimization under a fixed total
> reactivity or absorbance, site-dependent encounter transmission, and
> reaction-time multimodality are established. In particular, static
> heterogeneous killing has been optimized at fixed total strength, while
> encounter-history activation and stochastic reactive-state gating can
> generate bimodal or multimodal reaction-time laws. Our contribution is
> narrower: for two mobile reactants with finite-radius Doi killing, we keep
> the transport generator and discrete integrated killing fixed, redistribute
> only a time-independent spatial killing field, and locate a nondegenerate
> density-modality fold through \(f_t=f_{tt}=0\) and physical-path
> transversality. The present two-dimensional evidence is finite-grid and is
> not a continuum fold theorem.

The abstract's opening can be made equally safe with:

> Multimodal first-passage and reaction-time densities can arise from
> transport structure, stochastic reactive-state gating, or
> encounter-history-dependent reactivity. We ask instead whether a
> time-independent spatial Doi field can cross a derivative-certified
> modality boundary when transport and integrated killing are fixed.
