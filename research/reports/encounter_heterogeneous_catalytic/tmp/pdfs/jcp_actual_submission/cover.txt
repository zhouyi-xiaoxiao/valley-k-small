    Dear Editors,
    I would like to submit "Spectral diagnostics and local fixed-budget sensitivity of critical points
in finite encounter-reaction models" for consideration as a Regular Article in The Journal of
Chemical Physics.
    The paper starts from a practical question in stochastic reaction kinetics: if the total amount
of reactivity available to a pair of diffusing reactants is fixed, what can rearranging that reactivity
in space actually do to the shape of the reaction-time distribution? Multi-peaked reaction-time
densities are by now well documented, and it is tempting to read each peak as a mechanism.
What has been missing is a calculus that says, for a declared finite model, whether the observed
critical points are spectrally permitted at all, how they respond to an infinitesimal redistribution
of the killing field, and which feasible redistribution moves them fastest at fixed budget.
    The article builds that calculus in three exact steps: a reaction-support Green–Woodbury
reduction that separates transport from killing, a generalized-Descartes sign gate on the ordered
residues (with the reversibility hypothesis verified directly for both generators rather than
assumed), and a Frechet–Duhamel response identity projected onto the budget tangent space.
Two finite families then put the calculus to work, and both produce nondegenerate reaction-
density folds carrying the normal-form 1/2 and 3/2 exponents; the encounter chain reproduces
the normal-form amplitude to better than one percent.
    The result I most want a referee to test is the last one. The finite-grid folds sit inside the
physical control interval, and it would have been easy to report them as continuum predictions.
Instead, an exact fold-transfer theory, a cell-averaged refinement ladder run to 33×33 nodes under
two independent transport discretizations (upwind and Scharfetter–Gummel), and a 2 × 107 -
walker lattice-free Brownian realization with Doi killing all say the same thing: the physical
matched-budget path carries no interior continuum fold. The finite-grid critical controls are
discretization artifacts, and the organizing fold sits at the admissibility edge of the redistribution
family, where the far channel shuts off entirely. At fixed budget, spatial redistribution tunes the
two arrival-time scales; it cannot merge them. The refinement-ladder and off-lattice production
runs were carried out on the Isambard-AI facility and are acknowledged in the article.
    The paper is deliberately explicit about its machinery: complete operator-level proofs, per-grid
budget audits, refinement-ladder root tables, and stochastic validation gates are collected in the
supplementary material, and the exact algebraic components are machine-checked in Lean 4 with
the verification scope stated precisely there. I hope this is the kind of technical completeness that
readers working on stochastic reaction–diffusion methods, reaction–diffusion master equation
convergence, and imperfect-reactivity models will find genuinely usable.
    Disclosure of related work: a separate manuscript by the same author, "Geometry-controlled
folds of first-passage-time bimodality under localized absorption," is under review at Physical
Review E (submitted 30 July 2026). It concerns localized absorption of a single-particle first-
passage law on an interval and on finite networks. The present paper concerns two-particle
encounter reactions, a spectral necessary condition, a budget-constrained sensitivity calculus,
and a discrete-to-continuum transfer study; no numerical evidence, model, or theorem overlaps
between the two manuscripts.
    The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, and I have no conflicts of interest to
declare.
