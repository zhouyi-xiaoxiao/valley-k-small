Geometry-controlled folds of first-passage-time bimodality under localized absorption

                                                       Xiaoxiao Zhouyi∗
                                    School of Engineering Mathematics and Technology,
                                  University of Bristol, Bristol BS8 1TW, United Kingdom
                                                    (Dated: July 30, 2026)
                 First-passage laws can change their number of most-probable arrival times even while their mo-
              ments and relaxation spectrum vary smoothly. We show that localized absorption controls this mor-
              phology through signed modal cancellation, and we classify the resulting transitions. For Brownian
              motion on an interval with a localized absorbing gate, release away from an antipodal gate produces
              two genuine interior maxima inside a bounded, numerically continued wedge of gate strengths whose
              fold branches close at a reflected cusp pair; source-started release instead loses its inherited finite-
              time peak across a geometry-dependent fold line bc (θ) with endpoint law bc ∼ 0.789/ min(θ, 1 − θ).
              The extrema gap and prominence obey the saddle-node powers 1/2 and 3/2, while the mean first-
              passage time and the principal decay rate remain smooth: the transition belongs to the shape of
              the law, not to the generator. For any finite absorbing continuous-time Markov chain, localized
              killing has an exact linear-fractional transform response, implying Laplace-transform ordering and
              diminishing returns; a Drazin-corrected slow–fast reduction transfers every nondegenerate reduced
              fold to the full chain. On modular graphs this yields a conductance scale: a controlled slow-bridge
              barbell has upper-fold strength linear in the bottleneck rate and inverse-linear fold time, while an
              equal-conductance family has a monotone one-exponential slow limit, so scalar conductance alone
              cannot determine the conductance-scaled limiting morphology. Interval-arithmetic enclosures locally
              certify the slow-core fold and two endpoint cusp roots; exact finite-N algebra, Monte Carlo, and
              Brownian-dynamics simulations validate the classification, and a feedback-gated colloidal protocol
              can resolve the fold with on the order of 105 trials.
