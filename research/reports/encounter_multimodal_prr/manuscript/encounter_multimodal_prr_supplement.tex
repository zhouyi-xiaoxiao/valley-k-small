\section{Model, budget, and analytical scope}
\label{sec:supp-model}

We work with the Doi volume-reaction model \cite{doi1976stochastic} after the
exact symmetry reduction appropriate to longitudinal catalyst slabs.  Fix a
finite integer physical dimension \(d\ge2\); the quotient has dimension
\(d+1\).  Its bounded and unbounded versions are
\begin{align}
 \Qbox&=I_z\times I_{\parallel}\times\mathbb T_W^{d-1},
 \label{eq:qbox}\\
 \Qinf&=\mathbb R\times\mathbb R\times\mathbb T_W^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:qinf}
\end{align}
The transverse quotient is exact in every fixed finite dimension.  Haar
invariance gives, for integrable \(h\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\mathbb T_W^{d-1}}\!\int_{\mathbb T_W^{d-1}}
 h(y_1-y_2)\,\dd y_1\dd y_2
 =W^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb T_W^{d-1}}h(r_\perp)\,\dd r_\perp.
 \label{eq:diagonal-haar-quotient}
\end{equation}
Thus no global transverse midpoint coordinate on the torus is introduced.
The variables are \(x=(z,r_\parallel,r_\perp)\), where \(z\) is the
longitudinal midpoint and \(r\) is the relative coordinate.  For two
equal-diffusivity walkers, the free forward operator is
\begin{equation}
 \Lfree q=-\nabla\!\cdot(bq)+\nabla\!\cdot(\bm D\nabla q),
 \qquad
 \bm D=\operatorname{diag}(D/2,2D,\ldots,2D),
 \label{eq:free-generator}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
 b(x)=\bigl(-\gamma(z-\bar z),-\gamma r_\parallel,0,\ldots,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
On \(\Qbox\), the nonperiodic faces carry the zero-forward-flux condition
\begin{equation}
 (bq-\bm D\nabla q)\cdot n=0,
 \label{eq:no-flux}
\end{equation}
and the transverse coordinates are periodic.  On \(\Qinf\), natural decay is
encoded through the weighted space specified below, not through an artificial
reflecting box.

Let \(0<a<W/2\) and let \(\chi_a(r)\) be the indicator of the embedded
minimum-image \(d\)-ball \(\{|r|_{\mathrm{mi}}<a\}\).  Fixed nonnegative
longitudinal profiles satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \phi_j\in L^\infty,\qquad \int\phi_j(z)\,\dd z=1.
 \label{eq:patch-normalization}
\end{equation}
The integral in Eq.~\eqref{eq:patch-normalization} is over \(I_z\) for
\(\Qbox\) and over \(\mathbb R\) for \(\Qinf\).
Define
\begin{equation}
 V_j(z,r)=W^{-(d-1)}\chi_a(r)\phi_j(z),\qquad
 V_w=\sum_{j=1}^Jw_jV_j,
 \label{eq:profiles}
\end{equation}
where \(w_j\ge0\) and \(\sum_jw_j=1\).  The killing field is
\(K_{B,w}=BV_w\).  Because the factor \(W^{-(d-1)}\) normalizes the omitted
transverse common-centre directions, the installed centre-space catalyst
amount is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \int_{\text{centre space}}\kappa_{B,w}(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:physical-budget}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:physical-budget}, rather than the integral of \(K_{B,w}\)
over all relative configurations, is the conserved resource.  Supports,
transport, contact radius, and the initial law remain fixed when \(w\) varies.
If the local killing field has unit \(T^{-1}\), then
\([B]=L^dT^{-1}\) in physical dimension \(d\).  Thus the theorem's
dimensional budgets cannot be compared across dimensions by assigning them
the same numerical value without a separately declared nondimensionalization.

The initial density is independent of \(B\), \(w\), and every control
coordinate.  Throughout Secs.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}--\ref{sec:weak-bridge} we
assume
\begin{equation}
 q_0\ge0,\qquad \langle1,q_0\rangle=1,\qquad
 q_0\in
 \begin{cases}
  L^2(\Qbox),&\text{on the bounded quotient},\\
  X_\pi,&\text{on the unbounded quotient},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:initial-state-hypotheses}
\end{equation}
where \(X_\pi\) and the normalized density \(\pi\) are defined below.  The
operator estimates remain valid for arbitrary signed or complex \(q_0\) in the
corresponding Hilbert space; nonnegativity and unit mass are imposed only for
the survival and reaction-density interpretation.

With
\begin{equation}
 A_{B,w}=\Lfree-BM_{V_w},\qquad
 q_{B,w}(t)=\e^{tA_{B,w}}q_0,
 \label{eq:killed-state}
\end{equation}
the reaction-time density and survival probability obey
\begin{align}
 f_B(t,w)&=B\langle V_w,q_{B,w}(t)\rangle,
 \label{eq:reaction-density}\\
 S_B(t,w)&=\langle1,q_{B,w}(t)\rangle,
 &-\partial_tS_B&=f_B.
 \label{eq:mass-balance}
\end{align}
The differential identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass-balance} is classical for
\(t>0\) and holds in integrated form from \(t=0\).  The pairing is the integral
of observable times density.  Occupation-time
and Feynman--Kac interpretations of this reactivity-times-occupancy identity
are standard \cite{bressloff2022feynmanKac,grebenkov2020multiple}; the results
below concern its conserved-budget mixed jets and a direct physical
multimode construction.

The normalized reversible free density is
\begin{equation}
 \pi(x)=Z_\pi^{-1}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma(z-\bar z)^2}{D}
              -\frac{\gamma r_\parallel^2}{4D}\right],
 \label{eq:pi-generic}
\end{equation}
uniform in the torus coordinates; its normalized transverse factor is
\(W^{-(d-1)}\).  On \(\Qbox\), \(\pi\) is bounded above
and away from zero and the state space is \(X=L^2(\Qbox)\).  On \(\Qinf\),
the density space and its observable dual are
\begin{equation}
 X_\pi=L^2(\pi^{-1}\dd x),\qquad
 X_\pi^*=L^2(\pi\dd x).
 \label{eq:weighted-spaces}
\end{equation}
The map \(u\mapsto q=\pi u\) is unitary from \(L^2(\pi\dd x)\) to
\(X_\pi\).  The unbounded results require \(q_0\in X_\pi\); unweighted
\(L^2\) alone is not sufficient.

All time derivatives in this supplement are restricted to a compact window
\([\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)\).  We do not claim a mixed-jet estimate at
\(t=0\), on the long scale \(t=O(B^{-1})\), or uniformly in the narrow-noise
parameter used in Sec.~\ref{sec:direct-theorem}.

\section{Exact fixed-budget sensitivity hierarchy}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

\subsection{Budget-tangent coordinates and first variation}

For normalized patches, the tangent space of the conserved simplex is
\begin{equation}
 \Tspace=\{h\in\mathbb R^J:\bm1^{\mathsf T}h=0\}.
 \label{eq:tangent-space}
\end{equation}
Fix a positive-definite control metric \(M\) and a matrix
\(P\in\mathbb R^{J\times(J-1)}\) satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \bm1^{\mathsf T}P=0,
 \qquad P^{\mathsf T}MP=I_{J-1}.
 \label{eq:frozen-basis}
\end{equation}
Near a strictly interior control \(w_*\), write
\begin{equation}
 w(\theta)=w_*+P\theta,\qquad
 U_i=\sum_{j=1}^JP_{ji}V_j.
 \label{eq:theta-control}
\end{equation}
The basis and nondimensionalization are frozen before reporting singular
values.  Changing coordinates without transforming the metric changes their
numerical values.

For a fixed-support perturbation \(H\), Duhamel's identity gives the exact
first variation
\begin{align}
 D_Vf_B(t)[H]
={}&B\langle H,\e^{tA_{B,w}}q_0\rangle\nonumber\\
&-B^2\int_0^t
 \left\langle V_w,
 \e^{(t-s)A_{B,w}}M_H\e^{sA_{B,w}}q_0
 \right\rangle\dd s.
 \label{eq:duhamel-variation}
\end{align}
This is an amplitude derivative.  It is not a shape derivative for a moving
contact set or catalyst support.

\subsection{All finite state and observable sensitivities}

For a multi-index \(\beta\in\mathbb N_0^{J-1}\), let
\(q_\beta=\partial_\theta^\beta q_{B,w(\theta)}\), with \(q_{\bm0}=q\).
Write \(e_i\) for the \(i\)th coordinate multi-index.

\begin{theorem}[Affine-control sensitivity hierarchy]
\label{thm:sensitivity-hierarchy}
For every finite multi-index \(\beta\), the state sensitivity is the unique
mild solution of
\begin{align}
 \partial_tq_{\bm0}&=A_{B,w(\theta)}q_{\bm0},
 &q_{\bm0}(0)&=q_0,
 \label{eq:hierarchy-zero}\\
 \partial_tq_\beta
 &=A_{B,w(\theta)}q_\beta
   -B\sum_{i=1}^{J-1}\beta_iM_{U_i}q_{\beta-e_i},
 &q_\beta(0)&=0\quad(|\beta|>0).
 \label{eq:hierarchy-general}
\end{align}
Terms with \(\beta_i=0\) vanish.  For every integer \(r\ge0\) and \(t>0\),
the exact observable mixed jet is
\begin{align}
 \partial_t^r\partial_\theta^\beta f_B
 =B\left[
 \langle V_{w(\theta)},\partial_t^rq_\beta\rangle
 +\sum_{i=1}^{J-1}\beta_i
  \langle U_i,\partial_t^rq_{\beta-e_i}\rangle
 \right].
 \label{eq:observable-hierarchy}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bounded multiplication operators, the Dyson--Phillips series is
norm-convergent on every compact time set and is entire in the finite affine
control amplitudes.  Hence it may be differentiated term by term.  Since
\begin{equation}
 \partial_{\theta_i}A_{B,w(\theta)}=-BM_{U_i},
 \qquad
 \partial_{\theta_i}\partial_{\theta_j}A_{B,w(\theta)}=0,
 \label{eq:affine-generator-derivatives}
\end{equation}
the multi-index product rule applied to
\(\partial_tq=A_{B,w(\theta)}q\) produces exactly the multiplicity
\(\beta_i\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hierarchy-general}.  The initial law is
control-independent, which gives the zero initial data for every positive
order.  Applying the same product rule to
\(f_B=B\langle V_{w(\theta)},q\rangle\) yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:observable-hierarchy}, because the affine observable has no
control derivatives beyond first order.  Analytic-semigroup smoothing, proved
in Sec.~\ref{sec:weak-bridge}, permits every fixed number of time derivatives
for \(t>0\).
\end{proof}

At first and second order, Theorem~\ref{thm:sensitivity-hierarchy} gives
\begin{align}
 \partial_ts_i&=As_i-BU_iq,&s_i(0)&=0,
 \label{eq:first-sensitivity}\\
 \partial_ts_{ij}&=As_{ij}-BU_is_j-BU_js_i,&s_{ij}(0)&=0,
 \label{eq:second-sensitivity}
\end{align}
where multiplication symbols have been suppressed.  Correspondingly,
\begin{align}
 \partial_{\theta_i}f_B
 &=B\{\langle U_i,q\rangle+\langle V_w,s_i\rangle\},
 \label{eq:first-observable}\\
 \partial_{\theta_i\theta_j}f_B
 &=B\{\langle U_i,s_j\rangle+\langle U_j,s_i\rangle
       +\langle V_w,s_{ij}\rangle\}.
 \label{eq:second-observable}
\end{align}
The first term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:first-observable} and the first two terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:second-observable} are direct derivatives of the reaction
observable.  A sensitivity implementation that differentiates only the state
omits these terms and computes the wrong control jet.

For any \(r\ge0\), Eqs.~\eqref{eq:first-observable}--
\eqref{eq:second-observable} remain valid after replacing every state by its
\(r\)th time derivative.  In particular, a cusp calculation has access to
\begin{equation}
 \{f_t,f_{tt},f_{ttt},f_{tttt},
 f_{t\theta_i},f_{tt\theta_i},f_{ttt\theta_i}\},
 \label{eq:cusp-jet}
\end{equation}
and second control derivatives remain available for continuation diagnostics.
The safe time-derivative identity is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t^rf_B=B\langle V_w,A_{B,w}^rq(t)\rangle.
 \label{eq:safe-time-derivative}
\end{equation}
No application of \((A_{B,w}^*)^r\) to the discontinuous contact indicator is
required or claimed.

\subsection{Projected response under one conserved cost}

For completeness, let \(Y_a\) be a finite collection of time/control
observables and let \(G_{aj}=\partial_{w_j}Y_a\).  If a general patch basis has
cost covector \(c_j=\int\phi_j\), its budget-tangent response is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde G=G-
 \frac{(GM^{-1}c)c^{\mathsf T}}{c^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}c}.
 \label{eq:projected-response}
\end{equation}
When \(\widetilde G\) has full row rank, the unique minimum-\(M\)-norm
budget-preserving infinitesimal control producing \(y\) is
\begin{equation}
 h_*=M^{-1}\widetilde G^{\mathsf T}
 (\widetilde GM^{-1}\widetilde G^{\mathsf T})^{-1}y.
 \label{eq:minimum-norm-control}
\end{equation}
This is constrained linear algebra only: it is local and supplies no global
finite-amplitude controllability statement.

\section{Compact-positive-time weak-reaction mixed jets}
\label{sec:weak-bridge}

\subsection{Analytic killed semigroups}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded and unbounded analytic realizations]
\label{prop:analytic-semigroup}
On \(\Qbox\), realize \(\Lfree\) with Eq.~\eqref{eq:no-flux} and periodic
transverse conditions on \(X=L^2(\Qbox)\).  It generates a positive analytic
semigroup.  For every real \(B\ge0\) and real simplex control \(w\),
\(A_{B,w}\) has the same domain and generates a positive, mass-decreasing
analytic semigroup for real \(t\ge0\).  For every positive time, the state has
an entire extension in the finite complex control amplitudes.

On \(\Qinf\), the same statements hold on \(X_\pi\) for
\(q_0\in X_\pi\), and the observable pairing is continuous for
\(V_w\in X_\pi^*\cap L^\infty\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(q=\pi u\).  Direct substitution using
\(b=\bm D\nabla\log\pi\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \Lfree(\pi u)=\pi\mathcal Gu,
 \qquad
 \mathcal G=\pi^{-1}\nabla\!\cdot(\pi\bm D\nabla).
 \label{eq:similarity}
\end{equation}
For each fixed finite \(d\), take the weighted \(H^1\) form domain with
periodic traces in all \(d-1\) transverse coordinates and the declared
weighted-Neumann convention on nonperiodic bounded faces.  No Sobolev
embedding or dimension-uniform estimate is used.
On the weighted space \(L^2(\pi\dd x)\),
\begin{equation}
 \langle u,\mathcal Gv\rangle_{L^2(\pi)}
 =-\int(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bm D\nabla v\,\pi\dd x.
 \label{eq:dirichlet-form}
\end{equation}
The closed weighted-Neumann form therefore realizes \(\mathcal G\) as a
nonpositive self-adjoint generator.  Its semigroup is positive and
mass-conserving for real nonnegative time, and its norm is contractive for
complex time with nonnegative real part.  On \(\Qbox\),
multiplication by \(\pi\) is a bounded isomorphism between the weighted and
unweighted \(L^2\) spaces.  On \(\Qinf\), the same map is unitary into
\(X_\pi\).  Bounded multiplication by \(-BV_w\) preserves the generator
domain and analyticity.  The Dyson--Phillips expansion proves entire
finite-dimensional complex parameter dependence.  Positivity and mass decrease
follow from the product formula, or equivalently from Feynman--Kac, only for
real nonnegative \(V_w\), real \(B\ge0\), and real \(t\ge0\).
\end{proof}

The proposition gives time regularization in powers of the generator, not
global classical spatial regularity across the sharp contact interface.
Initial boundary compatibility is unnecessary for positive-time mixed jets.

For the physical two-dimensional quotient used by the numerical program, the
unbounded part of Proposition~\ref{prop:analytic-semigroup} has the following
explicit consequence.

\begin{corollary}[Physical natural-decay realization and observable calculus]
\label{cor:physical-natural-decay}
Fix \(d=2\), \(D,\gamma,W>0\), \(B\ge0\), a real simplex control \(w\), and
the sharp bounded field \(V_w\) of Eq.~\eqref{eq:profiles}.  On

\begin{equation}
 H=L^2(\Qinf,\pi\dd x),\qquad
 \mathcal V=\overline{C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\otimes
 C^\infty(\mathbb T_W)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal V}},
 \label{eq:physical-form-domain}
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}
 \|u\|_{\mathcal V}^2=\|u\|_H^2+
 \int_{\Qinf}(\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bm D\nabla u\,\pi\dd x,
 \label{eq:physical-form-norm}
\end{equation}

the killed form is a closed symmetric nonnegative Dirichlet form with this
same domain.  Its operator \(H_{\infty,w}\ge0\) generates a symmetric analytic
sub-Markov contraction semigroup, and multiplication by \(\pi\) gives the
unitary realization

\begin{equation}
 A_{B,w}=U(-H_{\infty,w})U^{-1},\qquad Uu=\pi u,
 \label{eq:physical-unitary-realization}
\end{equation}

of the natural-decay forward generator.  Here

\begin{equation}
 Z_\pi=\frac{2\pi DW}{\gamma},\qquad
 \bm D\nabla\log\pi=b.
 \label{eq:physical-reversible-identity}
\end{equation}

For \(u_0=q_0/\pi\in H\), \(r=0,1,2\), and \(t\in[\tau,T]\),

\begin{align}
 F_w^{(r)}(t)
 &=(-1)^r\langle V_w,H_{\infty,w}^{\,r}
          \e^{-tH_{\infty,w}}u_0\rangle_H,
 \label{eq:physical-observable-calculus}\\
 \sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}|F_w^{(r)}(t)|
 &\le \|V_w\|_H C_r(\tau)\|u_0\|_H,
 \quad C_0(\tau)=1,\quad
 C_r(\tau)=\left(\frac{r}{e\tau}\right)^r\quad(r=1,2).
 \label{eq:physical-spectral-bound}
\end{align}

If Eq.~\eqref{eq:initial-state-hypotheses} holds, then \(q_{B,w}(t)\ge0\),
\(F_w(t)\ge0\), and

\begin{equation}
 \int_{\Qinf}q_{B,w}(t)\dd x
 +B\int_0^tF_w(s)\dd s=1.
 \label{eq:physical-integrated-mass}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two Gaussian integrals and the normalized torus factor give
\(Z_\pi=2\pi DW/\gamma\), while direct differentiation gives
\(\bm D\nabla\log\pi=b\).  Bounded nonnegative multiplication preserves the
closed free form, its domain, and the Dirichlet property.  The representation
theorem and the Dirichlet-form correspondence give the asserted operator and
semigroup, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-unitary-realization} follows by
integration by parts, which identifies the form-associated realization with
the displayed algebraic action on the core.  No separate
essential-self-adjointness assertion for the minimal core operator is used.
The
spectral theorem gives
\(\|H^r\e^{-tH}\|\le\sup_{\lambda\ge0}\lambda^r
\e^{-\tau\lambda}=(r/(e\tau))^r\) for \(r=1,2\), proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-spectral-bound}.  Gaussian cutoffs put the constant
function one in \(\mathcal V\).  Testing the weak evolution with that function
gives \(\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int q=-B F_w\); the sub-Markov property gives
positivity, and integration proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:physical-integrated-mass}.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:physical-natural-decay} fixes the continuum operator
realization only.  It does not prove finite-volume Mosco convergence, a
computable spatial or box-truncation error, or continuum stationary topology.

\subsection{A conditional varying-space positive-time transfer}
\label{sec:varying-space-transfer}

The next proposition isolates the abstract implication that will be available
if a model-specific finite-volume resolvent limit is proved.  It is useful
precisely because the reconstruction and projection maps need not be
isometries at finite mesh width.

\begin{proposition}[Varying-space positive-time functional bridge]
\label{prop:varying-space-functional-bridge}
Let \(L_h\ge0\) and \(L\ge0\) be self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces
\(\mathcal H_h\) and \(\mathcal H\).  Suppose that
\(J_h:\mathcal H_h\to\mathcal H\) and
\(P_h:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H_h\) satisfy
\begin{equation}
 P_h=J_h^*,\qquad
 \sup_h\bigl(\|J_h\|+\|P_h\|\bigr)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \delta_h:=\|P_hJ_h-I_{\mathcal H_h}\|\longrightarrow0,
 \label{eq:varying-map-hypotheses}
\end{equation}
and, for one \(\alpha>0\),
\begin{equation}
 J_h(L_h+\alpha)^{-1}P_hu
 \longrightarrow (L+\alpha)^{-1}u
 \quad\text{strongly for every }u\in\mathcal H.
 \label{eq:varying-resolvent-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
Then, for \(r=0,1,2\), \(0<\tau\le T<\infty\), and every
\(u_0\in\mathcal H\),
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}
 \left\|J_hL_h^r\e^{-tL_h}P_hu_0
       -L^r\e^{-tL}u_0\right\|_{\mathcal H}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:varying-positive-time-state}
\end{equation}
If \(V_h\in\mathcal H_h\), \(V\in\mathcal H\), and
\(J_hV_h\to V\) strongly, then also
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}
 \left|
 \left\langle V_h,L_h^r\e^{-tL_h}P_hu_0\right\rangle_h
 -\left\langle V,L^r\e^{-tL}u_0\right\rangle_{\mathcal H}
 \right|\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:varying-positive-time-pairing}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(R_h=(L_h+\alpha)^{-1}\), \(R=(L+\alpha)^{-1}\), and
\(\widehat R_h=J_hR_hP_h\).  Compression is not exactly multiplicative, but
for every fixed \(k\ge2\) a telescoping expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \left\|\widehat R_h^k-J_hR_h^kP_h\right\|
 \le \|J_h\|\|P_h\|(k-1)\alpha^{-k}
      (1+\delta_h)^{k-2}\delta_h.
 \label{eq:varying-resolvent-power-defect}
\end{equation}
Indeed, with \(D_h=P_hJ_h\), expand
\(R_h(D_hR_h)^{k-1}-R_h^k\) by replacing one \(D_h\) at a time.
Equation~\eqref{eq:varying-resolvent-hypothesis}, uniform boundedness, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-resolvent-power-defect} therefore give convergence for
every polynomial in \(R_h\) with zero constant term.

For \(f\in C_0([0,\infty))\), the change of variables
\(y=(\lambda+\alpha)^{-1}\) produces a continuous function on
\([0,\alpha^{-1}]\) that vanishes at \(y=0\).  Polynomials with zero constant
term are uniformly dense in that class.  The spectral theorem consequently
gives
\begin{equation}
 J_hf(L_h)P_hu\longrightarrow f(L)u
 \quad (u\in\mathcal H).
 \label{eq:varying-czero-calculus}
\end{equation}
This zero-constant approximation is why no false operator-norm assertion
\(J_hP_h\to I_{\mathcal H}\) is needed.

For \(f_{r,t}(\lambda)=\lambda^r\e^{-t\lambda}\),
\begin{equation}
 \|f_{r,t}-f_{r,s}\|_\infty
 \le
 \left(\frac{r+1}{e\tau}\right)^{r+1}|t-s|.
 \label{eq:varying-time-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
A finite time net, Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-czero-calculus} at its nodes, and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-time-lipschitz} between nodes prove
Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-positive-time-state}.

Finally, exact adjointness gives, for every \(v_h\in\mathcal H_h\),
\begin{align}
 &\langle V_h,v_h\rangle_h-\langle V,J_hv_h\rangle_{\mathcal H}
 \notag\\
 &\quad=\langle V_h,(I-P_hJ_h)v_h\rangle_h
 +\langle J_hV_h-V,J_hv_h\rangle_{\mathcal H}.
 \label{eq:varying-moving-pairing}
\end{align}
Moreover,
\(\|J_hV_h\|^2=\langle V_h,P_hJ_hV_h\rangle_h
\ge(1-\delta_h)\|V_h\|_h^2\) for small \(h\), so
\(J_hV_h\to V\) implies \(\sup_h\|V_h\|_h<\infty\).  The spectral bound
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}\|L_h^r\e^{-tL_h}P_hu_0\|_h
 \le C_r(\tau)\|P_h\|\|u_0\|,
 \qquad
 C_0=1,\quad C_r=(r/(e\tau))^r
 \label{eq:varying-discrete-spectral-bound}
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-positive-time-state} now turn
Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-moving-pairing} into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:varying-positive-time-pairing}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:varying-space-functional-bridge} remains conditional in
general.  For the twelve formula-defined fixed-box dyadic families, a separate
audited ideal composition now verifies its reconstructed-resolvent hypothesis
and hence the stated Mosco and compact-positive-time conclusions.  This closes
an ideal fixed-box C1 composition only.  It does not certify a correlated
level-\(n=0\) production member, bind the production gauge/evaluator bridge,
or provide a computable production error.  Project-level and production-level
complete C1, quantitative C2, continuum root margins, and release therefore
remain false.

\subsection{The mixed-jet estimate}

Define the budget-normalized density and exact free-exposure law by
\begin{equation}
 F_B(t,\theta)=B^{-1}f_B(t,w(\theta)),\qquad
 G(t,\theta)=\langle V_{w(\theta)},\e^{t\Lfree}q_0\rangle.
 \label{eq:F-and-G}
\end{equation}
The definition of \(F_B\) applies for \(B>0\); the theorem below defines its
continuous extension at \(B=0\).

Let \(\Theta\) be a compact real control set strictly inside the simplex.  Fix
\(\delta>0\) and use the enlarged complex tube
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_\delta^+=
 \left\{\zeta\in\mathbb C^{J-1}:
 \operatorname{dist}_\infty(\zeta,\Theta)<2\delta\right\}.
 \label{eq:enlarged-control-tube}
\end{equation}
Every closed coordinate polydisc of radius \(\delta\) about a point of
\(\Theta\) lies strictly inside \(\Theta_\delta^+\); this is the Cauchy margin
used below.  Complex tube points are analytic auxiliaries and need not be
nonnegative simplex weights.  Set
\begin{equation}
 v_{\infty,\delta}=
 \sup_{\zeta\in\Theta_\delta^+}\|V_{w(\zeta)}\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:v-infty}
\end{equation}
For the bounded problem let
\begin{equation}
 v_{*,\delta}=
 \sup_{\zeta\in\Theta_\delta^+}\|V_{w(\zeta)}\|_{L^2},
 \qquad
 \kappa_\pi\le\sqrt{\pi_{\max}/\pi_{\min}}.
 \label{eq:bounded-constants}
\end{equation}
For the unbounded problem replace the first norm by
\(\|\cdot\|_{X_\pi^*}\) and set \(\kappa_\pi=1\).  In either case write
\(\|q_0\|_X\) for the corresponding state norm.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform weak-reaction mixed-jet bridge]
\label{thm:mixed-jet}
Fix \(0<\tau\le T<\infty\), \(0\le B\le B_{\max}\), an integer \(r\ge0\),
and a finite control multi-index \(\alpha\).  Then \(F_B\) extends
continuously to \(F_0=G\), and
\begin{align}
 &\sup_{(t,\theta)\in[\tau,T]\times\Theta}
 \left|\partial_t^r\partial_\theta^\alpha(F_B-G)\right|
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\le
 r!\,\alpha!\left(\frac2\tau\right)^r
 \delta^{-|\alpha|}v_{*,\delta}\kappa_\pi
 \left[\exp\!\left(\frac32Bv_{\infty,\delta}T\right)-1\right]
 \|q_0\|_X.
 \label{eq:mixed-jet-bound}
\end{align}
Here \(\alpha!=\prod_i\alpha_i!\).  Consequently the left side is at most
\(BC_{r,\alpha}\), where the explicit \(B\)-independent constant
\begin{align}
 C_{r,\alpha}={}&
 r!\,\alpha!\left(\frac2\tau\right)^r
 \delta^{-|\alpha|}v_{*,\delta}\kappa_\pi
 \frac{3T}{2}v_{\infty,\delta}\nonumber\\
 &\times
 \exp\!\left(\frac32B_{\max}v_{\infty,\delta}T\right)
 \|q_0\|_X
 \label{eq:mixed-jet-linear-constant}
\end{align}
works for all \(0\le B\le B_{\max}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the self-adjoint representation, take complex time \(z\) with
\(\operatorname{Re}z>0\), and define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_n=\{(s_1,\ldots,s_n):1\ge s_1\ge\cdots\ge s_n\ge0\}.
 \label{eq:ordered-simplex}
\end{equation}
The norm-convergent Dyson expansion is
\begin{align}
 \e^{z(\mathcal G-BV_w)}
 ={}&\e^{z\mathcal G}
 +\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-Bz)^n
 \int_{\Delta_n}\e^{z(1-s_1)\mathcal G}V_w
 \left[\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}
 \e^{z(s_k-s_{k+1})\mathcal G}V_w\right]
 \e^{zs_n\mathcal G}\,\dd\bm s,
 \label{eq:dyson}
\end{align}
where the product is ordered with increasing \(k\) from left to right and is
the identity for \(n=1\); the ordered simplex has volume
\(1/n!\).  Every free factor is a contraction because its scalar time
increment has nonnegative real part.  The norm estimates extend to
\(\operatorname{Re}z=0\) by strong continuity, although the Cauchy disks below
remain strictly inside \(\operatorname{Re}z>0\).  Multiplication by \(V_w\)
commutes with multiplication by
\(\pi\), so the similarity factor enters once rather than at every Dyson
factor.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \|\e^{zA_{B,w}}-\e^{z\Lfree}\|_{X\to X}
 \le\kappa_\pi\{\e^{B|z|\|V_w\|_\infty}-1\}.
 \label{eq:dyson-operator-bound}
\end{equation}
The complex-bilinear observable remainder
\begin{equation}
 R_B(z,\zeta)=
 \langle V_{w(\zeta)},
 [\e^{zA_{B,w(\zeta)}}-\e^{z\Lfree}]q_0\rangle
 \label{eq:analytic-remainder}
\end{equation}
therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |R_B(z,\zeta)|
 \le v_{*,\delta}\kappa_\pi
 \{\e^{Bv_{\infty,\delta}|z|}-1\}\|q_0\|_X.
 \label{eq:analytic-remainder-bound}
\end{equation}
For \(t\in[\tau,T]\), the disk \(|z-t|\le\tau/2\) stays in the open right
half-plane and obeys \(|z|\le3T/2\).  Cauchy's estimate on that disk and on
each closed radius-\(\delta\) control polydisc contained in
\(\Theta_\delta^+\) gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed-jet-bound}.  Finally,
\(\e^{Bx}-1\le Bx\e^{B_{\max}x}\) gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixed-jet-linear-constant}.
\end{proof}

Because \(G\) is affine in \(\theta\), Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} also gives
\begin{equation}
 D_\theta F_B=D_\theta G+O(B),
 \qquad D_\theta^kF_B=O(B)\quad(k\ge2),
 \label{eq:control-order-check}
\end{equation}
through every fixed number of time derivatives.  This is an independent
consistency check on the hierarchy in Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.

\subsection{First Dyson correction}

For real time define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_w(t)=\int_0^t
 \langle V_w,\e^{(t-s)\Lfree}M_{V_w}
 \e^{s\Lfree}q_0\rangle\,\dd s.
 \label{eq:first-dyson-correction}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 F_B=G-B\mathcal H_w+\mathcal R_{2,B}.
 \label{eq:first-correction-expansion}
\end{equation}
Defining \(\mathcal R_{2,B}\) by the \(n\ge2\) part of the complex Dyson
series and applying the same Cauchy argument gives
\begin{align}
 &\sup_{[\tau,T]\times\Theta}
 |\partial_t^r\partial_\theta^\alpha\mathcal R_{2,B}|
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\le
 \frac{B^2}{2}r!\alpha!\left(\frac2\tau\right)^r
 \delta^{-|\alpha|}v_{*,\delta}\kappa_\pi
 \left(\frac32v_{\infty,\delta}T\right)^2
 \e^{(3/2)B_{\max}v_{\infty,\delta}T}\|q_0\|_X.
 \label{eq:second-order-remainder}
\end{align}
Thus the first correction has a genuine \(O(B^2)\) remainder through every
fixed mixed jet; a real-axis remainder bound alone would not justify the
control derivatives in Eq.~\eqref{eq:second-order-remainder}.

\subsection{Quantitative local persistence, and what it does not imply}

The mixed-jet estimate transfers strict finite collections of inequalities.
For example, suppose on a closed interval \([a_-,a_+]\subset(\tau,T)\)
\begin{equation}
 G'(a_-)>\eta_1,\qquad G'(a_+)<-\eta_1,\qquad
 \sup_{[a_-,a_+]}G''<-\eta_2
 \label{eq:mode-margins}
\end{equation}
for \(\eta_1,\eta_2>0\).  If the \(C^2\) mixed-jet error is smaller than
\(\min(\eta_1,\eta_2)\), then \(F_B\) has exactly one critical point in the
interval and it is a nondegenerate maximum.

For folds and cusps, first freeze a square finite-dimensional unfolding.  Let
\(H_B:\mathcal U\subset\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n\) be the corresponding
\(C^1\) map, let \(H_0(x_0)=0\), put \(A_0=DH_0(x_0)\), and assume \(A_0\)
is invertible.  Suppose the closed ball \(\overline{B_r(x_0)}\) remains inside
positive time and, after the control slice is mapped back to \(\theta\), the
simplex interior.  If
\begin{align}
 \sup_{x\in\overline{B_r(x_0)}}
 \|I-A_0^{-1}DH_B(x)\|_2&\le\frac12,
 \label{eq:contraction-derivative}\\
 \|A_0^{-1}H_B(x_0)\|_2&\le\frac r2,
 \label{eq:contraction-residual}
\end{align}
then \(x\mapsto x-A_0^{-1}H_B(x)\) is a contraction of the ball into itself.
It has one zero \(x_B\) in that ball and
\begin{equation}
 \|x_B-x_0\|_2\le
 2\|A_0^{-1}H_B(x_0)\|_2.
 \label{eq:root-displacement}
\end{equation}
For a fold, freeze one unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^{J-1}\) in the dimensionless
\(\theta\)-metric, set \(\theta=\bar\theta+e\lambda\), and use the square map
\begin{equation}
 H_B^{\mathrm{fold}}(t,\lambda)=
 \bigl((F_B)_t,(F_B)_{tt}\bigr)
 (t,\bar\theta+e\lambda)\in\mathbb R^2.
 \label{eq:fold-slice}
\end{equation}
For a cusp, freeze an isometric two-plane
\begin{equation}
 E\in\mathbb R^{(J-1)\times2},\qquad E^{\mathsf T}E=I_2,
 \qquad \theta=\bar\theta+E\xi,
 \label{eq:cusp-plane}
\end{equation}
in the same metric, and use
\begin{equation}
 H_B^{\mathrm{cusp}}(t,\xi)=
 \bigl((F_B)_t,(F_B)_{tt},(F_B)_{ttt}\bigr)
 (t,\bar\theta+E\xi)\in\mathbb R^3.
 \label{eq:cusp-slice}
\end{equation}
The vectors \(e\) and the columns of \(E\), as well as the
nondimensionalization, are frozen before \(B\) is varied.

Define the full normalized response and the response restricted to that same
cusp plane by
\begin{equation}
 R_B(x)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \nabla_\theta(F_B)_t(x)\\
  \nabla_\theta(F_B)_{tt}(x)
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad R_B^E(x)=R_B(x)E\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}.
 \label{eq:rank-response}
\end{equation}
where \(x=(t,\xi)\) means evaluation at
\((t,\bar\theta+E\xi)\).
At a cusp root, with columns ordered as \((t,\xi_1,\xi_2)\),
\begin{equation}
 \det DH_B^{\mathrm{cusp}}
 =(F_B)_{tttt}\det R_B^E.
 \label{eq:cusp-jacobian-factorization}
\end{equation}
Thus the quartic time derivative must be nonzero and the restricted two-control
response must be nonsingular.  If on the same cusp ball
\begin{equation}
 s_*:=\inf_x\sigma_2(R_0^E(x))>0,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_R(B):=\sup_x\|R_B^E(x)-R_0^E(x)\|_2<s_*,
 \label{eq:weyl-hypotheses}
\end{equation}
then Weyl's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \inf_x\sigma_2(R_B^E(x))\ge s_*-\varepsilon_R(B)>0,
 \label{eq:weyl-conclusion}
\end{equation}
including at the displaced root.  This rank-two condition applies only when
two independent budget tangents exist.  The raw response for \(f_B\) is
\(BR_B^E\), so normalized rank may persist while both its absolute singular
value and event rate vanish as \(B\downarrow0\).

Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet} is local in positive time.  It does not exclude
late or remote extra extrema, provide a finite event-mass floor, prove that a
specified finite \(B\) lies in its asymptotic regime, or certify a numerical
discretization.  Every application requires explicit margins such as
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mode-margins}, \eqref{eq:contraction-derivative}, and
\eqref{eq:weyl-hypotheses}.

\section{Constructive fixed-finite-mode theorem in every fixed finite
\texorpdfstring{$d\ge2$}{d >= 2}}
\label{sec:direct-theorem}

\subsection{Narrow-noise OU slab family and initial law}

Fix once and for all
\begin{equation}
 d\in\mathbb N,\ d\ge2,\quad
 D_0,\gamma,W,\rho,s_0,u_0>0,\quad
 0<a<W/2,\quad z_0\ne\bar z.
 \label{eq:fixed-direct-data}
\end{equation}
For \(\epsilon>0\), let the longitudinal midpoint solve
\begin{equation}
 \dd Z_t=-\gamma(Z_t-\bar z)\,\dd t
 +\epsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd W_t,
 \qquad Z_0\sim N(z_0,\epsilon^2s_0^2).
 \label{eq:midpoint-sde}
\end{equation}
For each fixed \(\epsilon\), this is the generic model of
Sec.~\ref{sec:supp-model} with the explicit identification
\(D=\epsilon^2D_0\); the same identification gives the relative-coordinate
noise amplitudes below.
Its mean and variance are
\begin{align}
 \mu(t)&=\bar z+(z_0-\bar z)\e^{-\gamma t},
 \label{eq:midpoint-mean}\\
 \operatorname{Var}Z_t&=\epsilon^2s^2(t),
 &s^2(t)&=s_0^2\e^{-2\gamma t}
 +\frac{D_0}{2\gamma}(1-\e^{-2\gamma t}).
 \label{eq:midpoint-variance}
\end{align}
The relative quotient follows
\begin{align}
 \dd R_{\parallel,t}
 &=-\gamma R_{\parallel,t}\,\dd t
 +2\epsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd W_{\parallel,t},
 \label{eq:relative-longitudinal}\\
 \dd R_{\perp,t}
 &=2\epsilon\sqrt{D_0}\,\dd\bm W_{\perp,t}\pmod W.
 \label{eq:relative-transverse}
\end{align}
The complete initial law is the mutually independent product
\begin{align}
 R_{\parallel,0}&\sim
 N(r_{\parallel,0},\epsilon^2u_0^2),
 \label{eq:initial-rparallel}\\
 R_{\perp,0}&\sim
 \operatorname{WN}(r_{\perp,0},
 \epsilon^2\Sigma_{\perp,0}),
 \qquad \Sigma_{\perp,0}\succ0,
 \label{eq:initial-rperp}
\end{align}
and all initial variables and Brownian drivers are mutually independent.  Here
\(r_{\perp,0}\in\mathbb T_W^{d-1}\) and
\(\Sigma_{\perp,0}\in\mathbb R^{(d-1)\times(d-1)}\) is positive definite.

For each fixed \(\epsilon>0\), the invariant density \(\pi_\epsilon\) is
Gaussian with mean \(\bar z\) and variance
\(\epsilon^2D_0/(2\gamma)\) in \(Z\), Gaussian with mean zero and variance
\(2\epsilon^2D_0/\gamma\) in \(R_\parallel\), and uniform on the transverse
torus.  Direct Gaussian exponent comparison gives
\begin{equation}
 q_0\in X_{\pi_\epsilon}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 s_0^2<\frac{D_0}{\gamma}
 \ \text{and}\ 
 u_0^2<\frac{4D_0}{\gamma}
 \label{eq:weighted-initial-thresholds}
\end{equation}
for the two unbounded Gaussian factors; the wrapped factor is square
integrable against the uniform torus measure.  We impose both strict
inequalities.  They hold pointwise in \(\epsilon\), but the weighted norm is
not asserted to remain bounded as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\).

For fixed \(\rho>0\), define the normalized slabs
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{j,\epsilon}(z)=
 \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\epsilon\rho}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(z-c_j)^2}{2\epsilon^2\rho^2}\right]
 \label{eq:narrow-slabs}
\end{equation}
and the killing field
\begin{equation}
 K_{B,w,\epsilon}(Z,R)=
 \frac{B}{W^{d-1}}\chi_a(R)
 \sum_{j=1}^mw_j\phi_{j,\epsilon}(Z),
 \qquad w_j\ge0,\quad\sum_jw_j=1.
 \label{eq:direct-killing}
\end{equation}
Each slab has unit full centre-space integral, so the installed amount remains
\(B\).  The catalyst is a longitudinal slab, not an arbitrary localized
two- or three-dimensional patch.

\subsection{Target times and the contact-tail lemma}

Fix \(0<\tau<T<\infty\) and distinct target times
\begin{equation}
 \tau<t_1<\cdots<t_m<T.
 \label{eq:target-times}
\end{equation}
Because \(z_0\ne\bar z\), \(\mu\) is strictly monotone on this window.
Choose pairwise disjoint closed target neighborhoods whose union is \(I_*\),
with \(I_*\subset(\tau,T)\).  The deterministic relative path is
\begin{equation}
 r_*(t)=\bigl(r_{\parallel,0}\e^{-\gamma t},r_{\perp,0}\bigr).
 \label{eq:deterministic-relative-path}
\end{equation}
Assume a fixed contact-interior margin
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I_*}|r_*(t)|_{\mathrm{mi}}\le a-\eta
 \quad\text{for some }\eta>0.
 \label{eq:contact-interior}
\end{equation}
Finally set \(c_j=\mu(t_j)\).

Let
\(c_{d,\epsilon}(t)=\Pr\{|R_t|_{\mathrm{mi}}<a\}\).
The relative covariance is \(\epsilon^2\Sigma_R(t)\), where its longitudinal
coefficient and its unwrapped transverse coefficient are
\begin{align}
 u_0^2\e^{-2\gamma t}
 +\frac{2D_0}{\gamma}(1-\e^{-2\gamma t}),
 \label{eq:relative-long-variance}\\
 \Sigma_{\perp,0}+4D_0tI_{d-1},
 \label{eq:relative-transverse-variance}
\end{align}
respectively.

\begin{lemma}[Differentiated contact tail]
\label{lem:contact-tail}
For every fixed integer \(r\ge0\), there exist finite constants
\(C_{r,d},N_{r,d},q_d>0\), independent of all sufficiently small
\(\epsilon\), such that
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I_*}
 \left|\partial_t^r\{c_{d,\epsilon}(t)-1\}\right|
 \le C_{r,d}\epsilon^{-N_{r,d}}\e^{-q_d/\epsilon^2}.
 \label{eq:contact-tail}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(C_a=\{R:|R|_{\mathrm{mi}}<a\}\), and use the product geodesic distance
on \(\mathbb R\times\mathbb T_W^{d-1}\).  The reverse triangle inequality and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contact-interior} give the chart-independent bound
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{t\in I_*}\inf_{R\in C_a^c}
 d_{\mathrm{cyl}}(R,r_*(t))
 \ge a-\sup_{t\in I_*}|r_*(t)|_{\mathrm{mi}}\ge\eta.
 \label{eq:contact-geodesic-separation}
\end{equation}
The condition \(a<W/2\) keeps the contact boundary away from the torus cut
locus.  Uniform positive definiteness and boundedness of the covariance and
its fixed time derivatives give constants
\begin{equation}
 0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_+<\infty,
 \qquad
 \lambda_-I\preceq\Sigma_R(t)\preceq\lambda_+I,
 \qquad t\in I_*.
 \label{eq:relative-covariance-bounds}
\end{equation}

Let \(p_{\epsilon,t}\) be the normalized relative density and write its wrapped
transverse part as a lattice-image sum.  If \(Y_n(R,t)\) is the Euclidean
displacement from the mean in image \(n\in\mathbb Z^{d-1}\), differentiation
of one Gaussian image gives, for fixed \(r\), integers \(M_r,N_r\), and
constants independent of small \(\epsilon\),
\begin{equation}
 \left|\partial_t^rp_{\epsilon,t}^{(n)}(R)\right|
 \le C_r\epsilon^{-d-N_r}
 \left[1+\left(\frac{|Y_n(R,t)|}{\epsilon}\right)^{M_r}\right]
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{|Y_n(R,t)|^2}{C_r\epsilon^2}\right].
 \label{eq:differentiated-image-majorant}
\end{equation}
Uniform Gaussian summability on the compact positive-time set justifies
termwise differentiation and summation.  Equation~\eqref{eq:contact-geodesic-separation}
means that every Euclidean lift, including whichever image is nearest a
fundamental-cell face, has \(|Y_n|\ge\eta\) on \(C_a^c\).  More explicitly,
partition \(\mathbb Z^{d-1}\) into max-norm shells.  The number of indices in
shell \(k\) is at most \((2k+3)^{d-1}-(2k+1)^{d-1}\), whereas periodicity and
the fixed fundamental cell give \(|Y_n|\ge c_d k-C_d\).  Thus the polynomial
factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:differentiated-image-majorant} is dominated by a
summable fixed-dimensional Gaussian shell majorant, uniformly for small
\(\epsilon\).

Finally, normalization supplies the step needed to estimate derivatives by a
tail integral:
\begin{equation}
 \int\partial_t^rp_{\epsilon,t}(R)\,\dd R=0\quad(r\ge1),
 \qquad
 \partial_t^r(c_{d,\epsilon}-1)
 =-\int_{C_a^c}\partial_t^rp_{\epsilon,t}(R)\,\dd R
 \quad(r\ge0).
 \label{eq:contact-tail-normalization}
\end{equation}
For \(r=0\), the first equality is replaced by
\(c_{d,\epsilon}-1=-\int_{C_a^c}p_{\epsilon,t}\).
Integrating Eq.~\eqref{eq:differentiated-image-majorant} on this uniformly
separated set and summing the images gives a polynomial prefactor times
\(\exp(-q/\epsilon^2)\), proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:contact-tail}.
\end{proof}

In particular, \(c_{d,\epsilon}\to1\) in \(C^2(I_*)\).  The proof uses the
contact-interior hypothesis essentially; arbitrary approach or separation
paths are not covered.

\subsection{Exact free clocks and channel separation}

Independence of midpoint and relative motion gives the exact free-exposure
channel
\begin{align}
 g_{j,\epsilon}(t)
 &=\frac{c_{d,\epsilon}(t)}
 {W^{d-1}\sqrt{2\pi}\epsilon S(t)}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(c_j-\mu(t))^2}{2\epsilon^2S^2(t)}\right],
 \label{eq:exact-free-clock}\\
 S^2(t)&=s^2(t)+\rho^2,
 \label{eq:convolved-variance}
\end{align}
and
\begin{equation}
 G_\epsilon(t;w)=\sum_{j=1}^mw_jg_{j,\epsilon}(t).
 \label{eq:direct-free-mixture}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Own-channel local Gaussian]
\label{lem:own-channel}
For fixed \(L<\infty\), uniformly on \(|y|\le L\) and for \(r=0,1,2\),
\begin{align}
 \epsilon^{r+1}\partial_t^rg_{j,\epsilon}(t_j+\epsilon y)
 &\longrightarrow \partial_y^rA_j(y),
 \label{eq:own-channel-convergence}\\
 A_j(y)&=
 \frac{1}{W^{d-1}\sqrt{2\pi}S(t_j)}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\mu'(t_j)^2y^2}{2S^2(t_j)}\right].
 \label{eq:own-channel-limit}
\end{align}
There is one common \(L_0>0\), independent of small \(\epsilon\), such that
on
\begin{equation}
 I_{j,\epsilon}=
 [t_j-L_0\epsilon,t_j+L_0\epsilon]
 \label{eq:local-intervals}
\end{equation}
the own-channel endpoint slopes have signs \(+,-\) and magnitude
\(O(\epsilon^{-2})\), while the curvature is strictly negative with magnitude
\(O(\epsilon^{-3})\).  For all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), these
intervals are pairwise disjoint and each is contained in its declared
component of \(I_*\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Taylor expansion on a fixed \(y\)-interval gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{c_j-\mu(t_j+\epsilon y)}{\epsilon}
 =-\mu'(t_j)y+O(\epsilon)
 \label{eq:mean-taylor}
\end{equation}
in \(C^2\), and \(S(t_j+\epsilon y)\to S(t_j)>0\) in the same topology.
Smooth composition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-free-clock}, followed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-tail}, proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:own-channel-convergence}.  Since there are only finitely many
channels and
\begin{equation}
 A_j''(y)=\frac{\mu'(t_j)^2}{S(t_j)^2}
 \left(\frac{\mu'(t_j)^2y^2}{S(t_j)^2}-1\right)A_j(y),
 \label{eq:limiting-gaussian-curvature}
\end{equation}
choose
\(0<L_0<\min_j S(t_j)/|\mu'(t_j)|\), decreasing it if needed to fit the
declared target neighborhoods.  Every limiting Gaussian then has positive
left slope, negative right slope, and strictly negative curvature.  Uniform
\(C^2\) convergence then gives constants \(\alpha_j,\kappa_j>0\) such that
\begin{align}
 \partial_tg_{j,\epsilon}(t_j-L_0\epsilon)
 &\ge\alpha_j\epsilon^{-2},\nonumber\\
 \partial_tg_{j,\epsilon}(t_j+L_0\epsilon)
 &\le-\alpha_j\epsilon^{-2},\nonumber\\
 \sup_{I_{j,\epsilon}}\partial_t^2g_{j,\epsilon}
 &\le-\kappa_j\epsilon^{-3}.
 \label{eq:own-channel-margins}
\end{align}
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cross-channel exponential bound]
\label{lem:cross-channel}
For \(i\ne j\), all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\), and \(r=0,1,2\),
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in I_{j,\epsilon}}
 |\partial_t^rg_{i,\epsilon}(t)|
 \le C_{ijr}\epsilon^{-M_r}\e^{-q_{ij}/\epsilon^2}
 \label{eq:cross-channel-bound}
\end{equation}
for constants independent of \(\epsilon\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Strict monotonicity gives \(c_i\ne c_j\).  On the shrinking \(j\)th interval,
\(|c_i-\mu(t)|\ge|c_i-c_j|/2\) for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon\).
Every fixed time derivative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-free-clock} adds only a
polynomial power of \(\epsilon^{-1}\); the separated Gaussian exponent remains
\(-q_{ij}/\epsilon^2\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-tail} controls the common
contact factor and its derivatives, proving Eq.~\eqref{eq:cross-channel-bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The theorem and its quantifier order}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \Wset\subset\left\{w\in\mathbb R^m:
 w_j\ge w_{\min}>0,\ \sum_jw_j=1\right\}
 \label{eq:compact-weight-set}
\end{equation}
be compact and nonempty, so necessarily \(w_{\min}\le1/m\).

\begin{theorem}[At least \(m\) modes in every fixed finite dimension]
\label{thm:fixed-finite-m}
Fix integers \(d\ge2\) and \(m\ge1\), all dimension-sized data in
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fixed-direct-data}, \eqref{eq:initial-rparallel}, and
\eqref{eq:initial-rperp}, the strict weighted-space conditions
\eqref{eq:weighted-initial-thresholds}, target times
\eqref{eq:target-times}, the contact-interior condition
\eqref{eq:contact-interior}, and the compact interior weight set \(\Wset\).
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \exists\epsilon_0(d,m,\text{data},\Wset)>0\
 \ \forall\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0)\ 
 \exists B_0(d,m,\text{data},\Wset,\epsilon)>0\
 \ \forall B\in(0,B_0)\ 
 \forall w\in\Wset:\ \mathsf P_m(B,\epsilon,w),}
 \label{eq:nested-quantifiers}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathsf P_m\) means that the exact continuum Doi density has exactly
one nondegenerate local maximum in each of the named disjoint intervals
\(I_{j,\epsilon}\), and hence at least \(m\) local maxima.  It also has at
least one local minimum between every consecutive pair of certified intervals.
No exact global root count is asserted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(j\).  The own-channel contribution to the endpoint slopes is at least
\(w_{\min}\alpha_j\epsilon^{-2}\), and its negative-curvature margin is at
least \(w_{\min}\kappa_j\epsilon^{-3}\).  Because \(m\) is fixed and
\(0\le w_i\le1\), Lemma~\ref{lem:cross-channel} implies that the sum of all
cross-channel first and second derivatives is smaller than half of every
own-channel margin once \(\epsilon\) is sufficiently small.  The same
\(\epsilon_0\) works for all finitely many intervals and all \(w\in\Wset\).
Thus
\begin{align}
 G_\epsilon'(t_j-L_0\epsilon;w)&>0,\nonumber\\
 G_\epsilon'(t_j+L_0\epsilon;w)&<0,\nonumber\\
 G_\epsilon''(t;w)&<0\quad(t\in I_{j,\epsilon}).
 \label{eq:mixture-margins}
\end{align}
The derivative is strictly decreasing and changes sign exactly once, so the
interval contains one unique nondegenerate maximum.  On the gap between two
consecutive intervals, the derivative is negative at the left endpoint and
positive at the right endpoint.  A minimum of the continuous function on the
closed gap cannot occur at either endpoint, hence at least one local minimum
is interior.  It need not be nondegenerate.

To connect this weight set to Sec.~\ref{sec:weak-bridge}, choose
\(\bar w\in\Wset\) and a frozen matrix
\(P\in\mathbb R^{m\times(m-1)}\) whose columns span
\(\{h:\bm1^{\mathsf T}h=0\}\).  Every \(w\in\Wset\) has a unique coordinate
\(w=\bar w+P\theta\); hence
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_{\Wset}=\{\theta:\bar w+P\theta\in\Wset\}
 \label{eq:weight-to-control-set}
\end{equation}
is compact and lies strictly inside the real simplex chart.  Choose the
enlarged complex tube of Eq.~\eqref{eq:enlarged-control-tube} around this set.
Its complex points need not be probability weights: they are used only for the
entire affine-control extension and Cauchy estimates.

Now fix one \(\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0)\).  Every Gaussian slab is then
bounded, the initial law belongs to \(X_{\pi_\epsilon}\), and all tube norms
are finite.  The unbounded version of Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-jet}, with
constants allowed to depend on this fixed \(\epsilon\), gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{f_{B,\epsilon}}{B}\longrightarrow G_\epsilon
 \quad\text{in }C^2([\tau,T]),
 \label{eq:positive-B-transfer}
\end{equation}
uniformly over \(\Wset\) as \(B\downarrow0\).  The finitely many strict
inequalities in Eq.~\eqref{eq:mixture-margins} therefore persist for all
\(0<B<B_0(\epsilon)\), for one \(B_0(\epsilon)>0\) uniform over \(\Wset\).
The same sign and concavity argument proves \(\mathsf P_m\).
\end{proof}

The order in Eq.~\eqref{eq:nested-quantifiers} is essential.  The semigroup
constants can deteriorate rapidly as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), because the
profiles grow like \(\epsilon^{-1}\) and the weighted initial norm need not be
uniform.  There is no interchange of the \(\epsilon\) and \(B\) limits.

\begin{corollary}[Leading peak balance and event-mass boundary]
\label{cor:peak-balance}
Let
\begin{equation}
 H_j=\frac{1}{W^{d-1}\sqrt{2\pi}S(t_j)},
 \qquad
 w_j^{\mathrm{bal}}=\frac{S(t_j)}{\sum_iS(t_i)}.
 \label{eq:peak-balancing-weights}
\end{equation}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-finite-m} with the compact singleton
\(\Wset=\{w^{\mathrm{bal}}\}\).  Then the leading certified free-exposure peak
heights are equal.  Denote the unique free-exposure maximum in
\(I_{j,\epsilon}\) by \(t^{G,*}_{j,\epsilon}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{t^{G,*}_{j,\epsilon}-t_j}{\epsilon}\to0,
 \qquad
 \epsilon G_\epsilon(t^{G,*}_{j,\epsilon};w^{\mathrm{bal}})
 \to w_j^{\mathrm{bal}}H_j.
 \label{eq:balanced-peaks}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \int_{I_{j,\epsilon}}G_\epsilon(t;w^{\mathrm{bal}})\,\dd t
 \longrightarrow w_j^{\mathrm{bal}}
 \int_{-L_0}^{L_0}A_j(y)\,\dd y>0,
 \label{eq:free-exposure-area}
\end{equation}
whereas, after fixing \(\epsilon\), the physical Doi event mass satisfies, as
\(B\downarrow0\),
\begin{equation}
 \int_{I_{j,\epsilon}}f_{B,\epsilon}
 (t;w^{\mathrm{bal}})\,\dd t
 =B\int_{I_{j,\epsilon}}G_\epsilon
 (t;w^{\mathrm{bal}})\,\dd t+O_\epsilon(B^2).
 \label{eq:event-mass-order}
\end{equation}
The positive-\(B\) Doi maximum may separately be denoted
\(t^{B,*}_{j,\epsilon}\); Eq.~\eqref{eq:balanced-peaks} makes no assertion that
interchanges or couples the \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) and \(B\downarrow0\)
limits.  Thus the theorem supplies no positive absolute event-mass floor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:own-channel}, strict local concavity, and exponentially small
cross channels locate the free-exposure maximum at \(y=o(1)\) and give
Eq.~\eqref{eq:balanced-peaks}.  Changing variables
\(t=t_j+\epsilon y\) proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:free-exposure-area}.
The first-order weak-reaction expansion in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:first-correction-expansion}, integrated over the interval after
\(\epsilon\) has been fixed, gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:event-mass-order}; its
remainder constant is allowed to depend on that fixed \(\epsilon\).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed-finite-m} is pointwise in the fixed finite integers
\(d\) and \(m\), and is an existence result for a \(d\)-, \(m\)-, and
\(\epsilon\)-dependent slab family.  No constant, dimensional budget,
amplitude, event mass, or value of \(B_0\) is uniform or compared across
dimensions.  It does not show that
one fixed geometry realizes arbitrary \(m\), exclude extra early, late, or
interstitial extrema, certify nondegenerate separator minima, cover arbitrary
localized patches, take a \(d\to\infty\) limit, or demonstrate
that nontrivial approach/separation dynamics causes the peaks.  The latter
limitation is visible in Lemma~\ref{lem:contact-tail}: the construction makes
the contact factor tend to one near every designed clock.

The preceding theorem is retained because it allows a time-varying midpoint
variance and local contact assumptions.  It does not support the exact-mode
headline.  The stricter stationary-midpoint, whole-window subfamily needed for
that headline is proved next.
