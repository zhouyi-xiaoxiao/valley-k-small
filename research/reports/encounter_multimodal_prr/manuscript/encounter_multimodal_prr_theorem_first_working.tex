\author{Xiaoxiao Zhouyi}

\date{July 15, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Internal working draft---not a submission artifact.}
Can a fixed amount of spatial reactivity be arranged to prescribe the
finite-window topology of an encounter-reaction-time density?  We answer this
existence question for a Doi encounter process \cite{doi1976stochastic}.
For every fixed finite dimension $d\ge2$, prescribed fixed finite integer
$m$, and compact family of interior catalyst weights $w_j\ge w_*>0$, an
$m$-dependent support design of ordered narrow slabs along a monotone exposure
trajectory generates exactly $m$ nondegenerate maxima and $m-1$ nondegenerate
minima on a declared compact positive-time window.  The construction first
fixes a sufficiently small noise-and-width scale and only then takes a
sufficiently small positive installed reactivity budget.  Exhaustiveness
follows from a global zero bound for the separated Gaussian clocks and a
whole-window complement certificate; compact-positive-time mixed-jet
convergence transfers the strict topology to the killed process.  The result
is existential and deliberately nonuniform: its geometry and admissible
scales may depend on $d$ and $m$, and its small-noise contact factor is
asymptotically saturated on the design window.  It gives no useful budget
threshold, event-mass floor, or topology outside that window.  A practical
two-dimensional allocation-control realization is therefore a separate
numerical question.  We state a reproducible validation design based on fixed
same-budget controls, interval-certified finite-volume propagation, and an
independently powered off-lattice event-law comparison, without promoting
calculations that do not yet meet that standard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

First-passage and reaction-time densities can be multimodal when distinct
trajectory families reach a reactive set on separated time scales
\cite{godecMetzler2017twochannel,grebenkovMetzlerOshanin2018defocusing}.
Continuum trap placement can create multiple capture-time peaks
\cite{lindsaySpoonmoreTzou2016narrow}, while two-particle encounter laws in
physical dimensions two and three and exact lattice models exhibit related
multirate mechanisms
\cite{leVotEtAl2022encounter,giuggioli2020confined,
giuggioliEtAl2024multitarget}.  Existing theory also
describes spatially heterogeneous absorption, fixed-total-reactivity flux
optimization, and encounter-dependent reactions
\cite{nguyenGrebenkov2010porous,grebenkov2019heterogeneous,
nicolaouMulder2023flux,grebenkov2020paradigm}.  The question addressed here is
narrower: can the finite-window \emph{timing topology} of a two-particle
reaction be prescribed by support design while the installed amount of a
static reactivity resource remains fixed?

The fact that a univariate mixture of $m$ Gaussian components has at most
$m$ modes is classical in scale-space and Gaussian-mixture theory
\cite{carreiraPerpinanWilliams2003modes,amendolaEngstromHaase2020modes}.
We do not claim that upper bound as new.  What is needed here is a
margin-bearing construction inside a killed two-particle process: all
$2m-1$ stationary points are made simple and located, the whole declared
time-window complement is controlled after multiplication by the encounter
factor, and the resulting signature is transferred to positive Doi budget.

For each prescribed fixed finite $m$, the construction chooses an
$m$-dependent family of catalyst supports and keeps its transport, contact
geometry, initial law, and total installed centre-space reactivity fixed over
all admissible allocations.  Every interior allocation in that constructed
family has the same alternating signature of $m$ maxima and $m-1$ minima.  This
is a support-geometry construction under a conserved budget, not a proof that
one fixed support family selects different mode counts by reallocation.  A
weak-reactivity mixed-jet estimate transfers the strict compact-window topology
from free exposure to the positive-budget Doi law.  These are existence
results, not certificates for a chosen finite budget or discretization.

The finite-state and finite-grid predecessor developed Green-function
reduction, fixed-budget response identities, finite folds, and reduced-clock
screening.  Those ingredients are treated here as foundation rather than new
headlines; a public identifier and detailed overlap statement must be added
before submission.  Recent universal first-passage response theory concerns
the mean first-passage time under rare perturbation activation
\cite{keidarReuveni2026linear}; the mixed jet below is instead a
model-specific statement for the full transient reaction-time density under a
static conserved spatial allocation.

A physical two-dimensional control claim requires a different evidence chain:
unchanged one-, two-, and three-mode allocation vectors, complete root
exclusion on one common window, mesh/parity/alignment/box convergence, and an
independent event-law calculation using the same basin cuts and time windows.
The final section records that validation design and the claims it can and
cannot support.

\section{Conserved-reactivity encounter model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Budget and killed law}

Let $q(x,t)$ be the unreacted configuration density of two mobile particles,
with conservative generator $\mathcal L$.  Write $r$ for their relative
coordinate and $c$ for a declared encounter-centre coordinate.  For a fixed
contact profile $\chi_a(r)$ and normalized nonnegative centre-space profiles
$\phi_j(c)$, define

\begin{align}
 \kappa_{\bm w}(c)&=B\sum_{j=1}^{J}w_j\phi_j(c),
 &w_j&\ge0,\nonumber\\
 \sum_jw_j&=1,
 &\int\phi_j(c)\,\dd c&=1,\nonumber\\
 K_{B,\bm w}(x)&=\chi_a(r)\kappa_{\bm w}(c).
 \label{eq:budget-field}
\end{align}

Every admissible allocation has the same installed centre-space resource,

\begin{equation}
 \int\kappa_{\bm w}(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:conserved-budget}
\end{equation}

This is neither an integral over the full two-particle configuration space nor
a count of reactive grid cells.  The killed evolution and reaction-time
density are

\begin{align}
 \partial_tq_{B,\bm w}&=(\mathcal L-K_{B,\bm w})q_{B,\bm w},
 &q_{B,\bm w}(0)&=q_0,\nonumber\\
 f_{B,\bm w}(t)&=\langle K_{B,\bm w},q_{B,\bm w}(t)\rangle,
 &-S'_{B,\bm w}(t)&=f_{B,\bm w}(t),
 \label{eq:killed-law}
\end{align}

where $S_{B,\bm w}(t)=\int q_{B,\bm w}(x,t)\,\dd x$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:killed-law} is both the physical mass balance and a mandatory
numerical invariant.  This volume-reactivity killing representation is part of
the established Doi/area-reactivity and Feynman--Kac framework
\cite{isaacsonMauroNewby2016uniform,pruestelMeierSchellersheim2014area,
bressloff2022feynmanKac}; the novelty claim does not attach to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:killed-law} itself.

\subsection{Physical two-dimensional quotient and natural-decay realization}
\label{sec:d2-natural-decay}

The finite-parameter branch below targets physical dimension two.  Translation
invariance in the common transverse coordinate gives the exact quotient

\begin{equation}
 \Omega_\infty
 =\mathbb R_z\times\mathbb R_{r_\parallel}\times\mathbb T_W,
 \qquad x=(z,r_\parallel,r_\perp),
 \label{eq:d2-unbounded-quotient}
\end{equation}

where the last coordinate is periodic with period $W$.  For two
equal-diffusivity particles the free forward operator is

\begin{align}
 \mathcal Lq&=-\nabla\!\cdot(bq)
              +\nabla\!\cdot(\bm D\nabla q),\nonumber\\
 \bm D&=\operatorname{diag}(D/2,2D,2D),\qquad
 b=\bigl(-\gamma(z-\bar z),-\gamma r_\parallel,0\bigr).
 \label{eq:d2-free-generator}
\end{align}

There is no reflecting face in either unbounded coordinate.  Natural decay
means the weighted-space realization below, rather than a pointwise boundary
condition at infinity.

For the physical four-slab family, specialize the generic field in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget-field} to

\begin{equation}
 V_{\bm w}(z,r)
 =\frac{1}{W}\bm1_{\{|r|_{\mathrm{mi}}<a\}}
   \sum_{j=1}^{J}w_j\phi_j(z),
 \qquad K_{B,\bm w}=B V_{\bm w},
 \label{eq:d2-sharp-killing}
\end{equation}

where $0<a<W/2$, the bounded nonnegative profiles obey
$\int_{\mathbb R}\phi_j(z)\,\dd z=1$, and $\bm w$ is a real simplex
control.  The factor $W^{-1}$ accounts for the omitted common transverse
coordinate and preserves the centre-space budget in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:conserved-budget}.  The sharp disk contact is retained; replacing
it by a smoothed profile would define a different model.

The normalized reversible density is

\begin{align}
 \pi(x)&=Z_\pi^{-1}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\gamma(z-\bar z)^2}{D}
             -\frac{\gamma r_\parallel^2}{4D}\right],\nonumber\\
 Z_\pi&=\frac{2\pi DW}{\gamma},
 &\bm D\nabla\log\pi&=b,
 \label{eq:d2-reversible-density}
\end{align}

with the uniform factor on $\mathbb T_W$ included in $Z_\pi$.  Put

\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_\pi&=L^2(\Omega_\infty,\pi\,\dd x),\nonumber\\
 X_\pi&=L^2(\Omega_\infty,\pi^{-1}\dd x),
 &Uu&=\pi u .
 \label{eq:d2-weighted-spaces}
\end{align}

The map $U:\mathcal H_\pi\to X_\pi$ is unitary.  Let
$\mathcal C=C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^2)\otimes C^\infty(\mathbb T_W)$ and let
$\mathcal V$ be its closure in the norm

\begin{equation}
 \|u\|_{\mathcal V}^2
 =\|u\|_{\mathcal H_\pi}^2+
  \int_{\Omega_\infty}
  (\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bm D\nabla u\,\pi\,\dd x .
 \label{eq:d2-form-domain}
\end{equation}

For fixed $B\ge0$ and $\bm w$, define

\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak a_{B,\bm w}(u,v)
 =\int_{\Omega_\infty}
   (\nabla u)^{\mathsf T}\bm D\nabla v\,\pi\,\dd x
  +B\int_{\Omega_\infty}V_{\bm w}uv\,\pi\,\dd x .
 \label{eq:d2-killed-form}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Proposition 1 (physical natural-decay realization).---}
For the data above, $\mathfrak a_{B,\bm w}$ is a densely defined, closed,
symmetric, nonnegative Dirichlet form on the same domain $\mathcal V$ as the
free form.  In particular,

\begin{equation}
 \|u\|_{\mathcal V}^2
 \le \|u\|_{\mathcal H_\pi}^2+\mathfrak a_{B,\bm w}(u,u)
 \le(1+B\|V_{\bm w}\|_\infty)\|u\|_{\mathcal V}^2 .
 \label{eq:d2-form-equivalence}
\end{equation}

Its unique nonnegative self-adjoint operator $\mathsf H_{B,\bm w}$ generates a
symmetric analytic sub-Markov contraction semigroup, and

\begin{equation}
 A_{B,\bm w}=U(-\mathsf H_{B,\bm w})U^{-1}
 \label{eq:d2-natural-realization}
\end{equation}

is the form-associated natural-decay realization of
$\mathcal L-BM_{V_{\bm w}}$.  Its action agrees with the displayed algebraic
generator on $\mathcal C$; no essential-self-adjointness assertion for the
minimal core operator is required.

Let $q_0\in X_\pi$ be nonnegative with unit mass and put $u_0=q_0/\pi$.  Define
the budget-normalized observable

\begin{equation}
 F_{B,\bm w}(t)
 =\langle V_{\bm w},q_{B,\bm w}(t)\rangle
 =B^{-1}f_{B,\bm w}(t)\quad(B>0).
 \label{eq:d2-normalized-observable}
\end{equation}

For $r=0,1,2$ and $t\in[\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)$,

\begin{align}
 F_{B,\bm w}^{(r)}(t)
 &=(-1)^r\left\langle V_{\bm w},
   \mathsf H_{B,\bm w}^{\,r}
   \e^{-t\mathsf H_{B,\bm w}}u_0\right\rangle_{\mathcal H_\pi},
 \nonumber\\
 \sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}|F_{B,\bm w}^{(r)}(t)|
 &\le
 \|V_{\bm w}\|_{\mathcal H_\pi}C_r(\tau)
 \|u_0\|_{\mathcal H_\pi},
 \label{eq:d2-positive-time-bound}\\
 C_0(\tau)&=1,\qquad
 C_r(\tau)=\left(\frac{r}{e\tau}\right)^r\quad(r=1,2).
 \nonumber
\end{align}

Moreover, $q_{B,\bm w}(t)\ge0$, $F_{B,\bm w}(t)\ge0$, and

\begin{equation}
 \int_{\Omega_\infty}q_{B,\bm w}(t)\,\dd x
 +B\int_0^tF_{B,\bm w}(s)\,\dd s=1 .
 \label{eq:d2-integrated-mass}
\end{equation}

The Gaussian integrals and direct differentiation give
Eq.~\eqref{eq:d2-reversible-density}.  Bounded nonnegative multiplication
preserves the closed free form, its domain, and its Dirichlet property.  The
representation and spectral theorems then give
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:d2-natural-realization} and
\eqref{eq:d2-positive-time-bound}; Gaussian cutoffs allow the weak evolution to
be tested against the constant function and yield
Eq.~\eqref{eq:d2-integrated-mass}.  Full details and the bounded-box companion
statement are given in Secs.~S1 and S3 of the Supplemental Material.

This proposition identifies the unbounded physical target and its
positive-time observable calculus.  The reflecting boxes used below are
approximants, not boundary conditions of that target.  For the twelve
formula-defined fixed-box dyadic families, a separate ideal composition now
establishes the reconstructed-resolvent, Mosco, and positive-time conclusions
from its source-bound premises.  That closure concerns only the ideal
formula-defined refinement tails.  Equality with the production geometry at
level \(n=0\) is not a correlated containment receipt: it does not show that
one common ideal mass, flux, gauge, map, and killing member lies inside the
saved production intervals, and it does not close the production
gauge/evaluator bridge.  Project-level and production-level complete C1
therefore remain false.  Computable spatial and box-truncation errors for time
orders \(r=0,1,2\), together with a margin-bearing transfer of the complete
stationary-point signature to \(\Omega_\infty\), also remain open.  Proposition
1 licenses no production finite-volume-to-continuum topology claim.

\section{Analytical results}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Compact-positive-time weak-reactivity transfer}

Choose an interior reference allocation $\bar{\bm w}$ and a fixed matrix
$P\in\mathbb R^{J\times(J-1)}$ whose columns span
$\{h:\bm1^{\mathsf T}h=0\}$.  Write
$\bm w(\bm\theta)=\bar{\bm w}+P\bm\theta$ and let
$\Theta\subset\mathbb R^{J-1}$ be compact with all its weights strictly
positive.  Write $K_{B,\bm w}=B V_{\bm w}$ and set

\begin{equation}
 F_B(t,\bm\theta)=B^{-1}f_{B,\bm w(\bm\theta)}(t),\qquad
 G(t,\bm\theta)=
 \langle V_{\bm w(\bm\theta)},\e^{t\mathcal L}q_0\rangle.
 \label{eq:normalized-free-exposure}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Proposition 2 (mixed-jet transfer).---}
Let the free generator, with the declared reflecting/periodic conditions, be
similar to a nonpositive self-adjoint generator on either the bounded
$L^2$ space or the reversible unbounded space
$X_\pi=L^2(\pi^{-1}\dd x)$.  Assume $q_0$ belongs to that state space and that
$V_{\bm w(\bm\theta)}$ is affine in $\bm\theta$, uniformly bounded as a
multiplication operator on a complex control tube around $\Theta$, and belongs
to the corresponding observable dual.  Then, on
$I=[\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)$, for every fixed time order $r$ and finite
control multi-index $\alpha$,

\begin{equation}
 \sup_{I\times\Theta}
 \left|\partial_t^r\partial_{\bm\theta}^{\alpha}
       (F_B-G)\right|\le C_{r,\alpha,I,\Theta}B.
 \label{eq:mixed-jet-transfer}
\end{equation}

The constant is finite for the fixed tube and fixed data and is independent of
$B$ on a declared bounded interval.  The proof, including the exact Duhamel
sensitivity hierarchy and the bounded/unbounded analytic-semigroup
realizations, is contained in Secs.~S2--S3 of the Supplemental working source.
The Dyson expansion and time/control Cauchy estimates then preserve any fixed
finite collection of strict root-tube, curvature, complement, or
projected-rank margins once those objects have been explicitly defined.  Only
the $C^2$ time part is needed for Theorem~1 below.  The statement is local on a
compact positive-time window: it is not uniform on $t=O(B^{-1})$, does not
identify a useful numerical value of $B$, and supplies no event-mass lower
bound.  The complete exact-$m$ proof, including its global zero count,
full-complement certificate, slow-factor stability, and fixed-$\varepsilon$
Doi transfer, is given in the Supplemental working source.  This document
nevertheless remains a working paper, not a submission package, because the
finite-parameter two-dimensional validation and publication-integrity gates
remain open.

\section{Finite-parameter evidence boundary}
\label{sec:evidence-boundary}

The theorem above proves existence inside an explicitly constructed,
parameter-dependent family.  It does not certify the broad four-slab
finite-volume family used to seek a practical two-dimensional control.  Earlier
computations in that family provide design information only: one unchanged
allocation at $B=0.01$ retained five alternating stationary roots on odd
meshes $N=113$ and $N=129$ in one reflected box and the same finite-volume
solver family.  The saved floating-point screen searched roots only through
$t=35$, while basin and survival calculations extended to $t=100$; no
post-$35$ root-exclusion certificate was made.  Those calculations neither
exclude later extrema nor establish parity, alignment, box, continuum, or
independent-method robustness.  A later allocation-cusp search did not produce
a claimable branch and is not used here.

Three exact-rational, same-budget allocations have instead been fixed for a
prospective one-/two-/three-mode test.  Their positive-budget values have not
been evaluated or read in this working process.  No statement here asserts
that those controls realize the
target topologies, that a finite-budget cusp exists, or that a bounded
finite-volume calculation represents an unbounded continuum law.

\section{Reproducible two-dimensional validation design}
\label{sec:validation-design}

Before any positive-budget value may be evaluated or read, the transport,
contact radius, initial law, slab supports, installed budget, allocation
vectors, observation window, acceptance margins, rate-defined generator, and
common-observable rule must be frozen and independently accepted.  The
deterministic campaign then executes the predeclared 36-row mesh, parity,
alignment, and box family without refitting or adaptive repair after a first
scientific value is seen.

\subsection{Verified finite-volume propagation}

For each prescribed mesh, parity, slab alignment, and box, a rate-defined
uniformization calculation must enclose the density, its first two time
derivatives, survival, and valley-partitioned masses.  A separately replayable
interval certificate must include every stationary point with strict curvature
sign and exclude all other stationary points on the complete declared window.
Mass balance and tail truncation are checked in the same arithmetic envelope;
implementation-level provenance and memory-isolation controls belong to the
reproducibility record rather than to the physical claim.

The control-free preparation covers all 12 prospectively fixed production
configurations.  Exact axis partitions, free-axis forward/backward rate
enclosures, initial marginals, and sparse tensor boxes are stored in a
canonical file-backed bundle representing 34,787,462 states.  The historical
file role \texttt{stationary\_mass} does not contain a physical cell integral:
on axis \(a\) it is the ungauged representative quadrature primitive
\[
 \widetilde\mu_{a,i}
 =|C_{a,i}|\exp[-\Phi_a(x_{a,i}^{\rm rep})].
\]
It is neither box-normalized nor equal, in general, to
\(\int_{C_{a,i}}\pi_a(x)\,\dd x\).

A same-core artifact implementation reconstructs every canonical byte.  A
separate-source implementation checks containment of the partitions, directed
rates, ungauged representative primitives, initial marginals, and active
tensor components.  This is source-level semantic containment, not an
independent numerical backend.

Separate authenticated fixed-row sources now enclose the physical axis-cell
integrals and reconstruct the formula-defined common edge flux
\(\kappa_{a,i+1/2}\), the corresponding directed rates, and the factorized
density ratio.  Their producers and independently written validators use the
same pinned \texttt{gmpy2}/MPFR backend.  Authentication of the executed bytes
and runtime therefore supplies provenance, not backend independence.  These
fixed-row sources do not by themselves give a genuine refinement theorem or
one correlated same-member production receipt.

The canonical rate files are joined to native packed payloads and
round-tripped byte for byte.  Two serialized replays of the initial bundle and
a further two-repeat replay of the control-free factorized killing geometry
close only their declared file/process boundaries.  The latter contains no
concrete control, positive budget, tensor killing diagonal, reconstructed
multiplier, or full operator.  Separate ideal records define twelve genuine
dyadic refinement sequences and source-bound symbolic map, cut-layer, and
killing-residual estimates, but they do not bind the production member.
Consequently no external predecessor commitment or fresh ordered roles
8--10 replay exists.  Same-member containment, the production resource gate,
F0--F3, complete C1--C3, continuum topology and componentwise root transfer,
the physical control claim, release, and submission eligibility all remain
held.

The three controls are accepted as one family only if all prescribed
configurations retain, respectively, exactly one, two, and three maxima and
the associated basin masses remain above the declared numerical resolution
floor.  A failure of any configuration is reported at that bounded scope; it
is not repaired by dropping a mesh, changing a threshold, or replacing a
control.

\subsection{Common observables and an independent event law}

Only after the complete deterministic family is independently accepted may a
predeclared mechanical rule form common basin cuts and time windows from its
envelopes.  The off-lattice killed-process calculation then uses unchanged
physical inputs and two independent random-number pools: one for prescribed
precision decisions and one for the reported estimates.  Simultaneous
confidence intervals test basin probabilities, survival, and common-window
contrasts.
Trajectory samples do not certify time derivatives or a continuous-density
norm, and repeated quadrature or sparse-matrix evaluation of the same
finite-volume generator is not an independent physical method.

\subsection{Continuum claim boundary}

A claim of a resolved physical two-dimensional realization requires agreement
of all retained root intervals, topology margins, survival values, and basin
masses across the full mesh/parity/alignment/box family, followed by the
off-lattice comparison.  The result would remain finite-window and
same-family: three fixed controls do not by themselves establish a cusp,
global phase diagram, topology outside the window, or a positive-budget
three-dimensional result.
Even complete agreement in that deterministic and off-lattice campaign would
constitute continuum-consistent evidence only on the declared finite window;
it would not certify convergence from a finite-volume scheme to the unbounded
continuum.  That certification additionally requires the C0--C3 map,
same-member, quantitative discretization, and box-truncation chain together
with margin-bearing componentwise root transfer.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The analytical mechanism is constructive.  A monotone midpoint trajectory
converts separated catalyst locations into separated exposure clocks; narrow
Gaussian convolution creates one peak near each target time; a global zero
bound and a whole-window sign certificate make the count exhaustive; and a
weak-reactivity mixed-jet estimate preserves the resulting topology in the Doi
law.  This establishes arbitrary prescribed \emph{fixed finite} modality
without appealing to a reduced-clock universality claim.

The restrictions are scientifically consequential.  Geometry and smallness
scales depend on $d$ and $m$.  The deterministic relative trajectory stays
strictly inside the contact set on the designed window, and the free contact
probability tends to one there in $C^2$ as the small-noise scale vanishes; no
pathwise Brownian confinement is claimed.  The limits are sequential, and
observability at a common useful budget is not guaranteed.  The next
contribution is therefore not another parameter scan.
It is the independently reproducible two-dimensional validation described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:validation-design}.  Until that chain is complete, the theorem
and the historical computations remain separate results.
