\title{Prescribing finite-window reaction-time modes with a static fixed-budget Doi reactivity field}

\author{Xiaoxiao Zhouyi}
\email{xiaoxiao.zhouyi@bristol.ac.uk}
\affiliation{School of Engineering Mathematics and Technology, University of Bristol,
Bristol BS8 1TW, United Kingdom}

\date{August 14, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We ask whether a fixed amount of static spatial reactivity can prescribe
reaction-time modes.  For a two-particle Doi process, we prove that for every
fixed finite \(d\ge2\) and \(m\ge1\) there is a nonnegative lab-frame
reactivity field of fixed centre-coordinate mass
\(\int\kappa(c)\,\dd c=B\) for which the resulting reaction-time density has
exactly \(m\) nondegenerate
maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate minima on a declared compact positive-time
window.  Ordered narrow slabs along a monotone mean trajectory generate
separated Gaussian exposure clocks.  A global derivative-zero bound and
whole-window sign certificate establish this signature uniformly over a
compact family of positive slab weights; mixed-jet convergence on positive
times transfers it to the killed process for sufficiently small positive
\(B\).  Off-lattice simulations realize the construction at moderate noise
and order-one event mass, map noise--budget mode-retention boundaries, and
show predictable loss by peak merger or depletion.  The result does not
claim novelty for multimodal first-passage laws, topology outside the chosen
window, or parameters uniform in \(d\) and \(m\).  The contribution is instead
a constructive finite-window topology theorem under a static nonnegative
fixed-\(L^1\) reactivity constraint, supported by a finite-parameter
realization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reaction-time multimodality is established rather than new.  Strategically
arranged static traps can generate multimodal capture-time densities
\cite{lindsaySpoonmoreTzou2016narrow}, while distinct transport channels,
heterogeneous reaction control, and encounter geometries can create separated
time scales \cite{godecMetzler2017twochannel,
grebenkovMetzlerOshanin2018defocusing,leVotEtAl2022encounter}.  The question
here is therefore not whether several peaks can occur, but whether their
complete finite-window stationary-point topology can be constructed under a
specific static resource constraint.

Several adjacent inverse-design paradigms clarify that constraint.  Classical
inverse first-passage theory uses a time-dependent absorbing boundary to
realize a prescribed survival law \cite{ekstromJanson2016inverse}.  Soft-killing
variants translate a killing profile by a time-dependent barrier and establish
existence or uniqueness for prescribed lifetime distributions
\cite{ettingerEvansHening2014prescribed,
ettingerHeningWong2020inverse,klumpKolb2024soft}.  Finite-state phase-type and
hyper-Erlang constructions generate flexible positive waiting-time laws by
introducing hidden Markov phases
\cite{asmussenNermanOlsson1996phase,thuemmlerBuchholzTelek2006phase}.
Stochastic gating modifies detection times through switching reaction states
\cite{bressloffLawley2015gated,kumarEtAl2023gated}, and kinetic proofreading
uses sequential, energy-consuming reaction steps to impose temporal delays
\cite{hopfield1974proofreading,ninio1975amplification,
mckeithan1995proofreading}.  These results provide broader distributional
control in different design spaces; they do not impose the static spatial
constraint considered here.

Static spatial reactivity under a fixed total resource is itself also
established.  Heterogeneous bulk killing has been studied with fixed total
reactive strength, partially reactive network elements have been rearranged at
fixed total absorbance, and position-dependent Robin reactivity has been
optimized under a fixed integral to maximize steady flux
\cite{nguyenGrebenkov2010porous,scottEtAl2021tubular,
nicolaouMulder2023flux}.  Thus neither static killing, spatial redistribution,
nor an \(L^1\)-type resource constraint is a novelty claim here.  The objective
below is instead the exhaustive transient stationary-point topology of a
two-particle Doi reaction-time density, together with its whole-window
positive-budget transfer.

Our admissible control is a lab-frame, time-independent, nonnegative
centre-coordinate field \(\kappa(c)\), multiplied by a fixed encounter-contact
profile and constrained by
\begin{equation}
 \int\kappa(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:intro-budget}
\end{equation}
Every admissible allocation uses the same installed amount \(B\).  We neither
translate nor activate the field in time, introduce signed reactivity, nor add
an internal gating or proofreading state.  Under these restrictions we ask
whether a prescribed number of reaction-time modes can nevertheless be made
exact on a specified time window.

\paragraph{Main result.}
Fix finite \(d\ge2\), finite \(m\ge1\), and a compact interval
\(I=[\tau,T]\subset(0,\infty)\).  We construct transport and initial data,
contact geometry, and \(m\) ordered normalized catalyst slabs; these data may
depend on \((d,m)\).  There is \(\varepsilon_0>0\) such that, for every
\(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0\), the free-exposure clock has exactly \(m\)
nondegenerate maxima and \(m-1\) nondegenerate minima on \(I\), uniformly for
all slab weights satisfying \(w_j\ge w_*>0\) and \(\sum_jw_j=1\).  For each
fixed such \(\varepsilon\), there is a positive uniform topological threshold
\(B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)\) such that the positive-budget Doi
reaction-time density has the identical stationary signature whenever
\(0<B<B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)\).  For every allocation the field
remains static and satisfies Eq.~\eqref{eq:intro-budget}.

The upper bound of \(m\) modes for an \(m\)-component univariate Gaussian
mixture is classical
\cite{carreiraPerpinanWilliams2003modes,amendolaEngstromHaase2020modes};
it is not the contribution.  The proof burden created by the encounter
process is to construct all \(2m-1\) stationary points with uniform margins,
prove a global derivative-zero bound, exclude additional zeros over the
whole window after multiplication by the contact probability, and preserve
the root tubes, curvature signs, endpoint slopes, and complement margin under
positive-budget Doi killing.  Compact-positive-time mixed-jet convergence
provides the last transfer.

The theorem has a sequential quantifier order and does not supply a common
useful budget, a lower event-mass bound, or parameters uniform in \(d\) and
\(m\).  The off-lattice campaigns therefore serve a separate purpose: they
test finite-parameter realizability, map the operational noise--budget
retention boundary, and identify the predicted peak-merger and depletion
failures.  Their classifier is fixed in advance but operational, and no claim
is made about stationary points outside \(I\).  Doi killing, reaction mass
balance, and spatially heterogeneous reactivity are established ingredients
\cite{doi1976stochastic,isaacsonMauroNewby2016uniform,
pruestelMeierSchellersheim2014area,bressloff2022feynmanKac,
grebenkov2019heterogeneous}.

Let \(q(x,t)\) be the unreacted configuration density of two mobile
particles, with conservative generator \(\mathcal L\).  Write \(r\) for the
relative coordinate and \(c\) for an encounter-centre coordinate.  For a
fixed contact profile \(\chi_a(r)\) and normalized nonnegative centre-space
profiles \(\phi_j(c)\), define
\begin{align}
 \kappa_{\bm w}(c)&=B\sum_{j=1}^{J}w_j\phi_j(c),
 &w_j&\ge0,\nonumber\\
 \sum_jw_j&=1,
 &\int\phi_j(c)\,\dd c&=1,\nonumber\\
 K_{B,\bm w}(x)&=\chi_a(r)\kappa_{\bm w}(c).
 \label{eq:budget-field}
\end{align}
Every admissible allocation has the same installed centre-space resource,
\begin{equation}
 \int\kappa_{\bm w}(c)\,\dd c=B.
 \label{eq:conserved-budget}
\end{equation}
This is not an integral over the complete two-particle configuration space.
The killed evolution, reaction-time density, and survival probability are
\begin{align}
 \partial_tq_{B,\bm w}&=(\mathcal L-K_{B,\bm w})q_{B,\bm w},
 &q_{B,\bm w}(0)&=q_0,\nonumber\\
 f_{B,\bm w}(t)&=\langle K_{B,\bm w},q_{B,\bm w}(t)\rangle,
 &-S'_{B,\bm w}(t)&=f_{B,\bm w}(t).
 \label{eq:killed-law}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:killed-law} is the usual mass balance for a Doi
volume-reaction process, not a novelty claim.

For longitudinal catalyst slabs, translation invariance of the common
transverse coordinate gives an exact quotient
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_\infty
 =\mathbb R_z\times\mathbb R_{r_\parallel}\times
   \mathbb T_W^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
In the construction below the midpoint \(Z_t\) moves monotonically in mean,
the relative process remains a positive distance inside the contact region
along its deterministic trajectory on the chosen window, and each normalized
slab is centred at the midpoint mean at a target time.  The
\(W^{-(d-1)}\) factor in the killing field accounts for the omitted common
transverse directions and preserves Eq.~\eqref{eq:conserved-budget}.

\section{Positive-time transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

Write \(K_{B,\bm w}=BV_{\bm w}\) and introduce interior affine control
coordinates \(\bm w(\bm\theta)=\bar{\bm w}+P\bm\theta\).  The
budget-rescaled reaction density and the free-exposure law are
\begin{equation}
 F_B(t,\bm\theta)=B^{-1}f_{B,\bm w(\bm\theta)}(t),
 \qquad
 G(t,\bm\theta)=
 \langle V_{\bm w(\bm\theta)},\e^{t\mathcal L}q_0\rangle.
 \label{eq:normalized-exposure}
\end{equation}
On either a bounded reflecting/periodic quotient or the reversible
natural-decay realization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:quotient}, assume \(q_0\) belongs
to the corresponding state space and \(V_{\bm w}\) is bounded on a complex
control neighbourhood of a compact interior control set.  A
Dyson expansion followed by time and control Cauchy estimates gives, for
every fixed time order \(r\) and finite control multi-index \(\alpha\),
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{(t,\bm\theta)\in[\tau,T]\times\Theta}
 \left|
 \partial_t^r\partial_{\bm\theta}^{\alpha}(F_B-G)
 \right|
 \le C_{r,\alpha,\tau,T,\Theta}B.
 \label{eq:mixed-jet}
\end{equation}
The Supplemental Material accompanying this article
\cite{supplementalMaterial} gives the explicit constant and proof.  In
particular, \(C^2\) convergence on a compact positive-time window preserves
disjoint stationary-point neighbourhoods, curvature signs, endpoint slopes,
and a nonzero derivative margin on their complement.  The estimate is local
in positive time and provides neither a useful numerical value of \(B\) nor
control on the scale \(t=O(B^{-1})\).

\input{exact_m_theorem_spine.tex}

Three thresholds are kept distinct.  The theorem concerns the maximal
uniform topology-preservation threshold
\(B_{\rm top}^{\rm unif}(\varepsilon)\).  For one fixed allocation, the
Supplemental Material \cite{supplementalMaterial} derives a closed-form
sufficient value \(B_{\rm cert}(\varepsilon;\bm w,\{U_i\})\), from a flat
Dyson-chain estimate, such that every
\(0<B<B_{\rm cert}\) preserves the free-clock signature.  Only this
sufficient value, along the fixed anchor family used in its evaluation, has
the established asymptotic form
\begin{equation}
 \log B_{\rm cert}
 =-\frac{c_B}{\varepsilon^2}
  +O\!\left(\log\frac1\varepsilon\right),
 \qquad c_B>0.
\label{eq:bcert-rate}
\end{equation}
Here \(\{U_i\}\) is the selected system of disjoint root tubes; this
dependence is suppressed below.  The construction proves only
\(B_{\rm cert}\le B_{\rm top}(\varepsilon;\{\bm w\})\), not a bound for
the threshold uniform over the full allocation family.
High-precision, non-interval evaluations using the same relative-coordinate
initial law as the simulations give nominal values
\(\widehat B_{\rm cert}\simeq8.9\times10^{-15}\) for the \(m=2\),
\(\varepsilon=0.1\) anchor and \(2.6\times10^{-7}\) for the corresponding
\(m=3\) geometry.  By contrast, \(B_{\rm op}\) below is a finite-sample
crossing of a stated classifier: \(B_{\rm op}\simeq7.6\) and \(3.6\),
respectively.  It is not identified with either \(B_{\rm top}\) or
\(B_{\rm cert}\).  The large gap is dominated by the whole-window
left-endpoint derivative margin, while the weighted-norm obstruction of the
direct \(L^2\) route is absent from the flat-pairing formula.

\section{Numerical realization}
\label{sec:numerics}

The theorem is an existence statement with a deliberately sequential
quantifier order, and by itself does not identify a practically resolved
parameter set.  This section reports an off-lattice time-discrete simulation
of the same continuum model at moderate noise and order-one budgets, designed to
test the theorem's falsifiable finite-parameter implications, including its
predicted failure directions.  Table~\ref{tab:design-recipe} summarizes the
construction as the checkable procedure that the campaigns below execute.

\subsection{Method and sanity checks}
\label{sec:numerics-method}

No spatial lattice is introduced.  The midpoint, longitudinal, and
transverse coordinates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-m-process} are integrated by
Euler--Maruyama steps of size \(\Delta t=10^{-3}\), and Doi killing is
realized by per-step thinning with probability
\(1-\exp(-K_{B,\bm w,\varepsilon}\,\Delta t)\) evaluated at the current
position.  All runs use deterministically seeded counter-based random
streams, one substream per walker chunk.  Two automated checks accompany
every configuration: the per-step kill probability must remain in the unit
interval (largest value \(5.6\times10^{-3}\) across all production
configurations), and event counts must balance mass exactly (kills plus
survivors equal walkers).  Separately, a campaign-level zero-budget twin run
accumulates about \(10^{7}\) contact steps and produces no kills.  A
\(\Delta t\)-halving twin of the
\((m,\varepsilon,B)=(2,0.1,1)\) configuration reproduces both peak times at
bin resolution and both peak prominences to within \(0.3\%\), and the
\(d=3\) run passes a per-bin \(\Delta t\)-halving comparison with maximum
\(|z|=1.67\).  Modality is decided by a fixed operational
classifier: reaction times are histogrammed with bin width \(0.02\) and
smoothed with a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth \(0.04\), and a local maximum
counts as a mode only if its topographic prominence (peak minus contour
base) exceeds both five covariance-aware smoothed-Poisson standard
deviations and \(5\%\) of the maximum smoothed height on the window
\(I=[0.5,3.5]\).  Peak and contour base are linear statistics of the raw
bin counts, so the prominence variance is
\(\sum_j (A_{p,j}-A_{b,j})^2\,C_j/(N\delta)^2\), with \(A\) the kernel
smoothing weights, \(C_j\) the raw counts, \(N\) the walker count, and
\(\delta\) the bin width; a peak-only Poisson sigma, which omits the base
variance and the peak--base covariance, would only overstate the
significance ratio of counted maxima (by factors up to \(1.55\) across the
retained campaign) and is not used.  Post-hoc sensitivity analysis over bandwidths
\(0.03\)--\(0.05\) and relative-prominence floors \(3\%\)--\(7\%\) leaves
the principal anchors unchanged but changes the verdict in four of the 96
phase-diagram cells adjacent to the reported boundary.  Complete method
details, configuration tables, retained histogram counts, and additional
figures are given in the Supplemental Material \cite{supplementalMaterial}.

\subsection{Production grid at \texorpdfstring{$m=2$ and $m=3$}{m=2 and m=3}}
\label{sec:numerics-production}

Eighteen production configurations with \(5\times10^{6}\) walkers each
realize the slab design of Theorem~1 at \(d=2\).  For \(m=2\), all twelve
configurations across
\(\varepsilon\in\{0.1,0.2\}\), \(B\in\{0.5,1,2\}\), and weights
\((\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\) and \((0.7,0.3)\) are classified as bimodal under
the fixed rule,
with the weakest counted peak still \(72\) Poisson standard deviations
prominent.  Event mass is order one throughout: between \(0.29\) and
\(0.92\) of all walkers react inside the window.  The early peak sits within
\(0.01\)--\(0.03\) of its target time in every configuration; the late peak
is pulled earlier by progressive depletion as the budget grows, by
\(0.05\)--\(0.11\) at \(B=0.5\), \(0.09\)--\(0.19\) at \(B=1\), and
\(0.15\)--\(0.29\) at \(B=2\).

For \(m=3\) at \(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(B=1\), the fixed classifier identifies
three significant maxima for both the uniform and the
\((0.5,0.3,0.2)\) allocations, near
the targets \((0.8,1.6,2.8)\), with window event mass \(0.66\)--\(0.74\).
At \(\varepsilon=0.2\), \(B=1\), both allocations lose the third mode ---
consistent with the heuristic design margin becoming marginal: the minimum adjacent
centre spacing equals \(2.30\sigma\) at \(\varepsilon=0.2\), at the
\(C\approx2.3\) spacing boundary of Table~\ref{tab:design-recipe}, and the
exact free clock's third-peak prominence there is only \(5.1\%\) of the
window maximum, marginally above the classifier's \(5\%\) floor.  Reducing
the budget to \(B=0.25\) at uniform weights partially restores the third
maximum, which is visible at \(t=2.49\) but uncertified: its prominence is
\(4.8\sigma\), below the five-sigma gate, and \(2.7\%\) of the maximum
height, below the \(5\%\) floor, so the strict classifier records two
modes; consistently, the threshold study below certifies no third-mode
crossing anywhere in the scanned budget range for this column.

\subsection{Robustness and failure modes}
\label{sec:numerics-robustness}

Beyond the theorem's hypotheses, a jitter campaign perturbs the slab centres
by independent Gaussian displacements of scale \(\eta s\) (with \(s\) the
minimum adjacent centre spacing) and the weights by a
\(\mathrm{Dirichlet}(50\,\bar{\bm w})\) draw, with \(50\) replicas of
\(2\times10^{5}\) walkers per point at the \(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(B=1\)
anchors.  The target classifier mode count survives in at least \(96\%\) of
replicas up
to \(\eta=0.15\) for both designs, decaying to \(46\%\) (\(m=2\)) and
\(40\%\) (\(m=3\)) at \(\eta=0.4\); all \(50\) weight-jitter replicas
retain the target classifier mode count for both designs.  The observed
pattern is predominantly consistent with geometric merger: replica-level
classifier verdicts agree with the
semi-analytic verdict of each replica's own perturbed geometry in
\(86\)--\(100\%\) of cases, and the mismatches preclude a purely geometric
attribution.  Classified failures are associated mainly with mergers of
adjacent peaks when jittered spacing collapses, rather than with extra peaks.

\subsection{Consistent dimensionalization of the numerical anchor}
\label{sec:units-mapping}

Comparison of the generator and stochastic differential equations gives the
physical single-particle diffusivity \(D=\varepsilon^2D_0\), not \(D_0\).
With length and time units \(1~\mu\mathrm m\) and \(1~\mathrm s\), the
\(\varepsilon=0.1\), \(D_0=1\) anchor therefore corresponds to
\(D=0.01~\mu\mathrm m^2\mathrm s^{-1}\).  Retaining the simulated
\(W=1\) gives a \(1~\mu\mathrm m\) transverse period and, for the
equal-weight \(m=3\), \(B=1\) case, an on-contact single-slab peak rate of
\(1.33~\mathrm s^{-1}\).  This is a dimensionalization of the simulated
point, not evidence that \(B=1\) lies below \(B_{\rm top}\); the full
mapping is given in the Supplemental Material.

\subsection{What the numerics establish, and what they do not}
\label{sec:finite-scope}

The campaigns give finite-parameter evidence that the designed signatures
can be observed at moderate noise, order-one budgets, and order-one event
mass.  They also locate failures in the anticipated directions: depletion at
large budget, clock merger as noise or centre jitter grows, and loss of a
free-clock maximum when its separation margin closes.  These are classifier
outcomes, not exact root counts for the time-discretized process.

No spatial lattice is introduced, but the simulations retain both time-step
and sampling error.  The \(\Delta t\)-halving comparisons are consistency
checks, not a convergence theorem.  Three independent seeds agree on all 18
verdicts across six targeted configurations, and the \(m=3\) and \(m=5\)
interior anchors retain their mode counts when \(\Delta t\) is halved.
However, one independent \(\Delta t/2\) run of the boundary-adjacent
\((m,\varepsilon,B)=(3,0.175,0.5)\) cell changes from three to two classified
modes because the late candidate peak falls below both classifier
thresholds.
Since the step size also changes the random-number stream, this comparison
does not isolate time-step from sampling error; the displayed phase-grid
verdict is resolution/stream-sensitive.  Nothing numerical is uniform in
\((d,m)\).  The sufficient-bound formula \(B_{\rm cert}\) and the
operational
crossing \(B_{\rm op}\) differ by roughly fifteen decades at the matched
\(m=2\) anchor and seven for \(m=3\); the displayed \(B_{\rm cert}\) values
are reported as \(\widehat B_{\rm cert}\): high-precision evaluations without
interval enclosures.  Finally, the
principal anchors are stable over the tested classifier ranges, whereas four
of the 96 phase-diagram cells change verdict under the sensitivity sweep and
the \((m,\varepsilon)=(3,0.2)\) column lies at the relative-prominence floor.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The theorem occupies a narrow gap between spatial first-passage theory and
inverse timing design.  Relative to strategically arranged absorbing traps,
it provides an arbitrary prescribed fixed finite mode count, an exhaustive
maxima--minima count on a declared window, uniformity over a compact family
of positive allocations, and transfer to a two-particle positive-budget Doi
law under a conserved centre-coordinate resource.  Relative to inverse
first-passage constructions, it uses a static lab-frame field but prescribes
only finite-window topology rather than an arbitrary lifetime distribution.
Relative to phase-type, gated, and proofreading mechanisms, it introduces no
hidden reaction phase or time-dependent switch.  The novelty is this
conjunction of constraints, not multimodality by itself.

Mathematically, stationary catalyst locations become separated exposure
clocks.  The affine--exponential zero bound makes the Gaussian-clock count
exhaustive; root-tube and posterior-sector estimates retain a derivative
margin on the entire complement; the contact factor perturbs rather than
creates the clock signature; and compact-positive-time \(C^2\) convergence
transfers all maxima, minima, and curvature signs to the killed law.
Uniformity over \(w_j\ge w_*\) is important: the conclusion is not tied to one
finely tuned allocation, although changing weights on the same support is not
claimed to switch among arbitrary mode counts.

The simulations provide finite-parameter evidence rather than an additional
theorem.  At the scanned parameters they recover the designed classifier mode
counts, locate operational mode-retention boundaries, and show failure through
the same two mechanisms indicated by the proof margins: adjacent-clock merger
as noise or geometric jitter grows, and suppression of a late mode by
depletion as the budget grows.  The semi-analytic free clock is consequently
a useful preflight diagnostic, but the finite-walker classifier, seed,
bandwidth, prominence rule, and time step remain part of the operational
definition near a boundary.

The restrictions are substantive.  First, the deterministic relative
trajectory lies strictly inside the contact region and the free contact
probability approaches one in the small-noise limit.  The modal skeleton is
therefore generated primarily by separated longitudinal exposure clocks, not
by a nontrivial contact-gating effect.  Second, the extension to \(d\ge2\)
uses longitudinal slabs and a transverse symmetry quotient; it demonstrates
dimension compatibility rather than a new dimension-driven geometry.  Third,
the normalized slab height scales as \(1/\varepsilon\), so the fixed-\(L^1\)
constraint imposes neither an independent peak-rate cap nor a minimum feature
size.  Fourth, \(B_{\rm top}\) is a topology threshold,
\(B_{\rm cert}\) is only a fixed-allocation sufficient value, and
\(B_{\rm op}\) is a classifier-dependent finite-sample crossing; none is
identified with another.

Nothing is proved about topology outside \(I\), a parameter choice uniform in
\(d\) or \(m\), a common positive event-mass floor, or mode-count switching on
one fixed support family.  Natural next questions are whether prescribed
modality persists under simultaneous \(L^1\) and \(L^\infty\) constraints,
under a minimum catalyst width, or when the contact probability remains
genuinely nontrivial throughout the design window.  Within the stated scope,
the result gives a constructive topology theorem for a static
resource-constrained Doi field and a finite-parameter test of its mechanism.
