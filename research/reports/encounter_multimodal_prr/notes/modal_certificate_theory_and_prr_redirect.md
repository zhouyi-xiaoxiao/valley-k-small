## 1. Exposure-clock formulation

Let fixed nonnegative catalyst profiles produce free-exposure clocks

\[
 g_j(t)=\langle V_j,T_0(t)q_0\rangle,\qquad j=1,\ldots,J,
\]

on a compact positive-time window \(I=[\tau,T]\).  For normalized profiles
and a conserved allocation \(w\in\Delta_{J-1}\),

\[
 G_w(t)=\sum_{j=1}^J w_j g_j(t),\qquad
 w_j\ge0,\quad \sum_jw_j=1 .
\]

This is the exact \(B\downarrow0\) normalized Doi density.  Geometry and
dimension enter only through the clocks.  The optimization layer below does
not assume Gaussian clocks, a particular physical dimension, or a cusp.

For positive installed budget \(B\), write

\[
 F_{B,w}(t)=B^{-1}f_{B,w}(t).
\]

The existing mixed-jet theorem supplies explicit errors

\[
 \|\partial_t^r(F_{B,w}-G_w)\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le E_r(B),
 \qquad r=1,2,
\]

uniformly on a declared compact control set.  These errors provide the later
positive-budget transfer rule; they do not certify the present numerical
value \(B=0.01\) without an actual quantitative margin comparison.

## 2. Alternating-sign certificate

### Theorem 2.1 (ordered non-strict-extremum lower bound)

Let

\[
 \tau<s_1<s_2<\cdots<s_{2m}<T
\]

and suppose \(G_w\in C^1(I)\) obeys the strict alternating inequalities

\[
 (-1)^{\ell+1}G_w'(s_\ell)>0,
 \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,2m.
\]

Then \(G_w\) has at least \(m\) non-strict local maxima in the disjoint intervals
\((s_{2k-1},s_{2k})\), and at least \(m-1\) intervening local minima in
\((s_{2k},s_{2k+1})\).  The statement is local to the declared window and
does not exclude extra stationary points.  The selected extrema can be
degenerate or nonisolated (including a flat platform); this theorem alone is
therefore not a robust or exact topology certificate.

#### Proof

On an odd--even interval \([a,b]\), \(G_w'(a)>0\) implies that \(a\) cannot
maximize \(G_w\) on \([a,b]\), while \(G_w'(b)<0\) implies the same for \(b\).
The extreme-value theorem therefore places a maximizer in \((a,b)\), where it
is a non-strict local maximum.  Applying the same argument to a negative--
positive interval places an interior non-strict local minimum there.  The
open intervals are disjoint and ordered, so the selected extrema are
distinct.  \(\square\)

A pair of endpoint derivative signs proves an extremum but not strictness,
isolation, uniqueness, or nonzero curvature.
Those stronger properties are supplied by the following certificate or by a
separate interval/root validation.

### Theorem 2.2 (unique nondegenerate boxes)

Let \(G_w\in C^2(I)\).  Choose disjoint peak boxes
\(P_k=[a_k,b_k]\subset(\tau,T)\) and valley boxes
\(Q_k=[c_k,d_k]\subset(\tau,T)\), in the required alternating order.  If for
fixed positive margins
\(\eta_{\rm p},\eta_{\rm v},\kappa_{\rm p},\kappa_{\rm v}\),

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
G_w'(a_k)\ge\eta_{\rm p},&G_w'(b_k)\le-\eta_{\rm p},
&G_w''(t)\le-\kappa_{\rm p}\quad(t\in P_k),\\[1mm]
G_w'(c_k)\le-\eta_{\rm v},&G_w'(d_k)\ge\eta_{\rm v},
&G_w''(t)\ge\kappa_{\rm v}\quad(t\in Q_k),
\end{array}
\]

then each peak box contains exactly one nondegenerate maximum and each valley
box exactly one nondegenerate minimum.

#### Proof

Strict curvature makes \(G_w'\) strictly monotone in each box.  The endpoint
signs give existence, monotonicity gives uniqueness, and the curvature margin
gives nondegeneracy.  \(\square\)

This is the existing mode-persistence mechanism written as a design
certificate rather than as a post hoc root census.

## 3. Robust allocation is a linear program

At every fixed checkpoint, \(G_w'=w^Tg'\) is linear in \(w\).  Let
\(\sigma_\ell>0\) be a declared, allocation-independent scale, for example

\[
 \sigma_\ell=\max_j|g_j'(s_\ell)|.
\]

Fix an interior floor \(0\le w_{\min}<1/J\).  Every declared
\(\sigma_\ell\) must first be certified strictly positive; otherwise the
selector returns `HOLD_CHECKPOINT_SCALE_ZERO`.  The maximum normalized
alternating-sign margin is the linear program

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{w,\rho}\quad&\rho\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 &(-1)^{\ell+1}\frac{w^Tg'(s_\ell)}{\sigma_\ell}\ge\rho,
   \quad \ell=1,\ldots,2m,\\
 &w_j\ge w_{\min},\qquad \mathbf1^Tw=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus a strictly positive exact optimum is a constructive at-least-\(m\)
non-strict-extremum certificate.  A nonpositive optimum is not a certificate.
The future implementation must distinguish an infeasible extended interval
program, a zero/nonpositive optimum, and a positive optimum.  Among multiple
primary optimizers it must choose the lexicographically smallest weight vector
by a declared sequence of secondary LPs; solver-native arbitrary tie breaking
is not a frozen selector.
There is no nonlinear root finder, cusp continuation, or simplex scan in the
selection rule.

The unique-curvature certificate is also convex once interval enclosures of
the component clocks are available.  If, for example,

\[
 g_j''(t)\le \overline h_{jk}\quad(t\in P_k),
\]

then nonnegative weights imply

\[
 G_w''(t)\le\sum_jw_j\overline h_{jk}.
\]

Consequently upper/lower interval curvature margins add only linear
inequalities.  This uses \(w_j\ge0\) essentially.  The final implementation
must use simultaneous outward-rounded bounds over each entire interval (or an
equally rigorous independently validated enclosure); a dense floating-point
grid is only an exploratory diagnostic.

### Theorem 3.1 (complete finite-window box-and-complement certificate)

Let the peak and valley boxes in Theorem 2.2 form one ordered list
\(B_1,\ldots,B_n\), and let \(R_0,\ldots,R_n\) be the closed gap intervals
from \(\tau\) to the left endpoint of \(B_1\), between successive boxes, and
from the right endpoint of \(B_n\) to \(T\).  Suppose Theorem 2.2 holds in
every box and, for fixed signs \(q_r\in\{-1,+1\}\) and a margin
\(\zeta_*>0\),

\[
 q_rG_w'(t)\ge \zeta_*>0\qquad(t\in R_r),\quad r=0,\ldots,n.
\]

Then the stationary points supplied by the boxes are the complete stationary
list of \(G_w\) on \(I\); in particular, neither endpoint is stationary and
there are no extra roots, including even-multiplicity roots, in the gaps.

#### Proof

Theorem 2.2 supplies exactly one simple, typed root in each box.  The strict
gap bounds exclude every root on the complement, including the time-window
endpoints.  The boxes and gaps cover \(I\), so no other stationary point is
possible.  \(\square\)

The gap constraints remain linear in \(w\) when outward-rounded componentwise
lower bounds on \(q_rg_j'\) are available on every \(R_r\).  Publication-facing
claims of *exactly* one, two, or three modes require this full certificate;
Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 alone support only lower-bound or local-box claims.

Optional fixed-time height, contrast, or exposure constraints are linear as
well.  Event-basin probabilities at positive budget are not free-exposure
linear constraints and remain validation quantities.

## 4. Explicit positive-budget transfer

Let \(\eta_*>0\) be the smallest raw box-endpoint derivative margin in
physical derivative units, let \(\zeta_*>0\) be the smallest raw complement
derivative margin, and let \(\kappa_*>0\) be the certified uniform curvature
margin.  These are not the normalized LP objective or curvatures evaluated
only at floating-point roots.  Let \(E_r^{\rm bd}(B)\) be the explicit
monotone upper bounds registered by the mixed-jet theorem for
\(0\le B\le B_{\max}\).  The estimate transfers the complete certificate
whenever

\[
 E_1^{\rm bd}(B)<\min\{\eta_*,\zeta_*\},\qquad
 E_2^{\rm bd}(B)<\kappa_*.
\]

Define the analytical threshold

\[
 B_{\rm cert}=\sup\left\{b\in[0,B_{\max}]:
 \begin{array}{l}
 \text{for every }\beta\in[0,b],\\[-1mm]
 E_1^{\rm bd}(\beta)<\min\{\eta_*,\zeta_*\},\quad
 E_2^{\rm bd}(\beta)<\kappa_*
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]

For the registered monotone bounds it is equivalent to check only the
endpoint \(b\).  For every \(0<B<B_{\rm cert}\), the same boxes retain their
maxima/minima and the complement remains root-free.  If no complement margin
is certified, delete \(\zeta_*\) and conclude only persistence of the local
box certificate, not exact topology.
This is a computable sufficient bound, not an assertion that it is sharp or
that it reaches \(B=0.01\).  The contraction bound can be conservative,
especially for localized profiles.  A finite-budget calculation and an
independent killed-process method are therefore still required for the broad
finite-parameter realization.

## 5. Modal regions and the discriminant

Let \(W\) be relatively open in the affine hull of the budget simplex and
assume \((t,w)\mapsto F_{B,w}(t)\) is \(C^2\) on an open neighborhood of
\(I\times W\) in time and affine allocation coordinates.  Equivalently, one
may work in relative charts on a simplex face if the same extension property
is stated explicitly.  Define

\[
 \mathcal D_B=\{w\in W:\exists t\in(\tau,T),\
 F_{B,w}'(t)=F_{B,w}''(t)=0\}
\]

and the boundary-crossing set

\[
 \mathcal E_B=\{w\in W:F_{B,w}'(\tau)F_{B,w}'(T)=0\}.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (topology is constant off the discriminant)

For \(w\notin\mathcal D_B\cup\mathcal E_B\), write the ordered stationary
times as \(t_1(w)<\cdots<t_{N(w)}(w)\) and define the discrete finite-window
topology signature

\[
 \Sigma_B(w)=\bigl(N(w);\operatorname{sgn}F_{B,w}''(t_1(w)),\ldots,
 \operatorname{sgn}F_{B,w}''(t_{N(w)}(w))\bigr).
\]

The root times themselves are not part of this discrete signature and may
move continuously with \(w\).

On every path-connected component of
\(W\setminus(\mathcal D_B\cup\mathcal E_B)\), the number, order, and
maximum/minimum types of stationary points of \(F_{B,w}\) in \(I\) are
constant; equivalently, \(\Sigma_B\) is constant.

#### Proof

Fix \(w_0\notin\mathcal D_B\cup\mathcal E_B\).  Its stationary list is finite.
Indeed, an infinite list in compact \(I\) would have an accumulation point
\(t_*\).  If \(t_*\in\{\tau,T\}\), continuity would put \(w_0\) in
\(\mathcal E_B\).  If \(t_*\in(\tau,T)\), continuity gives
\(F_{B,w_0}'(t_*)=0\); were \(F_{B,w_0}''(t_*)\ne0\), local strict
monotonicity of \(F_{B,w_0}'\) would isolate the root, contradicting
accumulation.  Hence \(F_{B,w_0}''(t_*)=0\), which puts \(w_0\) in
\(\mathcal D_B\), again a contradiction.

The endpoint derivatives are nonzero, so continuity gives endpoint
neighborhoods that remain root-free for all allocations near \(w_0\).
Surround the finite simple interior roots by disjoint implicit-function
boxes.  On the compact complement of these boxes and the endpoint
neighborhoods, \(|F_{B,w_0}'|\) has a strictly positive minimum.  Uniform
continuity preserves that bound for nearby allocations, while the implicit
function theorem continues exactly one root in each box and preserves the
sign of \(F_{B,w}''\), hence its maximum/minimum type.  The complete ordered
root family therefore moves continuously locally, whereas the discrete
signature \(\Sigma_B\) is locally constant on
\(W\setminus(\mathcal D_B\cup\mathcal E_B)\).  The pullback of \(\Sigma_B\)
along a path is a locally constant discrete-valued map on a connected
interval, and is thus constant.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 (different complete topologies imply a control boundary)

Suppose two allocations \(w_0,w_1\in W\) have different **complete**
finite-window topology signatures, \(\Sigma_B(w_0)\ne\Sigma_B(w_1)\), with
each signature established either independently or by Theorem 3.1's full
box-and-complement certificate.  Every path joining them meets
\(\mathcal D_B\cup\mathcal E_B\).  A partial at-least or local-box certificate
does not satisfy this hypothesis.  If, on a particular path, both endpoint
derivatives are uniformly bounded away from zero, that path avoids
\(\mathcal E_B\) and must meet \(\mathcal D_B\).

At \((t_*,w_*)\in\mathcal D_B\), choose an allowed budget-tangent direction
\(h\).  For the following local fold statement only, assume joint \(C^3\)
regularity in time and a scalar allocation coordinate along \(h\) (or,
equivalently, existence and continuity of the displayed third and mixed
derivatives).  The regular fold conditions are

\[
 F_t=F_{tt}=0,\qquad F_{ttt}\ne0,\qquad D_hF_t\ne0,
\]

all evaluated at \((t_*,w_*)\).  Equivalently, the Jacobian of
\((F_t,F_{tt})\) with respect to time and the scalar control along \(h\) has
determinant \(-D_hF_t\,F_{ttt}\ne0\).  A cusp is a higher-codimension
organization of folds, not a prerequisite for the complete-topology boundary
conclusion.

This theorem supplies the correct general interpretation of validated
one-, two-, and three-mode allocations.  It does not by itself locate the
crossing, establish genericity, or prove a cusp.

## 6. Spatial-configuration answer

The theory separates geometry from allocation:

1. **Geometry creates clocks.**  A support located where the free process has
   a concentrated exposure near time \(t_j\) creates one clock \(g_j\).
2. **Temporal separation creates feasibility.**  Ordered supports along a
   monotone mean path, with passage-time separation large compared with the
   dynamic positional uncertainty and support width, make the alternating
   derivative rows nearly diagonal.
3. **Contact must not erase a channel.**  The common encounter factor must be
   positive and sufficiently slowly varying on the selected peak boxes.  The
   direct fixed-finite-\(d,m\) slab theorem makes this precise by requiring
   the deterministic relative path to remain inside contact near the target
   times.
4. **Allocation selects the modal class.**  The LP chooses nonnegative
   amplitudes under the exact installed-budget constraint.  A positive margin
   is the certificate; a visually separated set of patches alone is not.
5. **Weak reaction transfers the design.**  The mixed-jet bound preserves the
   certificate below an explicit sufficient budget.
6. **Finite-parameter physics needs independent confirmation.**  At a useful
   positive budget, mesh/alignment/box checks and an unbounded off-lattice
   process must preserve the predeclared event-law features.

For every fixed finite integer \(d\ge2\) and fixed finite \(m\), the existing
narrow-noise slab theorem proves that such separated-clock geometry exists.
The statement remains pointwise in \(d,m\); the supports and small parameters
may depend on both.  No one geometry, uniform-in-dimension threshold, global
mode count, or event-mass floor follows.

```text

  -> exact broad-family B-downarrow-0 design controls

  -> new fixed-control finite-B validation manifest
  -> one no-refit mesh/parity/alignment/box campaign
  -> one powered unbounded off-lattice campaign
```

The positive-budget campaign should compare three fixed controls prospectively
targeting exactly one, exactly two, and exactly three maxima on one declared
finite window.  Those labels become scientific claims only after the

retain the same transport,
contact geometry, initial law, support locations, support widths, installed
budget, time window, and event-mass definitions.  Only the allocation weights
change.  It must freeze:

- the exact binary64 selector, scales, checkpoint times, floor, tie breaks,
  output schema, and failure semantics;
- held-out quadrature plus outward-rounded box-curvature and complement-
  derivative enclosures over the entire declared window;

  enlargements, root/curvature/contrast/mass/survival gates, and the rule for
  excluding boundary-root changes;

## 9. Maximum defensible future claim

If the prospective finite-volume and off-lattice campaigns both pass without
refitting, the reader-facing synthesis can be:

> For every fixed finite dimension and prescribed fixed finite mode count, a
> separated spatial-reactivity design produces that many encounter-reaction
> time modes in a sequential weak-noise/weak-reaction limit.  More generally,
> mode certificates are convex constraints on free-exposure clocks under a
> conserved allocation.  In one broad two-dimensional family, three
> allocations selected before positive-budget validation produce exactly one,
> exactly two, and exactly three event-time maxima on the same declared window
> at the same installed budget.  Deterministic interval-certified calculations
> preserve these finite-window stationary topologies under the frozen mesh,
> parity, alignment, and box challenges.  A separately powered unbounded
> off-lattice process preserves the predeclared positive window contrasts,
> survival probabilities, and event-basin probabilities within the declared
> compatibility envelopes.

The prohibited promotions remain:

- continuum/PDE certification from finite-volume convergence alone;
- a global exact root count beyond the declared time window;
- a cusp, fold manifold, or phase diagram from the failed v6 branch;
- off-lattice validation of derivatives or singularities it does not estimate;
- off-lattice proof of the absence of extra roots or of an exact topology;
- a uniform-in-\(d\) or uniform-in-\(m\) theorem; and
- a positive-budget physical-\(d=3\) headline without a separate complete
  campaign.

This theorem-first route is broader than a single cusp and directly answers
the spatial-configuration question.  Whether it reaches PRR depends on the
prospective positive-budget and independent-method evidence, not on the
exploratory free-exposure success recorded here.
