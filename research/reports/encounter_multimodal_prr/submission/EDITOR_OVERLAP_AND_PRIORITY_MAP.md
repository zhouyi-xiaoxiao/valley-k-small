Article: “Prescribing finite-window reaction-time modes with a static
fixed-budget Doi reactivity field”

Author: Xiaoxiao Zhouyi

## Narrow priority claim

Multimodal first-passage or reaction-time densities are established and are
not claimed as new. Strategically arranged static traps, multiple transport
channels, inverse first-passage and soft-killing constructions, phase-type or
hyper-Erlang waiting-time models, stochastic gating, and kinetic proofreading
all provide adjacent mechanisms for rich timing laws. Static bulk or surface
killing and the redistribution or optimization of spatial reactivity under a
fixed total resource are also established; static reactivity and an L1 budget
are not priority claims.

The present priority claim is the conjunction of four restrictions and
conclusions:

1. the design variable is a lab-frame, time-independent, nonnegative
   centre-coordinate Doi reactivity field with fixed L1 mass;
2. every fixed finite mode count (m) is constructed in every fixed finite
   physical dimension d >= 2, with geometry and scales allowed to depend on
   pairs (d, m);
3. the complete alternating stationary signature is established over the
   whole declared positive-time window, uniformly over a compact family of
   strictly positive slab weights; and
4. root locations, curvature signs, endpoint slopes, and the complement
   margin are transferred from free exposure to a sufficiently small positive
   Doi budget.

The Gaussian-mixture mode bound, Doi killing representation, multimodality
itself, and distribution prescription under time-dependent or hidden-state
controls are prior ingredients or adjacent results. The claimed novelty is
their margin-bearing whole-window combination under the static nonnegative
fixed-resource constraint.

## Scientific scope and limitations

- The geometry and admissible scales may depend on (d) and (m).
- The small-noise/width and small-budget limits are sequential.
- The theorem does not provide a common useful budget, an event-mass floor, a
  support family that switches among arbitrary mode counts, or a
  stationary-point count outside the declared window.
- The deterministic relative trajectory remains inside the contact region and
  the small-noise contact factor tends to one; separated longitudinal exposure
  clocks, rather than a nontrivial contact gate, generate the modal skeleton.
- The higher-dimensional result uses a longitudinal-slab symmetry quotient and
  is a dimension-compatibility result, not a dimension-driven mechanism.
- The normalized slab height scales as the inverse slab width; no independent
  peak-rate cap or minimum feature size is imposed.
- The existential topological threshold, explicit sufficient analytic bound,
  and classifier-dependent operational crossing are distinct quantities.
- The off-lattice numerics are finite-parameter evidence with an operational
  classifier and time-discretization checks, not a numerical continuum proof.

## Related manuscript 1 — Physical Review E, EU13106

“Geometry-controlled folds of first-passage-time bimodality under localized
absorption,” Xiaoxiao Zhouyi, submitted to *Physical Review E*, accession
**EU13106**.

That manuscript studies a single localized killing/delivery gate, fold and cusp
structure of bimodality, marked slow-fast transfer, conductance scaling, and
finite-network or process-level checks. It shares first-passage, localized
absorption, and multimodality ancestry with the PRR manuscript. It does not
prove prescribed exact finite modality for arbitrary fixed finite (m) under a
static nonnegative multi-support L1 resource, nor the PRR whole-window
positive-budget Doi transfer.

## Related manuscript 2 — Journal of Chemical Physics, JCP26-AR-03623

“Spectral diagnostics and local fixed-budget sensitivity of critical points in
finite encounter-reaction models,” Xiaoxiao Zhouyi, submitted to *The Journal
of Chemical Physics*, manuscript **JCP26-AR-03623**.

That manuscript develops finite-state and finite-grid reaction-support
reduction, residue-sign diagnostics, Fréchet-Duhamel sensitivity, and local
fixed-budget responses of critical points. It then studies one fixed
matched-budget spatial redistribution path through a density-fold problem. An
exact fold-transfer theory, a cell-averaged refinement ladder under two
independent transport discretizations, and an off-lattice Brownian campaign
with 20 million walkers per sampled control settle that path's continuum
question negatively: the finite-grid interior fold is a coarse-discretization
artifact that moves to the admissibility edge, and the physical continuum path
has no interior fold. Its Supplemental Material also contains a bounded
three-patch finite-grid trimodality diagnostic and a free-space narrow-patch
generalized-inverse-Gaussian screen for two to four prescribed clocks in
dimensions one through four.

The overlap with PRR therefore includes Doi/encounter coordinates, fixed-budget
spatial redistribution, continuum analysis, off-lattice simulation,
finite-grid multimodality, and prescribed-clock ancestry. The separation is in
the question, control family, and result. JCP analyzes one matched-budget path
for a maximum--minimum fold and proves a negative continuum result for that
path. PRR uses a different OU-slab family and proves existence for arbitrary
fixed finite (m), the exhaustive alternating stationary signature on a whole
window, uniformity over a compact positive-weight family, and topology transfer
to sufficiently small positive Doi budget. PRR's off-lattice phase, threshold,
robustness, (m=5), and (d=3) campaigns are separate finite-parameter tests;
no scientific figure or table is reused from JCP.
