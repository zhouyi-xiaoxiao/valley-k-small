\title{Saddle-node bifurcation of first-passage-time densities induced by a directed shortcut}

\author{Xiaoxiao Zhouyi}
\email{xiaoxiao.zhouyi@bristol.ac.uk}
\author{Luca Giuggioli}
\affiliation{School of Engineering Mathematics and Technology, University of Bristol, Bristol BS8 1TW, United Kingdom}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
First-passage-time (FPT) densities in shortcut-assisted search can be bimodal: a fast ``direct''
arrival and a slow ``indirect'' one. While this bimodality is well documented, the
\emph{morphology} of the density---when a finite-time arrival peak is created or destroyed---has
lacked an exact threshold theory. Here we show that, in a minimal model, the morphology transition
is an observable-level \emph{saddle-node bifurcation} that can be solved exactly. A directed
shortcut to an absorbing target is, after elimination of the target, a rank-one killing defect on a
lazy lattice---in the survival sector the directedness enters only the absorption channel---and the
diffusive limit is Brownian motion on an interval with an interior $\delta$-sink of strength $b$ at
position $\theta$, released at $\xi$. The FPT density is a \emph{signed} spectral mixture
$\Phi(\tau)=\sum_j G_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}$, and its extrema merge at the compact fold criterion
$S_1=S_2=0$, where $S_n(\tau)=\sum_j G_j\mu_j^{n}e^{-\mu_j\tau}$ and $\Phi'=-S_1$, $\Phi''=S_2$
(primes denoting $\tau$-derivatives). For the source-started convention $\xi=\theta$ the density
carries an initial shortcut-capture feature and a finite-time interior peak; numerical continuation
shows that the latter survives on a single connected window $0<b<b_c(\theta)$, annihilated at the
computable boundary $b_c(\theta)$---symmetric about $\theta=\tfrac12$, minimal near
$\theta\simeq0.381$, and diverging as $0.789/\min(\theta,1-\theta)$ at the
endpoints. The fold has the generic saddle-node exponents
$(1/2,3/2)$ with prefactors analytic in the fold data, requires at least three spectral modes, and
persists when the walker is released off (but near) the gate, where both features become genuine
interior peaks. The local fold is structurally stable for reversible (symmetrizable) low-rank
killing, and it is \emph{not} a spectral exceptional point. The classification is validated by exact
finite-lattice residues, direct Monte Carlo, an exact channel decomposition, off-gate starts, and
Brownian-dynamics
simulations with no lattice and no spectral input; it extends to a rank-two cusp and to finite
two-dimensional lattices. The result recasts a known phenomenology as a predictive, exactly
computable bifurcation of a measurable time-domain observable, testable in feedback-controlled
colloidal search and engineered transport networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The first-passage time (FPT) to a target is a central observable of stochastic transport, search and
reaction kinetics~\cite{Redner2001,MetzlerBook2014,Benichou2011,Condamin2007,BenichouVoituriez2014}.
Its full distribution $f(t)$, rather
than the mean first-passage time (MFPT) alone, controls the timing statistics of diffusion-limited
reactions, of intracellular delivery, and of search under
deadlines~\cite{GrebenkovMetzlerOshanin2018}, and it is increasingly accessible
experimentally through single-particle tracking and feedback-controlled colloidal
platforms~\cite{TalFriedman2020,Grebenkov2020,Bressloff2022}.

A recurring and striking feature of $f(t)$ is \emph{multimodality}. When a searcher has access to a
``shortcut''---a relocation channel, a long-range rewired edge, or an active-transport route---the FPT
density can become bimodal: fast trajectories that use the shortcut form an early peak, while slow
trajectories that explore the domain form a later peak, provided the two time scales are well
separated~\cite{GodecMetzler2016}. Random walks on small-world and shortcut-rich networks likewise
exhibit shortcut-induced temporal structure in their first-passage statistics~\cite{Almaas2003}.
Multimodal capture-time densities are further known for interior partially absorbing traps when
several traps are strategically arranged~\cite{Bressloff2022,Grebenkov2020}, and exact spatiotemporal
solutions of confined lattice random walks with defects are by now a mature
program~\cite{Giuggioli2020,MontrollWeiss1965}.

These works analyze direct/indirect first-passage structure and partial-absorption
kinetics~\cite{GodecMetzler2016,GodecMetzler2017,Bressloff2022}---the two-channel (fast-capture vs
slow-diffusion) decomposition is explicit already in Ref.~\cite{GodecMetzler2017}; what has been missing
is an exactly solvable model
whose FPT-density \emph{extrema} undergo a genuine saddle-node fold at a computable shortcut-strength
threshold. That is the gap we fill. The distinction matters: the appearance and disappearance of a
finite-time arrival peak is a qualitative, controllable property of the distribution that is invisible
to the MFPT and to the spectral gap, and is, we argue, a genuine catastrophe of the observable.

Here we supply exactly that classification for a minimal directed shortcut, modeled as a rank-one
killing defect on an absorbing lattice. In the survival sector the directed shortcut is equivalent
to localized killing: the non-reversibility resides entirely in the absorbing channel, not in the
transient propagator (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An \emph{exact solution} (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}): the FPT density is a signed spectral mixture
whose amplitudes we derive in closed form from the defect resolvent, both on the finite lattice
(Montroll determinant, App.~\ref{app:det}) and in the diffusive limit (App.~\ref{app:amp}).
\item A \emph{saddle-node fold criterion and computable phase boundary}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:existence}): the FPT density carries a short-time shortcut-capture feature and a
finite-time interior peak; the birth and death of the latter is a fold governed by the compact local
criterion $S_1=S_2=0$ in signed spectral moments ($\Phi'=-S_1$, $\Phi''=S_2$), with a single
connected window $0<b<b_c(\theta)$ under the source-started convention. The boundary is symmetric
about $\theta=\tfrac12$, minimized near $\theta\simeq0.381$, and obeys the endpoint law $b_c\sim
B^*/\min(\theta,1-\theta)$ with $B^*=0.7890262$ fixed by an explicit half-line transform; a
\emph{minimal-mode theorem} (at least three spectral modes) and the generic $(1/2,3/2)$ saddle-node
scaling, with prefactors analytic in the fold data, complete the local classification.
\item An \emph{independent validation chain} (Sec.~\ref{sec:valid}; the exact finite-$N$ checks
appear in Secs.~\ref{sec:modelC}, \ref{sec:existB} and Apps.~\ref{app:amp}, \ref{app:numerics}):
exact finite-$N$ residues
against the continuum master curve, the fold threshold itself converging as
$b_{c,N}-b_c\propto N^{-2.08}$, a direct Monte-Carlo, an exact channel decomposition (the early
feature is pure shortcut capture; the late peak is fed by both channels), off-gate starts for which
both features are genuine interior peaks, and a Brownian-dynamics realization containing no lattice
and no spectral input.
\item \emph{Extensions and scope} (Secs.~\ref{sec:univ}, \ref{sec:physical}): structural stability
for reversible (symmetrizable) low-rank killing, a rank-two extension with a triple-peak region and
a nondegenerate organizing cusp point, finite-2D evidence with explicit limitations, the
demonstration that $b_c$ is \emph{not} a spectral exceptional point, and a quantitative
measurability estimate for feedback-controlled colloidal search.
\end{enumerate}

We are careful to separate what is new from what is not. Shortcut-induced bimodality and interior-trap
multimodality are known~\cite{GodecMetzler2016,Bressloff2022,Grebenkov2020}; the lattice
Green-function/defect method is that of Refs.~\cite{Giuggioli2020,MontrollWeiss1965}; and
Ref.~\cite{Mattos2012} concerns bimodality of a splitting probability $P(\omega)$, not of $f(t)$. Our
contribution is the exactly solvable \emph{bifurcation-theoretic} account: the fold criterion, the
predictive threshold $b_c(\theta)$, and the structural stability of the mechanism.

\section{Model and exact solution}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Lazy ring with a directed shortcut}\label{sec:modelA}
Consider a lazy random walk on a ring of $N$ sites [Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}(a)]: at each step the
walker stays with probability $1-q$ and hops to each neighbor with probability $q/2$. Site $v=0$ is an
absorbing target. A directed shortcut at a source site $u$ diverts probability $\lambda=\beta(1-q)$
from the $u$ self-loop onto the directed edge $u\to v$; upon taking it the walker is absorbed. Removing
the absorbed state leaves a transient (sub-stochastic) transition matrix on sites $1,\dots,N-1$: a
Dirichlet path with a rank-one diagonal killing defect of strength $\lambda=\beta(1-q)$ at $u$
(admissibly $0\le\lambda\le1-q$, as it is diverted from the self-loop probability),
\begin{equation}
P_\lambda=P_0-\lambda\,\ket{u}\!\bra{u},
\label{eq:Qb}
\end{equation}
so probability leaks irreversibly from $u$ into the target; the rescaled continuum sink strength
$b=\lambda N/q$ is introduced below. Although the shortcut is directed, the transient block remains
symmetric---the directedness enters only through the (one-way) absorption vector, not the survival
dynamics---so its spectrum is real. The eliminated survival operator is therefore self-adjoint: the
process is open (sub-Markovian, probability-losing), but the threshold is a time-domain morphology
change, not a non-Hermitian spectral (exceptional-point) effect (Sec.~\ref{sec:physC}).

\subsection{Exact generating function and Montroll determinant}\label{sec:modelB}
The FPT generating function is rational with an exact Montroll-type determinant. Applying the
matrix-determinant lemma to the rank-one defect \eqref{eq:Qb} and evaluating the resulting
path-resolvent element in closed form (App.~\ref{app:det}) gives, with $U_m$ the Chebyshev
polynomials of the second kind, $y=1+(z^{-1}-1)/q$ the spectral variable, and $a=q/\lambda$ the
dimensionless inverse shortcut strength, the characteristic determinant for a source at $u$,
\begin{equation}
D_u(y)=a\,U_{N-1}(y)+2\,U_{u-1}(y)\,U_{N-u-1}(y).
\label{eq:Du}
\end{equation}
The FPT probability is then a finite sum of geometric modes,
\begin{equation}
F(t)=\sum_j B_j\, s_j^{\,t-1},\qquad s_j=1-q+q\,y_j,
\label{eq:Ft}
\end{equation}
where $y_j$ are the roots $D_u(y_j)=0$ and the residues are $B_j=q\,\mathcal N_{r,u}(y_j)/D_u'(y_j)$
with $r$ the start site and $\mathcal N_{r,u}$ the piecewise numerator of App.~\ref{app:det}. In the
antipodal case $u=N/2$ the identity $U_{N-1}=2\,U_{N/2-1}T_{N/2}$ factorizes \eqref{eq:Du} as
$D_u=2\,U_{N/2-1}(y)\,[a\,T_{N/2}(y)+U_{N/2-1}(y)]$ (with $T_m$ the Chebyshev polynomial of the first
kind): the bracket carries the sink-coupled spectrum, while the factor $U_{N/2-1}$ carries the
antisymmetric (sink-decoupled, node) modes, recovering the reflection-symmetric problem as a special
case of the general rank-one theory. Equivalently, in terms of the defected matrix $P_\lambda$ of
\eqref{eq:Qb}, the total absorption current has the compact form
\begin{equation}
\tilde F_r(z)=z\Big[\lambda\bra{u}+\tfrac q2\big(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1}\big)\Big](I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},
\label{eq:genfun}
\end{equation}
whose two bracketed terms are the shortcut-capture and diffusive-absorption channels resolved in
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}. In the diffusive scaling the finite mass maps to a continuum density,
$F(t)\simeq(q/N^2)\,\Phi(qt/N^2)$ with $\tau=qt/N^2$.

\subsection{Diffusive limit}\label{sec:modelC}
Writing $\theta=u/N$ for the fractional shortcut position, $b=\beta(1-q)N/q$ for the rescaled sink
strength, $\tau=qt/N^2$ for diffusive time, and writing the transient eigenvalues as
$s=1-q+q\cos(2w/N)$---which defines the lattice root variable $w$, with continuum wavenumber $k=2w$
and eigenvalue $\mu=k^2/2=2w^2$---the walk converges to
Brownian motion on $[0,1]$ with absorbing ends and an interior $\delta$-sink [Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}(b)],
\begin{equation}
\partial_\tau p=\tfrac12\,p_{xx}-b\,\delta(x-\theta)\,p,\qquad p(0)=p(1)=0 .
\label{eq:continuum}
\end{equation}
The finite-$b$ continuum limit is the \emph{weak-per-visit} (partially absorbing) scaling
$\lambda=\beta(1-q)=bq/N=O(N^{-1})$: a fixed per-visit shortcut probability would instead approach
the absorbing-cut limit $b\to\infty$. The spectrum of \eqref{eq:continuum} is fixed by the
transcendental determinant
\begin{equation}
D_\theta(k;b)=k\sin k+2b\,\sin(k\theta)\sin\!\big(k(1-\theta)\big)=0,
\label{eq:Dtheta}
\end{equation}
with $k=2w$ and eigenvalues $\mu_j=k_j^2/2=2w_j^2$; for $\theta=\tfrac12$ this collapses to
$\tan w=-2w/b$. This interval $\delta$-sink (point-interaction) eigenvalue condition is the standard
point-sink spectrum, with a long lineage in the localized-sink and imperfect-reactivity
literature~\cite{Albeverio2005,WilemskiFixman1973,SzaboSchulten1980,Grebenkov2020,Bressloff2022};
our exact-solution novelty is not this spectrum
but the finite-$N$ directed-shortcut Montroll determinant $D_u$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:Du}] and the bifurcation
classification developed below. The FPT density from a start at $\xi=r/N$ is the signed spectral mixture
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\xi,\theta}(\tau;b)=\sum_j G_j\, e^{-\mu_j\tau},
\label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
whose amplitudes we derive from the defect resolvent (App.~\ref{app:amp}) as
\begin{equation}
G_{\xi,\theta}=2w^2\,\frac{\phi_{k,\theta}(\xi)\,I(k,\theta)}{J(k,\theta)},\qquad k=2w,
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\phi_{k,\theta}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\sin\!\big(k(1-\theta)\big)\sin(kx), & x\le\theta,\\[2pt]
\sin(k\theta)\sin\!\big(k(1-x)\big), & x\ge\theta,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and $I=\int_0^1\phi\,dx$, $J=\int_0^1\phi^2\,dx$ have the closed forms of App.~\ref{app:amp}. Modes with
$\sin(n\pi\theta)=0$ do not couple to the sink and contribute the free Dirichlet term
$G_n^{\mathrm{node}}=n\pi[1-(-1)^n]\sin(n\pi\xi)$ (even $n$ vanish). We have verified \eqref{eq:G}, the
crucial normalization identity $J=\sin(k)\,D_k/(4b)$, and the full master curve (affected plus node
modes) against exact finite-$N$ residues ($N=1200$) to a relative error $\lesssim6\times10^{-5}$ (per-mode
$\sim2\times10^{-4}$) with measured $O(N^{-2})$ convergence (fitted exponent $2.00$;
App.~\ref{app:amp}, App.~\ref{app:numerics}). Table~\ref{tab:notation} collects the notation.

\subsection{Signed spectral moments and the fold criterion}\label{sec:existA}
Define the signed spectral moments
\begin{equation}
S_n(\tau;b)=\sum_j G_j\,\mu_j^{\,n}\,e^{-\mu_j\tau},
\label{eq:Sn}
\end{equation}
so that $\Phi'=-S_1$ and $\Phi''=S_2$ [differentiate \eqref{eq:Phi}]. We are precise about what folds.
Under the source-started convention $\xi=\theta$ the density carries a short-time shortcut-capture
feature---a boundary/initial-time contribution whose weight vanishes as $b\to0$---together with \emph{interior}
finite-time extrema; an interior extremum is a zero of $S_1$. The finite-time interior peak
forms a valley--peak pair that is born or annihilated when the two interior extrema merge---a
\emph{saddle-node} fold of the density's critical points,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\,S_1(\tau_c,b_c)=0,\qquad S_2(\tau_c,b_c)=0\,},
\label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
with nondegeneracy $S_3(\tau_c,b_c)\neq0$ and $\partial_b S_1(\tau_c,b_c)\neq0$. No positivity is
assumed---the $G_j$ are genuinely signed---so \eqref{eq:fold} is the correct fold criterion for a
signed exponential mixture: the coincidence of a root and a critical point of the generalized (signed)
exponential sum $S_1$. This places the classification in a classical frame: the FPT density of any
finite absorbing chain is a (discrete- or continuous-time) phase-type law~\cite{Neuts1981}, whose
density is a matrix-exponential---in general signed---mixture, and \eqref{eq:fold} locates the
parameter values at which two extrema of
such a mixture merge. The amplitudes $G_j$, and hence the boundary, depend on the start, so
$b_c=b_c(\theta;\xi)$; unless stated otherwise we report $\xi=\theta$. The local fold \eqref{eq:fold} is
exact; the global statement is \emph{numerical}: for $\xi=\theta$, numerical continuation of the
interior extrema gives a single connected window $0<b<b_c(\theta)$ in which the finite-time interior
peak exists (the \emph{peak window}), with no re-entrant interval
over the scanned range (App.~\ref{app:numerics}). The window reaches down to arbitrarily small $b$:
released at the gate, the density diverges as $\Phi\simeq b/\sqrt{2\pi\tau}$ for $\tau\to0$ (killing at
rate $b$ against the returning heat kernel), while the inherited Dirichlet interior peak survives
continuously from $b=0$, so a valley separates the two features for every sufficiently small $b>0$. We
use theorem language only for the local fold and the exact finite-$N$ algebra.

\subsection{The existence boundary $b_c(\theta)$}\label{sec:existB}
Eliminating $\tau_c$ between the two equations in \eqref{eq:fold} yields a scalar boundary $b_c(\theta)$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a)]. It is symmetric, $b_c(\theta)=b_c(1-\theta)$, has a minimum at
$\theta\simeq0.381$ ($b_c\simeq2.165$), and obeys the endpoint law
\begin{equation}
b_c(\theta)\sim\frac{B^*}{d},\qquad \tau_c\sim c_*\,d^2,\qquad d=\min(\theta,1-\theta),
\label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
where the constant is fixed by the explicit closed-form transform of the half-line boundary-layer
problem [App.~\ref{app:existence}, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fhl}--\eqref{eq:Bstar}]: $B^*=0.7890262$ and
$c_*=0.1579221$; interval bisection on the fold independently confirms $B^*$ to six digits
($b_c\theta=0.789026$ across $\theta\in[0.20,0.27]$, no reference table) and $c_*$ to three
($\tau_c/d^2\approx0.158$); an elementary closed form
for $B^*$ remains open. In the antipodal case [Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b)]
\begin{equation}
b_c(\tfrac12)=3.07643,\qquad \tau_c=0.038363,
\label{eq:antip}
\end{equation}
independently verified to $10^{-6}$, with the numerically fitted near-antipodal expansion
$b_c(\tfrac12+\epsilon)\approx3.0764-133\,\epsilon^2$ (App.~\ref{app:existence}); the finite-lattice
threshold converges to this value as $b_{c,N}-b_c\propto N^{-2.08}$ over $N=100$--$1200$
(App.~\ref{app:numerics}). The two limits are physically transparent: as
$b\to0$ the capture feature's weight vanishes and the density reverts to the single-peaked Dirichlet FPT,
while as $b\to\infty$ the $\delta$-sink becomes an absorbing cut and, for $\xi=\theta$, the density
collapses toward $\tau\to0$---so the finite-time interior peak exists only in the finite window
$0<b<b_c$.

\subsection{Normal-form scaling}\label{sec:existC}
Near the fold the two merging extrema split with the generic saddle-node exponents
\begin{equation}
\tau_+-\tau_-\propto(b_c-b)^{1/2},\qquad
\Phi(\tau_+)-\Phi(\tau_-)\propto(b_c-b)^{3/2},
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
which we confirm numerically [Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(c)], the fitted exponents being $0.498(1)$ and
$1.493(2)$ (log--log fits over $b_c-b\in[1.4\times10^{-3},3.6\times10^{-2}]$, seven continuation
points; App.~\ref{app:numerics}). The prefactors are \emph{analytic} in the fold data [App.~\ref{app:existence},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefac}]: in the antipodal case $\tau_+-\tau_-\simeq0.0247518\,(b_c-b)^{1/2}$ and
prominence $\simeq0.357444\,(b_c-b)^{3/2}$, matching directly measured extrema splittings to better
than $2\times10^{-4}$ relative. The exponents identify the transition as a fold rather than a smooth
spectral crossover, and---being the generic saddle-node exponents---constitute a system-independent
experimental signature (Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}).

\subsection{Minimal mode count}\label{sec:existD}
A two-mode fold is impossible, even with signs. Writing $A_j=G_j\mu_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}$, $S_1=0$ forces
$A_2=-A_1$, whence $S_2=(\mu_1-\mu_2)A_1=0$ gives $A_1=A_2=0$, a contradiction. Thus \emph{at least
three} modes are required, and the fold ratio $A_1:A_2:A_3=(\mu_3-\mu_2):(\mu_1-\mu_3):(\mu_2-\mu_1)$
forces \emph{alternating signs} among the three participating contributions. Both statements also
follow from Laguerre's generalization of Descartes' rule: an $n$-term real exponential sum has at
most as many real zeros, counted with multiplicity, as its coefficient sequence has sign
changes~\cite{PolyaSzego1976}---so the double zero of $S_1$ at a fold requires $n\ge3$ terms, with
alternating signs among the participating triplet. The full fold is realized
by a many-mode signed cancellation; at the antipode this coincides with an alternating affected-mode
pattern ($G_1>0,G_2<0,G_3>0,\dots$), while away from the antipode the modal signs need not be strictly
alternating in eigenvalue order (App.~\ref{app:amp}). Retaining $K$ affected modes in the antipodal
case gives $b_c^{(2)}$ (none), $b_c^{(3)}=3.046$, $b_c^{(4)}$ (none), $b_c^{(5)}=3.0764$---the even-$K$
gaps reflecting the odd-mode parity of the alternating structure---converging to \eqref{eq:antip}.

\section{Independent validation}\label{sec:valid}
This section assembles the evidence that the fold of Sec.~\ref{sec:existence} is a property of the
physical process rather than of the spectral representation. The exact finite-$N$ checks---residues
against the continuum master curve (Sec.~\ref{sec:modelC}, App.~\ref{app:amp}) and the threshold
convergence $b_{c,N}\to b_c$ (App.~\ref{app:numerics})---are complemented by four independent probes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}): a direct Monte-Carlo, an exact channel resolution, non-antipodal
robustness, and start-position dependence (under which both features become genuine interior
peaks), followed by a Brownian-dynamics realization with no lattice and no spectral input
(Sec.~\ref{sec:univF}).

\subsection{Direct simulation, channel resolution, and start dependence}\label{sec:univE}
Four checks confirm that the fold is a physical mechanism rather than an artifact of the spectral
representation. \emph{(i) Direct simulation.} A direct Monte-Carlo of the lazy ring, using no spectral
input, reproduces the exact FPT density across four decades in $\tau$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(a)], with the
simulated splitting probability $\pi_{\rm sc}=0.56466\pm0.00078$ ($4\times10^5$ walkers) agreeing
with the exact $0.56522$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:pisc}]. \emph{(ii) Channel resolution.} The absorption current splits exactly into a shortcut
entry ($\lambda$ at $u$) and diffusive entries ($q/2$ at the neighbors of $v$),
$\Phi=f_{\rm sc}+f_{\rm diff}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(b)]: $f_{\rm sc}$ is monotone and carries the
short-time mass, while the late finite-time bump receives comparable contributions from both channels
(diffusive return and delayed capture). The fold thus annihilates the second arrival-time \emph{scale},
not a single pure channel. \emph{(iii) Non-antipodal robustness.} The interior fold occurs off the
reflection axis [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(c)]: at $\theta=0.38$ ($b_c=2.165$) the interior peak is present
at $b=1.5$, marginal at $b=2.16$ just below threshold, and absent at $b=2.8$. \emph{(iv) Start dependence.} Releasing the walker away from the gate ($\xi\neq\theta$)
removes the boundary/initial capture feature: both features become genuine \emph{interior} peaks that
annihilate through the fold [Fig.~\ref{fig:start}(a)], and the threshold $b_c(\theta;\xi)$ varies
smoothly and symmetrically about $\xi=\theta$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:start}(b)], so the saddle-node is not an
artifact of releasing exactly at the gate. (Far from the gate the morphology changes and the simple
peak window is lost.)

\subsection{A direct stochastic realization}\label{sec:univF}
Finally, the continuum fold is realized directly by stochastic simulation. A
continuum Brownian-dynamics simulation of the delivery-gate protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:physB})---Brownian
motion on $[0,1]$ with absorbing ends and killing at rate $b/\ell$ inside a narrow gate of width $\ell$
at $\theta$, integrated by Euler--Maruyama with \emph{no lattice and no spectral input}---reproduces
both the exact $\delta$-sink density and the fold: released off the gate ($\xi=0.44$), it shows two
interior peaks below $b_c$ and one above [Fig.~\ref{fig:bd}], matching the exact continuum $\Phi(\tau)$
to histogram precision. Because the simulation contains none of the exact machinery (a finite gate
that only coarse-grains to the $\delta$-sink, no lattice, no spectral decomposition), this confirms
that the fold is a property of the physical process rather than of the solution method---in
particular, not a lattice artifact. The realization is also converged: halving the gate width and
refining the Euler--Maruyama step leaves the fold morphology unchanged and the late-peak location
stable within the histogram resolution (App.~\ref{app:numerics}).

\section{Extensions, structural stability, and scope}\label{sec:univ}
Having validated the one-dimensional fold, we now delimit how far the mechanism extends---and where
its present evidence stops.

\subsection{Model-independence}\label{sec:univA}
The fold \eqref{eq:fold} is not special to the ring. For any absorbing continuous-time generator
$Q_0$ (or, analogously, any sub-stochastic transition matrix), a
rank-$m$ killing defect $Q_B=Q_0-UBU^\top$ gives, by the Woodbury identity,
\begin{equation}
\det(sI-Q_B)=\det(sI-Q_0)\,\det\!\big[I_m+BW(s)\big],
\label{eq:woodbury}
\end{equation}
with $W_{ab}(s)=\bra{u_a}(sI-Q_0)^{-1}\ket{u_b}$. For \emph{reversible} (symmetrizable) transient
dynamics with diagonal low-rank killing---as for the symmetric ring block here---$Q_B$ has a real
spectrum and the FPT density is again a real signed spectral mixture, whose second-peak birth/death is
the fold $S_1=S_2=0$. (For non-reversible directed transient dynamics the determinant identity still
holds, but the real modal expansion, and hence the fold classification, requires additional
hypotheses.) The Woodbury identity supplies only the finite-rank secular determinant; the universality
is the \emph{structural stability} of a generic extrema-pair fold (a codimension-one catastrophe of the
scalar density~\cite{Arnold1992}), not a consequence of the identity alone. Accordingly the double-peak
region is an \emph{open} set (structurally stable by the implicit-function theorem), and the honest
statement is not ``every shortcut creates a peak''---false in general---but ``whenever a
shortcut-induced peak is born or annihilated generically, it does so through a fold of the signed
spectral mixture, independent of the underlying geometry.''

\subsection{Multiple shortcuts and the cusp}\label{sec:univB}
For two shortcuts at $\theta_1=\alpha<\theta_2=\eta$ (positions; $\beta$ is reserved for the shortcut
rate) with strengths $b_1,b_2$, the secular determinant is (App.~\ref{app:cusp})
\begin{align}
D_{12}(k)={}&k\sin k+2b_1\sin(k\alpha)\sin\!\big(k(1-\alpha)\big)\notag\\
&+2b_2\sin(k\eta)\sin\!\big(k(1-\eta)\big)\notag\\
&+\tfrac{4b_1b_2}{k}\sin(k\alpha)\sin\!\big(k(\eta-\alpha)\big)\sin\!\big(k(1-\eta)\big),
\label{eq:D12}
\end{align}
the last term being a genuine two-defect interaction that vanishes as the sources coalesce (giving the
additive rule $b=b_1+b_2$). Two shortcuts can produce a \emph{third} FPT peak: at
$(\alpha,\eta,b_1,b_2)=(0.38,0.48,1.35,0.14)$, $\xi=0.463$, the exact ring ($N=1500$) shows three
peaks at $\tau\simeq3.0\times10^{-4},5.3\times10^{-3},6.1\times10^{-2}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:ext}(a)],
corresponding to three well-separated arrival-time scales (nominally near-shortcut capture,
far-shortcut capture, and diffusive arrival; as for the single-shortcut late peak of
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}, the later scales are fed by more than one channel).
A codimension-two \emph{cusp} point $S_1=S_2=S_3=0$---the generic organizing center for a triple-peak
region~\cite{Arnold1992}---exists in the same family: solving the three conditions for the three
unknowns $(\eta,b_2,\tau_c)$ at fixed $(\alpha,b_1)=(0.38,1.35)$ locates it at
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\alpha,\eta,b_1,b_2,\tau_c)=(&0.38,\,0.489580512568,\,1.35,\\
&1.08389,\,0.0012366),
\end{split}
\label{eq:cusp}
\end{equation}
with $S_4\neq0$ and unfolding Jacobian $\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)=-6.9\times10^7\neq0$, a
genuine codimension-two cusp (App.~\ref{app:cusp}). The triple-peak example and the cusp point are
\emph{distinct control values in the same rank-two family}---the example demonstrates a three-peak
region, while the cusp calculation locates a nearby codimension-two organizing point in the
$(b_1,b_2)$ unfolding at fixed geometry (they differ in $b_2$, $0.14$ vs $1.08$, not merely in $\eta$).
Thus $m$ shortcuts turn the single fold into fold hypersurfaces in $b$-space, with open regions of two
and three peaks and a nondegenerate cusp point as the organizing center~\cite{Arnold1992}; the secular
determinants and residues remain
finite-rank and explicitly computable, though the catastrophe locations are found numerically.

\subsection{Two dimensions}\label{sec:univC}
On an $L\times L$ torus with an absorbing target and a directed shortcut, the same algebra holds
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:woodbury} with the 2D lattice resolvent]. Specifically, we take the lazy walk (stay
$1-q$, hop $q/4$ to each of the four neighbors, $q=2/3$) on the $31\times31$ torus with absorbing
target $v=(0,0)$, shortcut source $u=(15,15)$ (antipodal up to lattice rounding, same rate convention
$\lambda=\beta(1-q)$), and start $r=(15,13)$, two sites off the gate---the 2D test therefore realizes
the off-gate convention in which both features are genuine interior peaks (cf.\
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}). Numerically, a single diffusive
peak at $\beta=0$ acquires a shortcut-induced capture peak; the two coexist over a window, and the
second (late) interior maximum then annihilates by extremum merging near $\beta_c^{2D}\approx0.68$,
its prominence falling to zero between $\beta=0.68$ and $0.70$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:ext}(b,c)]---the same
fold morphology as in one dimension. A size sweep locates the threshold at
$\beta_c^{2D}=0.669,0.684,0.686,0.682$ for $L=21,31,41,51$ (App.~\ref{app:numerics})---nearly
size-independent over accessible sizes. One caveat is essential:
a single-site sink is marginal in 2D (its self-Green function is logarithmically divergent), so the
\emph{bare} threshold is expected to drift as $\beta_c^{2D}(L)\sim1/\log L$ in the large-$L$ limit;
no such drift is yet visible at these sizes, the continuum point-sink
limit therefore requires logarithmic renormalization, and we leave a size-independent 2D scaling test
(the analogue of \eqref{eq:scaling}) to future work. We thus present the 2D result as finite-lattice
evidence that the mechanism is not one-dimensional, not as a proof of 2D universality.

\subsection{Robustness}\label{sec:univD}
The fold persists under any perturbation that preserves the nondegeneracy $S_3\neq0$,
$\partial_b S_1\neq0$: to first order $\delta b_c=-(\partial_\varepsilon S_1/\partial_b S_1)\,
\delta\varepsilon$ for a perturbation $\varepsilon$. Concretely it survives weak hop-rate disorder
(which can even help, by lifting accidental dark modes), small shifts of the source or start
(Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}), and the hop probability $q$, which is already scaled out of the reduced
threshold and
reduced fold time (the raw step time obeys $t_c\sim N^2\tau_c/q$). It is destroyed by source
coalescence in the rank-two family (rank drop) or by moving the source
adjacent to the absorbing boundary. Recall that at $\xi=\theta$ the shortcut-capture feature is a
boundary/initial-time contribution, so the interior fold concerns the finite-time interior
peak; moving the start off the gate turns the capture feature into an interior peak in its own right
(Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}).


\section{Physical realization and non-Hermitian character}\label{sec:physical}

\subsection{Embedding}\label{sec:physA}
Physically the shortcut is a localized target-delivery channel. From $p'(t)=Q_0p-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}p$
the absorption current splits as $f(t)=f_\partial(t)+b\,p_u(t)$, where $f_\partial$ is the
boundary-absorption flux of the \emph{defected} density $p$ (not the $b=0$ current): while the
searcher is at $u$ it is irreversibly delivered to the target at rate $b$. In the survival sector this is a local killing term
$-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$, not a transport edge, and a finite gate of physical width $\ell$ with local
trigger rate $\kappa$ coarse-grains to a $\delta$-sink. Restoring dimensions with domain length $L$
and diffusion constant $D$ in the standard convention $\langle X^2\rangle=2Dt$ [the reduced equation
\eqref{eq:continuum} has unit variance rate, so $\tau=2Dt/L^2$, i.e.\ $t=L^2\tau/(2D)$], the
dimensionless sink strength is
\begin{equation}
b\;=\;\frac{\kappa\,\ell\,L}{2D}\qquad(\ell\ll L),
\label{eq:nondim}
\end{equation}
so a laboratory quadruple $(\kappa,\ell,L,D)$ maps to a definite $b$; the smallest threshold
$b_c(0.38)\simeq2.165$ corresponds to a trigger rate $\kappa\simeq4.3\,D/(\ell L)$ at a gate placed at
$x\simeq0.38\,L$. The sharp physical statement is that a localized delivery channel supports an
\emph{additional arrival-time scale}---a finite-time interior peak in the full FPT density---over
the predictable finite window $0<b<b_c$ of gate strengths, and that this scale is annihilated at
$b_c$ through a measurable saddle-node fold: scanning the delivery rate should reveal two
arrival-time scales for $b<b_c$, with the late scale merging with its valley and disappearing at
$b_c$.

\subsection{Realizations}\label{sec:physB}
We rank candidate realizations by cleanliness. \emph{(i) Feedback-controlled colloidal or
active-particle search} is the strongest: a narrow gate at $x=\theta L$ (optical tweezers, a
microfluidic pulse, or active-particle feedback) that, upon entry, occasionally delivers the particle
to the target and ends the trial; the controlled parameter is the local trigger rate
$\kappa$---equivalently the dimensionless gate strength $b=\kappa\ell L/(2D)$ (in a frame-based
implementation, $\kappa$ is the per-frame trigger probability divided by the frame time). Releasing
the particle at the gate
($\xi=\theta$, the convention under which $b_c(\theta)$ is quoted), recording first-arrival times over
many trials, and scanning $b$ at fixed $\theta$---best near $\theta\simeq0.38$, where $b_c$ is smallest
and the effect largest---should reveal the second peak for $b<b_c(\theta)$ and its annihilation at
$b_c$, with the fold collapse \eqref{eq:scaling} as the sharp signature. A simulated experiment
(Brownian dynamics with counting noise) confirms measurability: the robust second-peak observable
$R=\Phi(\tau_p)/\Phi(\tau_v)$ (second-peak over valley density) decreases through unity across the
fold; $\sim\!4\times10^4$ delivery-and-terminate trials resolve $b_c$ to roughly $\pm0.25$, and
$\sim\!2\times10^5$ to $\pm0.1$
(App.~\ref{app:numerics}); the same holographic-tweezer/feedback stack that realizes controlled
first-passage and resetting experiments~\cite{TalFriedman2020} can implement the delivery gate. We
caution that standard resetting-to-origin~\cite{TalFriedman2020,PalReuveni2017} is \emph{not} this problem: it
reinjects probability into the survival domain, whereas the correct protocol is
delivery-and-terminate---a gated detection that separates delivery times from bare first-passage
times~\cite{Kumar2023}. \emph{(ii) Engineered or small-world transport
networks} with a one-way express edge to an absorbing node realize $-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$ directly
(microfluidic maze with a one-way valve; programmable packet-routing graph; electrical random-walk
analog with a tunable shunt to ground; lossy photonic link); two such edges provide the cleanest test
of the rank-two triple peak and cusp. \emph{(iii) Intracellular motor capture} to a reaction site
realizes it in a coarse-grained, ideally reconstituted, in-vitro setting. In each case the caveat is
that only a genuinely one-way, terminating channel maps to the rank-one killing defect.

\subsection{Non-Hermitian character}\label{sec:physC}
We stress what the non-Hermitian character is and is not. $P_\lambda$ is a passive open-Markov loss
defect, analogous to an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian with a localized absorber (imaginary
potential)~\cite{Ashida2020}; it is \emph{not} PT-symmetric, since there is no balanced gain and loss.
The survival-sector operator is itself self-adjoint in the one-defect model (the directedness enters
only the absorption vector), so ``non-Hermitian'' here denotes open-system probability loss, not a
spectral mechanism. Crucially, $b_c$ is \emph{not} an exceptional point: an EP requires eigenvalue
coalescence with a defective (Jordan) block, whereas we find numerically ($N=200$) that the eigenvalues
remain real, simple, and smooth through $b_c$, with consecutive gaps bounded away from zero, and the
fold arises from cancellation among at least three signed residues, not from spectral degeneracy. The
$(b_c-b)^{1/2}$ scaling in \eqref{eq:scaling} is a saddle-node of the scalar $\Phi(\tau;b)$, not EP
eigenvalue splitting---the same exponent for a different object. In the reversible diffusive limit the
$\delta$-sink operator is self-adjoint (Sturm--Liouville), so no spectral EP exists at all.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
We do not claim the first observation of shortcut-induced bimodality; the robust result of this
paper is the observable-level fold classification with its computable threshold.
We have shown that shortcut-induced FPT bimodality, long known phenomenologically, is the observable
signature of an exactly solvable saddle-node fold: a rank-one directed defect adds a short-time
capture channel, while the pre-existing finite-time interior peak survives, for the source-started
convention, only on a single numerically continued interval $0<b<b_c(\theta;\xi)$, annihilated at
$b_c$ through a generic $(1/2,3/2)$ fold, with a minimal count
of three modes, a rank-two extension with a cusp-organized triple-peak region, and the same fold
morphology on finite two-dimensional lattices. A direct Monte-Carlo, an exact channel decomposition,
and the start-position dependence confirm the mechanism physically. The single most defensible novelty
is the bifurcation-theoretic classification with a predictive, computable threshold, distinct from the
known bimodality and from the underlying defect-resolvent method. The generic fold exponents and the
two-shortcut triple peak are directly measurable, on feedback-controlled colloidal search and
engineered transport networks.

Beyond the results verified here, the analysis leaves a small set of open questions. A fully rigorous
$N\to\infty$ statement requires, in addition to the standard convergence of fixed-mode residues
(App.~\ref{app:amp}), uniform $C^1$ convergence of the moment sums $S_{1,2,3}$ near the fold; we state
the precise hypotheses and support them by the observed $O(N^{-2})$ convergence, but do not prove
uniform $C^1$ control of the tail. The endpoint constant is fixed by an explicit boundary-layer
integral [Eq.~\eqref{eq:phihl}], $B^*=0.7890262$, and confirmed independently to six digits, though
neither it nor the antipodal $b_c$ is known in elementary closed form; and the large-$L$ renormalized
scaling of $b_c$ in two dimensions is open. None of these affects the exact finite-system results.

\section{Exact finite-\texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} determinant and residues}\label{app:det}
We derive Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Du}--\eqref{eq:Ft}. Let $P_0$ be the $(N-1)\times(N-1)$ transient
(sub-stochastic) transition matrix of the lazy ring with $v=0$ deleted; on the interior sites it is a
symmetric tridiagonal path with diagonal $1-q$ and off-diagonal $q/2$. The directed shortcut diverts
$\lambda=\beta(1-q)$ from the self-loop at $u$ into the absorbing target, i.e.\ it subtracts $\lambda$
from the $(u,u)$ entry: $P_\lambda=P_0-\lambda\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:Qb}].
The first-passage generating function to $v$ from a start $r$, $\tilde F_r(z)=\sum_{t\ge1}F(t)z^t$, is a
ratio of resolvent elements of $P_\lambda$; by the matrix-determinant lemma its poles are the zeros of
\begin{equation}
\det\!\big(I-zP_\lambda\big)=\det\!\big(I-zP_0\big)\,\big[1+z\lambda\,G^{0}_{uu}(z)\big],
\label{eq:mdl}
\end{equation}
with $G^0(z)=(I-zP_0)^{-1}$. Introducing the spectral variable $y=1+(z^{-1}-1)/q$ maps the lazy path
onto the standard second-difference operator, $I-zP_0=zq\,(yI-\tfrac12A_{\rm path})$, with
$\det(yI-\tfrac12A_{\rm path})=2^{-(N-1)}U_{N-1}(y)$; by the cofactor structure of the tridiagonal
inverse $(yI-\tfrac12A_{\rm path})^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
G^0_{uu}(z)=\frac{1}{zq}\,\frac{2\,U_{u-1}(y)\,U_{N-u-1}(y)}{U_{N-1}(y)} .
\label{eq:Guu}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:Guu} into \eqref{eq:mdl} and clearing $U_{N-1}$ gives the characteristic
determinant $D_u(y)=a\,U_{N-1}(y)+2\,U_{u-1}(y)U_{N-u-1}(y)$ of \eqref{eq:Du}, with $a=q/\lambda$.

The residues follow from the full defect-resolvent column. Writing $A_m:=U_{m-1}(y)$ (with the
convention $A_0=U_{-1}=0$, so $A_N=U_{N-1}$), the Dirichlet-path Green function has the
tridiagonal-inverse entries $\mathcal G_{rs}=2A_{\min(r,s)}A_{N-\max(r,s)}/A_N$, and
Sherman--Morrison gives
\begin{equation}
(I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r}=\frac{1}{zq}\Big[\mathcal G\ket{r}
-\mathcal G\ket{u}\,\frac{\mathcal G_{ur}}{a+\mathcal G_{uu}}\Big].
\label{eq:smcol}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:smcol} into \eqref{eq:genfun} and collecting over the common denominator
$a+\mathcal G_{uu}=D_u/A_N$ yields, for $r\le u$,
\begin{align}
\tilde F_r(z)={}&\frac{1}{D_u}\Big[a(A_r+A_{N-r})+2A_rA_{N-u}\notag\\
&\quad+\frac{2A_{N-u}}{A_N}\big(A_{N-r}A_u-A_rA_{N-u}\big)\Big],
\label{eq:Fraw}
\end{align}
and the Chebyshev product identity $A_{N-r}A_u-A_{N-u}A_r=A_N\,A_{u-r}$ [set $y=\cos\varphi$, so
$A_m=\sin(m\varphi)/\sin\varphi$, and reduce by product-to-sum] collapses the last term of
\eqref{eq:Fraw} to $2A_{N-u}A_{u-r}$: $\tilde F_r(z)=\mathcal N_{r,u}(y)/D_u(y)$ with the piecewise
numerator
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N_{r,u}=a\,[A_r+A_{N-r}]+2A_{N-u}[A_r+A_{u-r}],\qquad r\le u,
\label{eq:num}
\end{equation}
and its mirror ($r\leftrightarrow N-r$, $u\leftrightarrow N-u$) for $r\ge u$. Expanding
$\tilde F_r=\sum_j B_j z/(1-s_jz)$ about the pole $z_j=1/s_j$ and using $dy/dz=-1/(qz^2)$ with
$z_js_j=1$ converts the residue of $\mathcal N_{r,u}/D_u$ into $B_j=q\,\mathcal
N_{r,u}(y_j)/D_u'(y_j)$, i.e.\ the geometric sum \eqref{eq:Ft} with $s_j=1-q+q\,y_j$. Setting $z=1$
(i.e.\ $y=1$, where $A_m(1)=m$) in the shortcut component of \eqref{eq:genfun}---which
\eqref{eq:smcol} reduces to $\mathcal G_{ur}/(a+\mathcal G_{uu})$---gives the eventual channel
(splitting) probability, the fraction of trajectories absorbed through the shortcut,
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\rm sc}(r)=\frac{2\min(r,u)\,[N-\max(r,u)]}{aN+2u(N-u)},
\label{eq:pisc}
\end{equation}
which for the antipodal case $u=N/2$ reduces to $\pi_{\rm sc}=\rho/(a+N/2)$ with $\rho=\min(r,N-r)$
the start's short-arc distance to the absorbing target. We
emphasize that $\pi_{\rm sc}$ is the eventual splitting probability, \emph{not} the mass under the
first peak; conflating the two is a common error we deliberately avoid. The two absorption channels of
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE} have the explicit generating functions
\begin{align}
\tilde F^{\rm sc}_r(z)&=z\lambda\bra{u}(I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},\notag\\
\tilde F^{\rm diff}_r(z)&=z\tfrac q2\big(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1}\big)(I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},
\label{eq:genchan}
\end{align}
with $\tilde F_r=\tilde F^{\rm sc}_r+\tilde F^{\rm diff}_r$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:genfun}]. Their $t$-domain
inverses $f_{\rm sc}(t)=\lambda\bra{u}P_\lambda^{\,t-1}\ket{r}$ and $f_{\rm diff}(t)=\tfrac
q2(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1})P_\lambda^{\,t-1}\ket{r}$ are the channel densities of Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(b).

\section{Continuum amplitude from the defect resolvent}\label{app:amp}
We derive Eqs.~\eqref{eq:G}--\eqref{eq:phi} from the resolvent of $H_b=-\tfrac12\partial_x^2
+b\,\delta_\theta$ on $(0,1)$ with Dirichlet ends, continued to $z=-k^2/2$ (so $\mu=k^2/2=2w^2$).

\emph{1. Free resolvent.} With $\varkappa=\sqrt{2z}$ (a spectral variable; distinct from the gate
trigger rate $\kappa$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:physA}),
$R_0(x,y;z)=2\sinh(\varkappa x_<)\sinh(\varkappa(1-x_>))/(\varkappa\sinh \varkappa)$; continued to $\varkappa=ik$,
\begin{equation}
r_0(x,y;k)=\frac{2\sin(kx_<)\sin\!\big(k(1-x_>)\big)}{k\sin k}.
\label{eq:r0}
\end{equation}
\emph{2. Defect resolvent (Sherman--Morrison, rank-one $\delta$-sink).}
\begin{equation}
r_b=r_0-\frac{b\,r_0(x,\theta)r_0(\theta,y)}{1+b\,r_0(\theta,\theta)}
=r_0-\frac{4b\,\phi(x)\phi(y)}{k\sin k\;D_\theta},
\label{eq:rb}
\end{equation}
with $\phi=\phi_{k,\theta}$ of \eqref{eq:phi}; the pole condition $1+b\,r_0(\theta,\theta)=0$ is exactly
$D_\theta(k;b)=0$ of \eqref{eq:Dtheta}. \emph{3. Eigenfunction.} The residue eigenfunction is
$\psi_j=\phi_{k_j,\theta}$: it is continuous at $\theta$, satisfies the Dirichlet conditions, and obeys
the $\delta$-sink jump $\psi'(\theta^+)-\psi'(\theta^-)=2b\,\psi(\theta)$. Indeed
$\phi'(\theta^+)-\phi'(\theta^-)=-k[\sin(k\theta)\cos(k(1-\theta))+\cos(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))]
=-k\sin k$, so the jump condition reads $-k\sin k=2b\sin(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))$, which is
precisely $D_\theta=0$ (numerically $1.8\times10^{-14}$). \emph{4. Residue $=$ FPT amplitude.} The
survival transform is $\bar Q_\xi(z)=\int_0^1 r_b(y,\xi;z)\,dy$ and $\bar f=1-z\bar Q$, so
$G_j=\operatorname{Res}_{z=-\mu_j}\bar f=\mu_j\operatorname{Res}_{z=-\mu_j}\bar Q$. Near a root,
$D_\theta(k)\simeq D_k(k_j)\,(k-k_j)$ with $k-k_j=-(z+\mu_j)/k_j$ (from $z=-k^2/2$), so the pole part
of \eqref{eq:rb} is $4b\,\phi(x)\phi(y)/[\sin(k_j)\,D_k(k_j)\,(z+\mu_j)]$, and integrating over the
first argument gives the normalization-independent amplitude
\begin{equation}
G_j=\frac{4b\,\mu_j\,\phi_{k_j,\theta}(\xi)\,I}{\sin(k_j)\,D_k(k_j)}
=\mu_j\,\frac{\phi_{k_j,\theta}(\xi)\,I}{J}
=\mu_j\,\psi_j(\xi)\,\frac{\int_0^1\psi_j}{\int_0^1\psi_j^2},
\label{eq:Gderiv}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ Eq.~\eqref{eq:G} with $\mu_j=2w_j^2$; the second equality is the normalization identity
\eqref{eq:JD} below, which is thus not a mere consistency check but the bridge between the resolvent
residue and the eigenfunction-expansion form. \emph{5. Closed forms.} With $A=\sin k\theta$,
$B=\sin k(1-\theta)$,
\begin{align}
I(k,\theta)&=\tfrac1k\big[B\,(1-\cos k\theta)+A\,(1-\cos k(1-\theta))\big],\label{eq:I}\\
J(k,\theta)&=\tfrac12\big[\theta B^2+(1-\theta)A^2\big]-\frac{AB\sin k}{2k},\label{eq:J}
\end{align}
matching direct quadrature to $\sim10^{-9}$ (here and below, $A$, $B$, $C$, $E$ are local
abbreviations of this appendix, distinct from the residues $B_j$, the Woodbury matrix $B$, and the
boundary-layer strength $B=b\,d$). \emph{6. Normalization identity.} At a root of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta},
\begin{equation}
J(k_j,\theta)=\frac{\sin(k_j)\,D_k(k_j;b)}{4b},
\label{eq:JD}
\end{equation}
with $D_k=\sin k+k\cos k+2b[\theta\cos(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))+(1-\theta)\sin(k\theta)
\cos(k(1-\theta))]$; this exact algebraic identity (proven below; verified to $2.9\times10^{-15}$)
ties the residue norm to the determinant derivative, and $D_k(k_j)\neq0$ confirms each affected mode
as nondegenerate. The proof is elementary: abbreviate $A=\sin k\theta$, $B=\sin k(1-\theta)$,
$C=\cos k\theta$, $E=\cos k(1-\theta)$, so that $\sin k=AE+CB$ and $\cos k=CE-AB$, and use the root
condition $k\sin k=-2bAB$ in the form $1/(4b)=-AB/(2k\sin k)$. Then
\begin{align}
\frac{\sin k\,D_k}{4b}={}&-\frac{AB}{2k}\,(\sin k+k\cos k)\notag\\
&+\frac{\sin k}{2}\big[\theta CB+(1-\theta)AE\big],\notag
\end{align}
and expanding with $\sin k=AE+CB$, $\cos k=CE-AB$ cancels the mixed $ACBE$ terms, leaving
$\tfrac12[\theta B^2(A^2{+}C^2)+(1-\theta)A^2(B^2{+}E^2)]=\tfrac12[\theta B^2+(1-\theta)A^2]$; hence
$\sin k\,D_k/(4b)=-AB\sin k/(2k)+\tfrac12[\theta B^2+(1-\theta)A^2]=J$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:J}.
\emph{7. Antipodal reduction.} For $\theta=\tfrac12$ ($A=B=\sin w$, $k=2w$) the root condition reads
$\tan w=-2w/b$, whence $\cos w=-b\sin w/(2w)$ and $\sin^2w=4w^2/(4w^2+b^2)$; then
$I=\sin w\,(1-\cos w)/w$ and
$J=\tfrac12\sin^2w\,[1-\sin(2w)/(2w)]=\tfrac12\sin^4w\,[1+b(b+2)/(4w^2)]$, and \eqref{eq:Gderiv}
collapses to
$G_{\xi,1/2}=4w(1-\cos w)\sin(2w\xi^*)/[\sin^2 w\,(1+b(b+2)/4w^2)]$ with $\xi^*=\min(\xi,1-\xi)$. \emph{8. Node modes.} When $\sin(n\pi\theta)=0$
the mode decouples from the sink and is the free Dirichlet mode $\sin(n\pi x)$, with amplitude
$G_n^{\mathrm{node}}=n\pi[1-(-1)^n]\sin(n\pi\xi)$ (even $n$ vanish, and all node modes vanish at
$\xi=\theta$). The reconstructed master curve (affected plus node) matches exact finite-$N$ residues
($N=1200$) to
$\lesssim6\times10^{-5}$ (worst case in the early-$\tau$ tail; per-mode $\sim2\times10^{-4}$) with
measured $O(N^{-2})$ convergence (exponent $2.00$, median over cases, $\tau$, and $N$-pairs).

\emph{The $N\to\infty$ limit.} Convergence of fixed-mode residues $G_{N,j}\to G_j$ follows from
norm-resolvent convergence of the killed evolution operator under standard
hypotheses~\cite{Kato1995,Albeverio2005}: (A) $\xi_N\to\xi$,
$\theta_N\to\theta$; (B) bulk convergence $H_N\to-\tfrac12\partial_x^2$; (C) $\delta$-sink form scaling
$V_{N,\theta}\sim bN$; (D) a symmetrizable transient matrix; (E) simple isolated poles. Passing the
fold \eqref{eq:fold} to the limit additionally requires (F) uniform $C^1$ convergence of $S_{1,2,3}$
(including $\partial_b$) on a neighborhood $\tau\in[\tau_c/2,3\tau_c/2]$ of the fold---not down to
$\tau=0$, where the heat-kernel short-time singularity lives---for which a sufficient practical bound is
$\mu_{N,j}\ge cj^2$, $|G_{N,j}|\le Cj^p$ uniformly. Under (F), fold persistence is a consequence of the
implicit-function theorem: the Jacobian $\det DF_c=+S_{1,b}S_3$ is nonzero by nondegeneracy, giving a
unique nearby finite-$N$ fold with $b_{c,N}-b_c=O(\varepsilon_N)$, consistent with the directly
measured threshold convergence $b_{c,N}-b_c\propto N^{-2.08}$ (App.~\ref{app:numerics}). We state (F)
as a hypothesis, supported by the numerics, rather than a theorem.

\section{Fold criterion: proofs and asymptotics}\label{app:existence}
\emph{Fold criterion.} From $\Phi=\sum_j G_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}$, $\Phi'=-\sum_j G_j\mu_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}
=-S_1$ and $\Phi''=\sum_j G_j\mu_j^2 e^{-\mu_j\tau}=S_2$, so an interior extremum is $S_1=0$ and two
extrema merge at $S_1=S_2=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fold}. The parameter derivative of a root of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta} is $\partial_b k_j=-D_b/D_k$ with $D_b=2\sin(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))$ and $D_k$ as
in \eqref{eq:JD}, which propagates into $\partial_b S_1$ and gives the nondegeneracy condition needed
for a structurally stable fold. In practice $\partial_bS_1$ is evaluated by high-precision central
differences (mpmath, 40 digits), with $\partial_bk_j=-D_b/D_k$ serving as an independent consistency
propagation.

\emph{Normal-form prefactors.} Expanding $S_1$ about the fold and using $S_2(\tau_c,b_c)=0$,
\begin{equation}
S_1(\tau,b)\simeq\tfrac12 S_3\,(\tau-\tau_c)^2-S_{1,b}\,(b_c-b),
\label{eq:nf}
\end{equation}
with $S_{1,b}=\partial_bS_1>0$ and $S_3>0$ here, so the two extrema sit at
$\tau_\pm-\tau_c=\pm\delta$ with $\delta=[2S_{1,b}(b_c-b)/S_3]^{1/2}$, and the prominence is
$\Delta\Phi=\Phi(\tau_+)-\Phi(\tau_-)=-\int_{\tau_-}^{\tau_+}S_1\,d\tau=\tfrac23S_3\delta^3$. The
scaling amplitudes in \eqref{eq:scaling} are therefore \emph{analytic} in the fold data,
\begin{align}
\tau_+-\tau_-&=2\Big[\frac{2S_{1,b}}{S_3}\Big]^{1/2}\!(b_c-b)^{1/2},\notag\\
\Delta\Phi&=\frac{4\sqrt2}{3}\,\frac{S_{1,b}^{3/2}}{S_3^{1/2}}\,(b_c-b)^{3/2},
\label{eq:prefac}
\end{align}
giving at the antipodal fold $0.0247518\,(b_c-b)^{1/2}$ and $0.357444\,(b_c-b)^{3/2}$, which match
directly measured extrema splittings to better than $2\times10^{-4}$ relative for
$b_c-b\le10^{-3}$.

\emph{Minimal-mode theorem.} As in Sec.~\ref{sec:existD}, $S_1=S_2=0$ has no two-mode solution with
$A_j\neq0$; the minimal count is three, with the alternating-sign ratio
$A_1:A_2:A_3=(\mu_3-\mu_2):(\mu_1-\mu_3):(\mu_2-\mu_1)$. This retires an earlier (incorrect) two-mode
heuristic and shows the fold is intrinsically a many-mode cancellation.

\emph{Endpoint law.} As $d=\min(\theta,1-\theta)\to0$ the gate approaches the absorbing boundary.
Rescaling $y=x/d$, $\sigma=\tau/d^2$, $B=b\,d$ (the boundary-layer strength; not the residues $B_j$ of
App.~\ref{app:det}) and letting $d\to0$ turns \eqref{eq:continuum} into a half-line boundary-layer
problem: diffusion on $(0,\infty)$ with an absorbing end at $0$ and a $\delta$-sink of strength $B$ at
$y=1$, started at $y=1$ (the far boundary recedes as $1/d$). This problem is exactly solvable. With
$\varkappa=\sqrt{2s}$ the half-line Dirichlet resolvent is
$r_0^{\rm hl}(y,y';s)=2\sinh(\varkappa y_<)\,e^{-\varkappa y_>}/\varkappa$, and Sherman--Morrison [as
in \eqref{eq:rb}] gives the arrival-time transform (boundary flux plus sink capture) in closed form,
\begin{equation}
\hat f(s;B)=\frac{e^{-\varkappa}+(B/\varkappa)\big(1-e^{-2\varkappa}\big)}
{1+(B/\varkappa)\big(1-e^{-2\varkappa}\big)},\qquad \varkappa=\sqrt{2s},
\label{eq:fhl}
\end{equation}
which obeys $\hat f(0)=1$ (certain absorption) and reduces at $B=0$ to the L\'evy transform
$e^{-\varkappa}$. The half-line operator has purely continuous spectrum, so the discrete criterion
$S_1=S_2=0$ is replaced by its density form $\Phi_{\rm hl}'=\Phi_{\rm hl}''=0$: Bromwich inversion
along the branch cut $s=-v^2/2$ ($\varkappa=iv$) gives the real integral representation
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\rm hl}(\sigma;B)=\frac1\pi\!\int_0^\infty\!\frac{v\,e^{-\sigma v^2/2}\,(1+g)\sin v}
{1+2g\cos v+g^2}\,dv,
\label{eq:phihl}
\end{equation}
with $g(v)=2B\sin(v)/v$ [the denominator equals $(g+\cos v)^2+\sin^2v$, strictly positive on the
support]. Imposing the fold on \eqref{eq:phihl} selects
\begin{equation}
B^*=0.7890262,\qquad \sigma^*=c_*=0.1579221,
\label{eq:Bstar}
\end{equation}
whence $b_c(\theta)\sim B^*/d$ and $\tau_c\sim c_*\,d^2$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint}]: the endpoint
constant is fixed by an explicit one-dimensional integral, though an elementary closed form remains
open. Independently, resolving $b_c(\theta)$ on the \emph{interval} by bisection on the fold
count---with the root search extended to the high-$k$ fold mode ($w\sim1.78/d$), which a naive
locator misses---gives $b_c(\theta)\,\theta=0.789026$ stably for $\theta\in[0.20,0.27]$, agreeing
with \eqref{eq:Bstar} to six digits without any reference table (below $\theta\simeq0.15$ the fold
mode is too high-$k$ for clean interval resolution). Near the
antipode the boundary is smooth and even in $\epsilon$; a quadratic fit to the numerically continued
threshold over $|\epsilon|\le0.04$ gives
$b_c(\tfrac12+\epsilon)\approx3.0764-133\,\epsilon^2$ (fitted coefficient $-133.1$, with the quartic
term controlled), with the antipodal values \eqref{eq:antip} from
$S_1=S_2=0$ directly ($S_3=2.8286\times10^5$, $\partial_bS_1=21.662$, nondegenerate). The truncated
thresholds ($K=2$ none, $K=3\to3.046$, $K=4$ none, $K=5\to3.076$) confirm rapid, parity-structured
convergence.

\section{Two-shortcut determinant and cusp}\label{app:cusp}
For two sources the secular condition is $\det[I_2+B\,W(k)]=0$ with $W_{ab}=r_0(k;\theta_a,\theta_b)$
the interval resolvent \eqref{eq:r0}; expanding the $2\times2$ determinant gives $D_{12}(k)$ of
\eqref{eq:D12}, whose cross term $\propto b_1b_2$ is the genuine two-defect interaction (it $\to0$ as
$\alpha\to\eta$, recovering a single defect of strength $b_1+b_2$); the exact ring ($N=800$)
eigenvalues satisfy $D_{12}(k_j)=0$ to $\lesssim1.3\times10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:verif}). The rank-two residue
eigenfunction follows from the Woodbury pole structure: at a root of $\det[I_2+BW]$ the resolvent
residue factorizes on the null vector
$c^{(j)}\in\ker[I_2+W(k_j)B]$, giving $\psi_j(x)=-\sum_a b_a c_a^{(j)}r_0(k_j;x,\theta_a)$,
and $G_j=\mu_j\psi_j(\xi)\int\psi_j/\int\psi_j^2$ exactly as in the rank-one case, with
$\int\psi_j=-\sum_a b_a c_a I_{\theta_a}$ and $\int\psi_j^2=\sum_{ab}b_a c_a b_b c_b\,C_{ab}$. The
constituents are $I_\theta$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:I}] and the overlap
$C_{ab}=\int_0^1 r_0(x,\theta_a)\,r_0(x,\theta_b)\,dx$, which evaluates in closed form by splitting
the integral at $\theta_a\le\theta_b$ into three segments of sine products (the explicit three-line
expression is displayed in the accompanying script \texttt{dpma\_cusp\_verify.py}). The cusp solves the
three conditions
$S_1=S_2=S_3=0$ for the three unknowns $(\eta,b_2,\tau_c)$ at fixed $(\alpha,b_1)=(0.38,1.35)$---hence
the many digits quoted in \eqref{eq:cusp}---with $S_4\neq0$ and a nonzero unfolding Jacobian
$\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)$. At the located point \eqref{eq:cusp} an independent
reimplementation gives normalized residuals $\tau S_1/\Phi\approx7\times10^{-12}$,
$\tau^2S_2/\Phi\approx5\times10^{-11}$, $\tau^3S_3/\Phi\approx4\times10^{-10}$,
$\tau^4S_4/\Phi\approx-1.67$, and $\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)\approx-6.9\times10^7$,
confirming a nondegenerate codimension-two cusp.

\section{Numerical methods and reproducibility}\label{app:numerics}
Exact finite-$N$ FPT poles are computed from the symmetric transient block via \texttt{numpy}
\texttt{eigvalsh}, with amplitudes from the closed-form residues; high-precision cross-checks use \texttt{mpmath}. Continuum spectra are the roots of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta}/\eqref{eq:D12}, and amplitudes use \eqref{eq:G} and App.~\ref{app:cusp}. All scripts
are deterministic. Table~\ref{tab:verif} collects the verification tolerances quoted throughout.
The figures are generated by \texttt{dpma\_prr\_figures.py} (fold results and extensions) and
\texttt{dpma\_schematic.py}
(model); the existence boundary by \texttt{dpma\_saddle\_node\_bc\_theta.py}; the amplitude/master
curve by \texttt{dpma\_general\_u\_master\_curve.py}; the fold scaling by
\texttt{dpma\_saddle\_node\_certification.py}; the cusp by \texttt{dpma\_cusp\_verify.py}; the rank-two
determinant and triple peak by \texttt{dpma\_multishortcut.py}; the 2D test by
\texttt{dpma\_2d\_finite\_lattice.py}; the direct-simulation, channel-resolution, and non-antipodal panels
by \texttt{dpma\_channel\_mc.py}; the start-dependence panels by \texttt{dpma\_start\_dependence.py}; the Brownian-dynamics
realization by \texttt{dpma\_brownian\_fold.py}; the endpoint-constant resolution by
\texttt{dpma\_endpoint\_law.py}; the half-line transform inversion and fold
[Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fhl}--\eqref{eq:Bstar}] by \texttt{dpma\_halfline\_bstar.py}; the normal-form
prefactors and the near-antipodal quadratic coefficient by \texttt{dpma\_normal\_form.py}; the
window-connectivity scan by \texttt{dpma\_window\_scan.py}; the 2D size sweep by
\texttt{dpma\_2d\_Lsweep.py}; the finite-$N$ threshold convergence by
\texttt{dpma\_bcN\_convergence.py}; the Brownian-dynamics convergence sweeps by
\texttt{dpma\_bd\_convergence.py}; and the
measurability estimate by \texttt{dpma\_measurement.py}. All randomized computations use fixed seeds
recorded in the scripts.
The 1D boundary $b_c(\theta)$ is obtained by bisection on the valley--peak-pair count over
$\theta\in[0.2,0.5]$, $b\in[0.5,20]$, retaining the affected roots with $w\le40$ (i.e.\ $k\le80$).
Connectivity of the window is verified separately by a dense-$b$ scan [$\Delta b=0.02$, seven values
$\theta\in\{0.20,0.25,\dots,0.50\}$, spectrum deepened to $w\le120$ so the mode sum is converged
down to the small fold times of
off-center $\theta$]: exactly one transition per $\theta$---two interior extrema for all $0<b<b_c$,
none above---with no re-entrance (\texttt{dpma\_window\_scan.py}); the $b\to0$ end is closed
analytically by the short-time argument of Sec.~\ref{sec:existA}, and the tabulated boundary agrees
with an independent reimplementation to $\sim10^{-6}$ (Table~\ref{tab:verif}). The normal-form
exponents are log--log fits over $b_c-b\in[1.4\times10^{-3},3.6\times10^{-2}]$ (seven continuation
points), giving $0.498(1)$ and $1.493(2)$ (OLS standard errors). The 2D fold is computed at
$L=31$ (target $(0,0)$, source $(15,15)$, start $(15,13)$, horizon $T=6000$ steps) by extremum
merging (the second interior maximum's prominence $\to0$ near $\beta\approx0.68$),
not an ad-hoc valley heuristic; peak detection separates capture from late arrivals at $t=120$ and
uses a relative height threshold $10^{-3}\max F$, with the fold location insensitive to both over
wide ranges. A size sweep with the same criterion gives
$\beta_c^{2D}=0.669,0.684,0.686,0.682$ at $L=21,31,41,51$ (horizon scaled as $L^2$;
\texttt{dpma\_2d\_Lsweep.py}).

\emph{Threshold and discretization convergence.} Bisection on the exact ring gives the
finite-lattice fold threshold converging monotonically from above to the continuum value,
$b_{c,N}(\tfrac12)-b_c=4.7\times10^{-3},\dots,2.6\times10^{-5}$ over $N=100$--$1200$, with fitted
$|b_{c,N}-b_c|\propto N^{-2.08}$. The Brownian-dynamics realization is likewise converged: halving
the gate width ($\ell=0.04,0.02,0.01$) and halving/doubling the Euler--Maruyama step
($3\times10^{-5}$ to $7.5\times10^{-6}$) leaves the late-peak location within two histogram bins of
the exact $\tau_p$ in every cell, while the ratio observable agrees with the exact $\delta$-sink
value at the $1$--$3.5\%$ level (within $\sim2.6$ standard errors; a small first-order killing bias
shrinks with the step), so the fold morphology of Fig.~\ref{fig:bd} is not a discretization
artifact. The Monte-Carlo uses $4\times10^5$ vectorized walkers with a fixed
seed, logarithmic $\tau$-binning, and shortcut-vs-diffusive absorption events recorded per walker; the
quoted $\pi_{\rm sc}$ uncertainty is the binomial standard error, and the exact $\pi_{\rm sc}$ is
evaluated from the closed form \eqref{eq:pisc}. The histogram of Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(a) uses $59$
logarithmic bins with a median occupancy of $\sim4\times10^{3}$ events; occupancy falls to $O(1)$
only in the extreme late-$\tau$ tail, where the visible Poisson scatter remains consistent with the
exact curve.

\emph{Measurability.} The simulated experiment behind Sec.~\ref{sec:physB} draws $N_{\rm t}=4000$
delivery-and-terminate trials per run from the continuum gate protocol of Fig.~\ref{fig:bd}
($\ell=0.02$, $\theta=\tfrac12$, $\xi=0.44$, Euler--Maruyama step $1.5\times10^{-5}$) and evaluates
the ratio observable $R=\hat\Phi(\tau_p)/\hat\Phi(\tau_v)$ from histogram densities in fixed
$\pm20\%$ windows around the exact below-threshold peak and valley locations ($\tau_p=0.0462$,
$\tau_v=0.0253$); the exact $R(b)$ decreases monotonically through unity across the fold, and the
simulated estimates track it within counting noise. Ten independent runs
give ${\rm s.d.}(R)\simeq0.061$ at $N_{\rm t}=4000$; the exact $R(b)$ table gives the local
sensitivity $|dR/db|\simeq0.09$ per unit $b$ near the fold, so with
${\rm s.d.}(R)\propto N_{\rm t}^{-1/2}$, $N_{\rm t}\simeq4\times10^4$ trials resolve $b_c$ to
roughly $\pm0.25$ and $N_{\rm t}\simeq2\times10^5$ to $\pm0.1$ (\texttt{dpma\_measurement.py}).
