\title{Saddle-node bifurcation of first-passage-time densities induced by a directed shortcut}

\author{Xiaoxiao Zhouyi}
\email{xiaoxiao.zhouyi@bristol.ac.uk}
\author{Luca Giuggioli}
\affiliation{School of Engineering Mathematics and Technology, University of Bristol, Bristol BS8 1TW, United Kingdom}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
First-passage-time (FPT) densities in shortcut-assisted search can be
bimodal: a fast ``direct'' arrival and a slow ``indirect'' one. We show that this bimodality is the
observable-level signature of a \emph{saddle-node bifurcation} that can be solved exactly. Modeling a
directed shortcut to an absorbing target as a rank-one killing defect on a lazy lattice---whose
diffusive limit is Brownian motion on an interval with an interior $\delta$-sink of strength $b$ at
fractional position $\theta$---we find that the FPT density $\Phi$ carries a short-time
shortcut-capture mode together with a finite-time diffusive-return peak, and that this second,
interior peak is created and annihilated through a fold of a \emph{signed} spectral mixture governed by
the compact criterion $S_1=S_2=0$, where $S_n(\tau)=\sum_j G_j\mu_j^{n}e^{-\mu_j\tau}$ and
$\Phi'=-S_1$, $\Phi''=S_2$. Numerical continuation gives a single connected two-mode window
$0<b<b_c(\theta;\xi)$ (source-started, $\xi=\theta$), with a computable boundary $b_c(\theta)$ that is
symmetric about $\theta=\tfrac12$, minimal near $\theta\simeq0.381$, and diverges as
$b_c\sim0.789/\min(\theta,1-\theta)$ near the endpoints; the fold obeys the generic saddle-node scaling
$\tau_+-\tau_-\propto(b_c-b)^{1/2}$, $\Delta\Phi\propto(b_c-b)^{3/2}$, requires at least three modes, and
persists when the walker is released away from the gate, where both features become genuine interior
peaks. The mechanism is structurally stable rather than lattice-specific: it follows from a low-rank
killing defect, it admits a rank-two extension in which a cusp organizes a triple-peak region, and
finite two-dimensional lattice simulations show the same fold morphology, and an independent continuum
Brownian-dynamics simulation---no lattice, no spectral input---reproduces both the exact density and the
fold; a direct Monte-Carlo and an exact channel decomposition identify the early feature as shortcut
capture and the late peak as diffusive return. The threshold $b_c$ is a time-domain morphology fold, \emph{not} a spectral
exceptional point. The result recasts a known phenomenology as a predictive, exactly solvable
bifurcation with a measurable threshold and generic scaling, testable in feedback-controlled colloidal
search and engineered transport networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
The first-passage time (FPT) to a target is a central observable of stochastic transport, search and
reaction kinetics~\cite{Redner2001,MetzlerBook2014,Benichou2011}. Its full distribution $f(t)$, rather
than the mean first-passage time (MFPT) alone, controls the timing statistics of diffusion-limited
reactions, of intracellular delivery, and of search under deadlines, and it is increasingly accessible
experimentally through single-particle tracking and feedback-controlled colloidal
platforms~\cite{Grebenkov2020,Bressloff2022}.

A recurring and striking feature of $f(t)$ is \emph{multimodality}. When a searcher has access to a
``shortcut''---a relocation channel, a long-range rewired edge, or an active-transport route---the FPT
density can become bimodal: fast trajectories that use the shortcut form an early peak, while slow
trajectories that explore the domain form a later peak, provided the two time scales are well
separated~\cite{GodecMetzler2016}. Random walks on small-world and shortcut-rich networks likewise
exhibit shortcut-induced temporal structure in their first-passage statistics~\cite{Almaas2003}.
Multimodal capture-time densities are further known for interior partially absorbing traps when
several traps are strategically arranged~\cite{Bressloff2022,Grebenkov2020}, and exact spatiotemporal
solutions of confined lattice random walks with defects are by now a mature
program~\cite{Giuggioli2020,MontrollWeiss1965}.

These works analyze direct/indirect first-passage structure and partial-absorption
kinetics~\cite{GodecMetzler2016,GodecMetzler2017,Bressloff2022}---the two-channel (fast-capture vs
slow-diffusion) decomposition is explicit already in Ref.~\cite{GodecMetzler2017}; what has been missing
is an exactly solvable model
whose FPT-density \emph{extrema} undergo a certified saddle-node fold at a computable shortcut-strength
threshold. That is the gap we fill. The distinction matters: the appearance and disappearance of a
finite-time arrival peak is a qualitative, controllable property of the distribution that is invisible
to the MFPT and to the spectral gap, and is, we argue, a genuine catastrophe of the observable.

Here we supply exactly that classification for a minimal directed shortcut, modeled as a rank-one
killing defect on an absorbing lattice. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An \emph{exact solution} (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}): the FPT density is a signed spectral mixture
whose amplitudes we derive in closed form from the defect resolvent, both on the finite lattice
(Montroll determinant, App.~\ref{app:det}) and in the diffusive limit (App.~\ref{app:amp}).
\item A \emph{saddle-node fold criterion} (Sec.~\ref{sec:existence}): the FPT density carries a
short-time shortcut-capture mode and a finite-time diffusive-return peak; the birth and death of the
latter is a fold governed by the compact local criterion $S_1=S_2=0$ in signed spectral moments, with
$\Phi'=-S_1$, $\Phi''=S_2$. For the source-started convention ($\xi=\theta$), numerical continuation
gives a single connected two-mode window $0<b<b_c(\theta;\xi)$.
\item A \emph{computable phase boundary} $b_c(\theta)$: symmetric about $\theta=\tfrac12$, minimized
near $\theta\simeq0.381$, with the endpoint law $b_c\sim0.789/\min(\theta,1-\theta)$; a
\emph{minimal-mode theorem} (at least three modes are required); and the generic saddle-node scaling
$\tau_+-\tau_-\propto(b_c-b)^{1/2}$, $\Delta\Phi\propto(b_c-b)^{3/2}$.
\item A \emph{structural-stability/universality} argument (Sec.~\ref{sec:univ}): for reversible
(symmetrizable) low-rank killing the fold is model-independent, admits a rank-two extension in which a
cusp organizes a triple-peak region, reproduces on finite two-dimensional lattices, and is realized in
an independent continuum Brownian-dynamics simulation (no lattice, no spectral input).
\item A \emph{physical embedding} (Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}): a direct Monte-Carlo and an exact channel
decomposition identify the early feature as shortcut capture and the late peak as diffusive return; the
fold persists for starts away from the gate; and the threshold $b_c$ is a time-domain morphology fold,
\emph{not} a spectral exceptional point.
\end{enumerate}

We are careful to separate what is new from what is not. Shortcut-induced bimodality and interior-trap
multimodality are known~\cite{GodecMetzler2016,Bressloff2022,Grebenkov2020}; the lattice
Green-function/defect method is Giuggioli's~\cite{Giuggioli2020,MontrollWeiss1965}; and
Ref.~\cite{Mattos2012} concerns bimodality of a splitting probability $P(\omega)$, not of $f(t)$. Our
contribution is the exactly solvable \emph{bifurcation-theoretic} account: the fold criterion, the
predictive threshold $b_c(\theta)$, and the universality of the mechanism.

\section{Model and exact solution}\label{sec:model}

$v=0$ and a directed shortcut $u\to v$ of rate $\lambda=\beta(1-q)$; deleting $v$ leaves a rank-one
killing defect at $u$. (b) Diffusive limit: Brownian motion on $[0,1]$ with absorbing ends and an

\subsection{Lazy ring with a directed shortcut}\label{sec:modelA}
Consider a lazy random walk on a ring of $N$ sites [Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}(a)]: at each step the
walker stays with probability $1-q$ and hops to each neighbor with probability $q/2$. Site $v=0$ is an
absorbing target. A directed shortcut at a source site $u$ diverts probability $\lambda=\beta(1-q)$
from the $u$ self-loop onto the directed edge $u\to v$; upon taking it the walker is absorbed. Removing
the absorbed state leaves a transient (sub-stochastic) generator on sites $1,\dots,N-1$ that is a
Dirichlet path with a rank-one diagonal killing defect of strength $\lambda=\beta(1-q)$ at $u$
(admissibly $0\le\lambda\le1-q$, as it is diverted from the self-loop probability),
\begin{equation}
Q_\lambda=Q_0-\lambda\,\ket{u}\!\bra{u},
\label{eq:Qb}
\end{equation}
so probability leaks irreversibly from $u$ into the target; the rescaled continuum sink strength
$b=\lambda N/q$ is introduced below. Although the shortcut is directed, the transient block remains
symmetric---the directedness enters only through the (one-way) absorption vector, not the survival
dynamics---so its spectrum is real. The eliminated survival operator is therefore self-adjoint: the
process is open (sub-Markovian, probability-losing), but the threshold is a time-domain morphology
change, not a non-Hermitian spectral (exceptional-point) effect (Sec.~\ref{sec:physC}).

\subsection{Exact generating function and Montroll determinant}\label{sec:modelB}
The FPT generating function is rational with an exact Montroll-type determinant. Applying the
matrix-determinant lemma to the rank-one defect \eqref{eq:Qb} and evaluating the resulting
path-resolvent element in closed form (App.~\ref{app:det}) gives, with $U_m$ the Chebyshev
polynomials of the second kind, $y=1+(z^{-1}-1)/q$ the spectral variable, and $a=q/\lambda$ the
dimensionless inverse shortcut strength, the characteristic determinant for a source at $u$,
\begin{equation}
D_u(y)=a\,U_{N-1}(y)+2\,U_{u-1}(y)\,U_{N-u-1}(y).
\label{eq:Du}
\end{equation}
The FPT probability is then a finite sum of geometric modes,
\begin{equation}
F(t)=\sum_j B_j\, s_j^{\,t-1},\qquad s_j=1-q+q\,y_j,
\label{eq:Ft}
\end{equation}
where $y_j$ are the roots $D_u(y_j)=0$ and the residues are $B_j=q\,\mathcal N_{r,u}(y_j)/D_u'(y_j)$
with $r$ the start site and $\mathcal N_{r,u}$ the piecewise numerator of App.~\ref{app:det}. The
antipodal case $u=N/2\equiv L$ reduces \eqref{eq:Du} to $D\propto a\,T_L+U_{L-1}$ (with $T_L$ the
Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind), recovering the reflection-symmetric problem as a special case
of the general rank-one theory. Equivalently, with the transient transition matrix
$P_\lambda=P_0-\lambda\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$, the total absorption current has the compact form
\begin{equation}
\tilde F_r(z)=z\Big[\lambda\bra{u}+\tfrac q2\big(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1}\big)\Big](I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},
\label{eq:genfun}
\end{equation}
whose two bracketed terms are the shortcut-capture and diffusive-absorption channels resolved in
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}. In the diffusive scaling the finite mass maps to a continuum density,
$F(t)\simeq(q/N^2)\,\Phi(qt/N^2)$ with $\tau=qt/N^2$.

\subsection{Diffusive limit}\label{sec:modelC}
Writing $\theta=u/N$ for the fractional shortcut position, $b=\beta(1-q)N/q$ for the rescaled sink
strength, $\tau=qt/N^2$ for diffusive time, and expanding $s=1-q+q\cos(2w/N)$, the walk converges to
Brownian motion on $[0,1]$ with absorbing ends and an interior $\delta$-sink [Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}(b)],
\begin{equation}
\partial_\tau p=\tfrac12\,p_{xx}-b\,\delta(x-\theta)\,p,\qquad p(0)=p(1)=0 .
\label{eq:continuum}
\end{equation}
Its spectrum is fixed by the transcendental determinant
\begin{equation}
D_\theta(k;b)=k\sin k+2b\,\sin(k\theta)\sin\!\big(k(1-\theta)\big)=0,
\label{eq:Dtheta}
\end{equation}
with $k=2w$ and eigenvalues $\mu_j=k_j^2/2=2w_j^2$; for $\theta=\tfrac12$ this collapses to
$\tan w=-2w/b$. This interval $\delta$-sink (point-interaction) eigenvalue condition is the standard
point-sink spectrum~\cite{Grebenkov2020,Bressloff2022}; our exact-solution novelty is not this spectrum
but the finite-$N$ directed-shortcut Montroll determinant $D_u$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:Du}] and the bifurcation
classification developed below. The FPT density from a start at $\xi=r/N$ is the signed spectral mixture
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\xi,\theta}(\tau;b)=\sum_j G_j\, e^{-\mu_j\tau},
\label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
whose amplitudes we derive from the defect resolvent (App.~\ref{app:amp}) as
\begin{equation}
G_{\xi,\theta}=2w^2\,\frac{\phi_{k,\theta}(\xi)\,I(k,\theta)}{J(k,\theta)},\qquad k=2w,
\label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\phi_{k,\theta}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\sin\!\big(k(1-\theta)\big)\sin(kx), & x\le\theta,\\[2pt]
\sin(k\theta)\sin\!\big(k(1-x)\big), & x\ge\theta,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
and $I=\int_0^1\phi\,dx$, $J=\int_0^1\phi^2\,dx$ have the closed forms of App.~\ref{app:amp}. Modes with
$\sin(n\pi\theta)=0$ do not couple to the sink and contribute the free Dirichlet term
$G_n^{\mathrm{node}}=n\pi[1-(-1)^n]\sin(n\pi\xi)$ (even $n$ vanish). We have verified \eqref{eq:G}, the
crucial normalization identity $J=\sin(k)\,D_k/(4b)$, and the full master curve (affected plus node
modes) against exact finite-$N$ residues to a relative error $\lesssim6\times10^{-5}$ (per-mode
$\sim2\times10^{-4}$) with clean $O(N^{-2})$ convergence (App.~\ref{app:amp}, App.~\ref{app:numerics}).

\subsection{Signed spectral moments and the fold criterion}\label{sec:existA}
Define the signed spectral moments
\begin{equation}
S_n(\tau;b)=\sum_j G_j\,\mu_j^{\,n}\,e^{-\mu_j\tau},
\label{eq:Sn}
\end{equation}
so that $\Phi'=-S_1$ and $\Phi''=S_2$ [differentiate \eqref{eq:Phi}]. We are precise about what folds.
Under the source-started convention $\xi=\theta$ the density carries a short-time shortcut-capture
feature---a boundary/initial-time mode whose weight vanishes as $b\to0$---together with \emph{interior}
finite-time extrema; an interior extremum is a zero of $S_1$. The finite-time (diffusive-return) peak
forms a valley--peak pair that is born or annihilated when the two interior extrema merge---a
\emph{saddle-node} fold of the density's critical points,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\,S_1(\tau_c,b_c)=0,\qquad S_2(\tau_c,b_c)=0\,},
\label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
with nondegeneracy $S_3(\tau_c,b_c)\neq0$ and $\partial_b S_1(\tau_c,b_c)\neq0$. No positivity is
assumed---the $G_j$ are genuinely signed---so \eqref{eq:fold} is the correct fold criterion for a
signed exponential mixture: the coincidence of a root and a critical point of the generalized (signed)
exponential sum $S_1$. The amplitudes $G_j$, and hence the boundary, depend on the start, so
$b_c=b_c(\theta;\xi)$; unless stated otherwise we report $\xi=\theta$. The local fold \eqref{eq:fold} is
exact; the global statement is \emph{numerical}: for $\xi=\theta$, numerical continuation of the
interior extrema gives a single connected two-mode window $0<b<b_c(\theta)$ with no re-entrant interval
over the scanned range (App.~\ref{app:numerics}). We use theorem language only for the local fold and
the exact finite-$N$ algebra.

\subsection{The existence boundary $b_c(\theta)$}\label{sec:existB}
Eliminating $\tau_c$ between the two equations in \eqref{eq:fold} yields a scalar boundary $b_c(\theta)$
[Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a)]. It is symmetric, $b_c(\theta)=b_c(1-\theta)$, has a minimum at
$\theta\simeq0.381$ ($b_c\simeq2.16$), and obeys the endpoint law
\begin{equation}
b_c(\theta)\sim\frac{B^*}{d},\qquad \tau_c\sim c_*\,d^2,\qquad d=\min(\theta,1-\theta),
\label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
with an independently resolved constant $B^*\approx0.78903$ (and $c_*\approx0.158$) from the half-line
boundary-layer problem of App.~\ref{app:existence}: bisection on the fold gives $b_c\theta=0.789026$
across $\theta\in[0.20,0.27]$ (six digits, no reference table); a closed form for $B^*$ remains open. In the antipodal case
\begin{equation}
b_c(\tfrac12)=3.07643,\qquad \tau_c=0.038363,
\label{eq:antip}
\end{equation}
independently certified to $10^{-6}$, with the near-antipodal expansion
$b_c(\tfrac12+\epsilon)\approx3.0764-133\,\epsilon^2$. The two limits are physically transparent: as
$b\to0$ the capture mode's weight vanishes and the density reverts to the single-peaked Dirichlet FPT,
while as $b\to\infty$ the $\delta$-sink becomes an absorbing cut and, for $\xi=\theta$, the density
collapses toward $\tau\to0$---so the interior second peak exists only in the finite window $0<b<b_c$.

\subsection{Normal-form scaling}\label{sec:existC}
Near the fold the two merging extrema split with the generic saddle-node exponents
\begin{equation}
\tau_+-\tau_-\propto(b_c-b)^{1/2},\qquad
\Phi(\tau_+)-\Phi(\tau_-)\propto(b_c-b)^{3/2},
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
which we confirm numerically [Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(c)], the fitted exponents being $0.50$ and $1.49$;
in the antipodal case the prefactors are $\tau_+-\tau_-\simeq0.025\,(b_c-b)^{1/2}$ and prominence
$\simeq0.36\,(b_c-b)^{3/2}$. The exponents certify the transition as a fold rather than a smooth
spectral crossover, and---being the generic saddle-node exponents---constitute a system-independent
experimental signature (Sec.~\ref{sec:physical}).

\subsection{Minimal mode count}\label{sec:existD}
A two-mode fold is impossible, even with signs. Writing $A_j=G_j\mu_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}$, $S_1=0$ forces
$A_2=-A_1$, whence $S_2=(\mu_1-\mu_2)A_1=0$ gives $A_1=A_2=0$, a contradiction. Thus \emph{at least
three} modes are required, and the fold ratio $A_1:A_2:A_3=(\mu_3-\mu_2):(\mu_1-\mu_3):(\mu_2-\mu_1)$
forces \emph{alternating signs} among the three participating contributions. The full fold is realized
by a many-mode signed cancellation; at the antipode this coincides with an alternating affected-mode
pattern ($G_1>0,G_2<0,G_3>0,\dots$), while away from the antipode the modal signs need not be strictly
alternating in eigenvalue order (App.~\ref{app:amp}). Retaining $K$ affected modes in the antipodal
case gives $b_c^{(2)}$ (none), $b_c^{(3)}=3.046$, $b_c^{(4)}$ (none), $b_c^{(5)}=3.0764$---the even-$K$
gaps reflecting the odd-mode parity of the alternating structure---converging to \eqref{eq:antip}.

\section{Universality}\label{sec:univ}

\subsection{Model-independence}\label{sec:univA}
The fold \eqref{eq:fold} is not special to the ring. For any absorbing Markov generator $Q_0$, a
rank-$m$ killing defect $Q_B=Q_0-UBU^\top$ gives, by the Woodbury identity,
\begin{equation}
\det(sI-Q_B)=\det(sI-Q_0)\,\det\!\big[I_m+BW(s)\big],
\label{eq:woodbury}
\end{equation}
with $W_{ab}(s)=\bra{u_a}(sI-Q_0)^{-1}\ket{u_b}$. For \emph{reversible} (symmetrizable) transient
dynamics with diagonal low-rank killing---as for the symmetric ring block here---$Q_B$ has a real
spectrum and the FPT density is again a real signed spectral mixture, whose second-peak birth/death is
the fold $S_1=S_2=0$. (For non-reversible directed transient dynamics the determinant identity still
holds, but the real modal expansion, and hence the fold classification, requires additional
hypotheses.) The Woodbury identity supplies only the finite-rank secular determinant; the universality
is the \emph{structural stability} of a generic extrema-pair fold (a codimension-one catastrophe of the
scalar density~\cite{Arnold1992}), not a consequence of the identity alone. Accordingly the double-peak
region is an \emph{open} set (structurally stable by the implicit-function theorem), and the honest
statement is not ``every shortcut creates a peak''---false in general---but ``whenever a
shortcut-induced peak is born or annihilated generically, it does so through a fold of the signed
spectral mixture, independent of the underlying geometry.''

\subsection{Multiple shortcuts and the cusp}\label{sec:univB}
For two shortcuts at $\theta_1=\alpha<\theta_2=\eta$ (positions; $\beta$ is reserved for the shortcut
rate) with strengths $b_1,b_2$, the secular determinant is (App.~\ref{app:cusp})
\begin{align}
D_{12}(k)={}&k\sin k+2b_1\sin(k\alpha)\sin\!\big(k(1-\alpha)\big)\notag\\
&+2b_2\sin(k\eta)\sin\!\big(k(1-\eta)\big)\notag\\
&+\tfrac{4b_1b_2}{k}\sin(k\alpha)\sin\!\big(k(\eta-\alpha)\big)\sin\!\big(k(1-\eta)\big),
\label{eq:D12}
\end{align}
the last term being a genuine two-defect interaction that vanishes as the sources coalesce (giving the
additive rule $b=b_1+b_2$). Two shortcuts can produce a \emph{third} FPT peak: at
$(\alpha,\eta,b_1,b_2)=(0.38,0.48,1.35,0.14)$, $\xi=0.463$, the exact ring ($N=1500$) shows three
peaks at $\tau\simeq3.0\times10^{-4},5.3\times10^{-3},6.1\times10^{-2}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(d)],
corresponding to capture via the near shortcut, capture via the far shortcut, and diffusive arrival.
The triple-peak wedge in $(b_1,b_2)$ is organized by a \emph{cusp} $S_1=S_2=S_3=0$; we locate it at
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(\alpha,\eta,b_1,b_2,\tau_c)=(&0.38,\,0.489580512568,\,1.35,\\
&1.08389,\,0.0012366),
\end{split}
\label{eq:cusp}
\end{equation}
with $S_4\neq0$ and unfolding Jacobian $\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)=-6.9\times10^7\neq0$, a
genuine codimension-two cusp (App.~\ref{app:cusp}). The triple-peak example and the cusp point are
\emph{distinct control values in the same rank-two family}---the example demonstrates a three-peak
region, while the cusp calculation locates a nearby codimension-two organizing point in the
$(b_1,b_2)$ unfolding at fixed geometry (they differ in $b_2$, $0.14$ vs $1.08$, not merely in $\eta$).
Thus $m$ shortcuts turn the single fold into fold hypersurfaces in $b$-space, with open regions of two
and three peaks organized by a cusp~\cite{Arnold1992}; the secular determinants and residues remain
finite-rank and explicitly computable, though the catastrophe locations are found numerically.

\subsection{Two dimensions}\label{sec:univC}
On an $L\times L$ torus with an absorbing target and a directed shortcut, the same algebra holds
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:woodbury} with the 2D lattice resolvent]. Numerically (at $L=31$), a single diffusive
peak at $\beta=0$ acquires a shortcut-induced capture peak; the two coexist over a window, and the
second (diffusive) interior maximum then annihilates by extremum merging near $\beta_c^{2D}\approx0.69$,
its prominence falling to zero between $\beta=0.68$ and $0.70$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(e,f)]---the same
fold morphology as in one dimension. One caveat is essential:
a single-site sink is marginal in 2D (its self-Green function is logarithmically divergent), so the
\emph{bare} threshold is expected to drift as $b_c(L)\sim1/\log L$; the large-$L$ continuum point-sink
limit therefore requires logarithmic renormalization, and we leave a size-independent 2D scaling test
(the analogue of \eqref{eq:scaling}) to future work. We thus present the 2D result as finite-lattice
evidence that the mechanism is not one-dimensional, not as a proof of 2D universality.

\subsection{Robustness}\label{sec:univD}
The fold persists under any perturbation that preserves the nondegeneracy $S_3\neq0$,
$\partial_b S_1\neq0$: to first order $\delta b_c=-(\partial_\varepsilon S_1/\partial_b S_1)\,
\delta\varepsilon$ for a perturbation $\varepsilon$. Concretely it survives weak hop-rate disorder
(which can even help, by lifting accidental dark modes), small shifts of the source or start
(Sec.~\ref{sec:univE}), and the laziness $q$, which is already scaled out of the reduced threshold and
reduced fold time (the raw step time obeys $t_c\sim N^2\tau_c/q$). It is destroyed by placing the source
on a symmetry node (dark modes $G_j=0$), by source coalescence (rank drop), or by moving the source
adjacent to the absorbing boundary. Recall that at $\xi=\theta$ the shortcut-capture feature is a
boundary/initial-time mode, so the interior fold concerns the finite-time diffusive-return peak; moving
the start off the gate turns the capture feature into an interior peak in its own right
(Fig.~\ref{fig:start}).

\subsection{Direct simulation, channel resolution, and start dependence}\label{sec:univE}
Four checks confirm that the fold is a physical mechanism rather than an artifact of the spectral
representation. \emph{(i) Direct simulation.} A direct Monte-Carlo of the lazy ring, using no spectral
input, reproduces the exact FPT density across four decades in $\tau$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(a)], with the
simulated splitting probability $\pi_{\rm sc}=0.5647\pm0.0008$ ($4\times10^5$ walkers) agreeing with the
exact $0.5652$. \emph{(ii) Channel resolution.} The absorption current splits exactly into a shortcut
entry ($\lambda$ at $u$) and diffusive entries ($q/2$ at the neighbors of $v$),
$\Phi=f_{\rm sc}+f_{\rm diff}$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(b)]: $f_{\rm sc}$ is monotone and carries the
short-time mass, while the late finite-time bump receives comparable contributions from both channels
(diffusive return and delayed capture). The fold thus annihilates the second arrival-time \emph{scale},
not a single pure channel. \emph{(iii) Non-antipodal robustness.} The interior fold occurs off the
reflection axis [Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(c)]: at $\theta=0.38$, $b_c(0.38)$ lies between the $b=2.16$ and
$b=2.8$ curves. \emph{(iv) Start dependence.} Releasing the walker away from the gate ($\xi\neq\theta$)
removes the boundary/initial capture mode: both features become genuine \emph{interior} peaks that
annihilate through the fold [Fig.~\ref{fig:start}(a)], and the threshold $b_c(\theta;\xi)$ varies
smoothly and symmetrically about $\xi=\theta$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:start}(b)], so the saddle-node is not an
artifact of releasing exactly at the gate. (Far from the gate the morphology changes and the simple
two-mode window is lost.)

\subsection{An independent continuum instance}\label{sec:univF}
Finally, the model-independence is realized directly, in a system that is \emph{not} the lazy ring. A
continuum Brownian-dynamics simulation of the delivery-gate protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:physB})---Brownian
motion on $[0,1]$ with absorbing ends and killing at rate $b/\ell$ inside a narrow gate of width $\ell$
at $\theta$, integrated by Euler--Maruyama with \emph{no lattice and no spectral input}---reproduces
both the exact $\delta$-sink density and the fold: released off the gate ($\xi=0.44$), it shows two
interior peaks below $b_c$ and one above [Fig.~\ref{fig:bd}], matching the exact continuum $\Phi(\tau)$
to histogram precision. This converts the Woodbury model-independence from an algebraic identity into a
demonstrated second instance, and confirms that the fold is not a lattice artifact.

\section{Physical realization and non-Hermitian character}\label{sec:physical}

\subsection{Embedding}\label{sec:physA}
Physically the shortcut is a localized target-delivery channel. From $p'(t)=Q_0p-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}p$
the absorption current is $f(t)=f_0(t)+b\,p_u(t)$: while the searcher is at $u$ it is irreversibly
delivered to the target at rate $b$. In the survival sector this is a local killing term
$-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$, not a transport edge, and a finite gate of physical width $\ell$ with local
trigger rate $\kappa$ coarse-grains to a $\delta$-sink. Restoring dimensions with domain length $L$,
diffusion constant $D$, and $t=(L^2/D)\,\tau$, the dimensionless sink strength is
\begin{equation}
b\;\sim\;\frac{\kappa\,\ell\,L}{D}\qquad(\ell\ll L),
\label{eq:nondim}
\end{equation}
so a laboratory quadruple $(\kappa,\ell,L,D)$ maps to a definite $b$; the smallest threshold
$b_c(0.38)\simeq2.16$ corresponds to a trigger rate $\kappa\simeq2.16\,D/(\ell L)$ at a gate placed at
$x\simeq0.38\,L$. The sharp physical statement is that a single localized delivery channel creates an
\emph{additional arrival-time scale}---a second interior peak in the full FPT density---above a
predictable threshold.

\subsection{Realizations}\label{sec:physB}
We rank candidate realizations by cleanliness. \emph{(i) Feedback-controlled colloidal or
active-particle search} is the strongest: a narrow gate at $x=\theta L$ (optical tweezers, a
microfluidic pulse, or active-particle feedback) that, upon entry, occasionally delivers the particle
to the target and ends the trial; $b$ is the per-frame trigger rate. Releasing the particle at the gate
($\xi=\theta$, the convention under which $b_c(\theta)$ is quoted), recording first-arrival times over
many trials, and scanning $b$ at fixed $\theta$---best near $\theta\simeq0.38$, where $b_c$ is smallest
and the effect largest---should reveal the second peak for $b<b_c(\theta)$ and its annihilation at
$b_c$, with the fold collapse \eqref{eq:scaling} as the sharp signature. A simulated experiment
(Brownian dynamics with counting noise) confirms measurability: the robust second-peak observable
$R=\Phi(\tau_p)/\Phi(\tau_v)$ (diffusive-peak over valley density) decreases through unity across the
fold, and resolving $b_c$ to $\pm0.1$ requires $\sim4\times10^4$ delivery-and-terminate trials
(App.~\ref{app:numerics}); the same holographic-tweezer/feedback stack that realizes controlled
first-passage and resetting experiments~\cite{TalFriedman2020} can implement the delivery gate. We
caution that standard resetting-to-origin~\cite{TalFriedman2020} is \emph{not} this problem: it
reinjects probability into the survival domain, whereas the correct protocol is
delivery-and-terminate---a gated detection that separates delivery times from bare first-passage
times~\cite{Kumar2023}. \emph{(ii) Engineered or small-world transport
networks} with a one-way express edge to an absorbing node realize $-b\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$ directly
(microfluidic maze with a one-way valve; programmable packet-routing graph; electrical random-walk
analog with a tunable shunt to ground; lossy photonic link); two such edges provide the cleanest test
of the rank-two triple peak and cusp. \emph{(iii) Intracellular motor capture} to a reaction site
realizes it in a coarse-grained, ideally reconstituted, in-vitro setting. In each case the caveat is
that only a genuinely one-way, terminating channel maps to the rank-one killing defect.

\subsection{Non-Hermitian character}\label{sec:physC}
We stress what the non-Hermitian character is and is not. $Q_\lambda$ is a passive open-Markov loss
defect, analogous to an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian with a localized absorber (imaginary
potential)~\cite{Ashida2020}; it is \emph{not} PT-symmetric, since there is no balanced gain and loss.
The survival-sector operator is itself self-adjoint in the one-defect model (the directedness enters
only the absorption vector), so ``non-Hermitian'' here denotes open-system probability loss, not a
spectral mechanism. Crucially, $b_c$ is \emph{not} an exceptional point: an EP requires eigenvalue
coalescence with a defective (Jordan) block, whereas we find numerically ($N=200$) that the eigenvalues
remain real, simple, and smooth through $b_c$, with consecutive gaps bounded away from zero, and the
fold arises from cancellation among at least three signed residues, not from spectral degeneracy. The
$(b_c-b)^{1/2}$ scaling in \eqref{eq:scaling} is a saddle-node of the scalar $\Phi(\tau;b)$, not EP
eigenvalue splitting---the same exponent for a different object. In the reversible diffusive limit the
$\delta$-sink operator is self-adjoint (Sturm--Liouville), so no spectral EP exists at all.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
We have shown that shortcut-induced FPT bimodality, long known phenomenologically, is the observable
signature of an exactly solvable saddle-node fold: a rank-one directed defect adds a finite-time
diffusive-return peak whose birth and death, for the source-started convention, occupy a single
numerically continued interval $0<b<b_c(\theta;\xi)$, with generic $(1/2,3/2)$ scaling, a minimal count
of three modes, a rank-two extension in which a cusp organizes a triple-peak region, and the same fold
morphology on finite two-dimensional lattices. A direct Monte-Carlo, an exact channel decomposition,
and the start-position dependence confirm the mechanism physically. The single most defensible novelty
is the bifurcation-theoretic classification with a predictive, computable threshold, distinct from the
known bimodality and from the underlying defect-resolvent method. The generic fold exponents and the
two-shortcut triple peak are directly measurable, on feedback-controlled colloidal search and
engineered transport networks.

Beyond the results verified here, the analysis leaves a small set of open questions. A fully rigorous
$N\to\infty$ statement requires, in addition to the standard convergence of fixed-mode residues
(App.~\ref{app:amp}), uniform $C^1$ convergence of the moment sums $S_{1,2,3}$ near the fold; we state
the precise hypotheses and support them by the observed $O(N^{-2})$ convergence, but do not prove
uniform $C^1$ control of the tail. The endpoint constant $B^*\approx0.78903$ is resolved numerically
(independently, to six digits) but lacks a closed form, the large-$L$ renormalized
scaling of $b_c$ in two dimensions is open, and whether $B^*$ and the antipodal $b_c$ admit elementary
closed forms is unknown. None of these affects the exact finite-system results.

\section{Exact finite-\texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} determinant and residues}\label{app:det}
We derive Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Du}--\eqref{eq:Ft}. Let $P_0$ be the $(N-1)\times(N-1)$ transient
(sub-stochastic) transition matrix of the lazy ring with $v=0$ deleted; on the interior sites it is a
symmetric tridiagonal path with diagonal $1-q$ and off-diagonal $q/2$. The directed shortcut diverts
$\lambda=\beta(1-q)$ from the self-loop at $u$ into the absorbing target, i.e.\ it subtracts $\lambda$
from the $(u,u)$ entry: $P_\lambda=P_0-\lambda\ket{u}\!\bra{u}$, the rank-one defect of \eqref{eq:Qb}.
The first-passage generating function to $v$ from a start $r$, $\tilde F_r(z)=\sum_{t\ge1}F(t)z^t$, is a
ratio of resolvent elements of $P_\lambda$; by the matrix-determinant lemma its poles are the zeros of
\begin{equation}
\det\!\big(I-zP_\lambda\big)=\det\!\big(I-zP_0\big)\,\big[1+z\lambda\,G^{0}_{uu}(z)\big],
\label{eq:mdl}
\end{equation}
with $G^0(z)=(I-zP_0)^{-1}$. Introducing the spectral variable $y=1+(z^{-1}-1)/q$ maps the lazy path
onto the standard second-difference operator: $\det(yI-\tfrac12A_{\rm path})=2^{-(N-1)}U_{N-1}(y)$ and,
by the cofactor structure of a tridiagonal inverse,
\begin{equation}
G^0_{uu}\propto\big[(yI-\tfrac12A_{\rm path})^{-1}\big]_{uu}=\frac{2\,U_{u-1}(y)\,U_{N-u-1}(y)}{U_{N-1}(y)} .
\label{eq:Guu}
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:Guu} into \eqref{eq:mdl} and clearing $U_{N-1}$ gives the characteristic
determinant $D_u(y)=a\,U_{N-1}(y)+2\,U_{u-1}(y)U_{N-u-1}(y)$ of \eqref{eq:Du}, with $a=q/\lambda$. The
residue of $\tilde F_r$ at each root $y_j$ produces the geometric sum \eqref{eq:Ft} with
$s_j=1-q+q\,y_j$ and $B_j=q\,\mathcal N_{r,u}(y_j)/D_u'(y_j)$. Writing $A_m:=U_{m-1}(y)$ (with the
convention $A_0=U_{-1}=0$), the piecewise numerator is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N_{r,u}=a\,[A_r+A_{N-r}]+2A_{N-u}[A_r+A_{u-r}],\qquad r\le u,
\label{eq:num}
\end{equation}
and its mirror ($r\leftrightarrow N-r$, $u\leftrightarrow N-u$) for $r\ge u$. Finally, by
Sherman--Morrison the eventual channel (splitting) probability---the fraction of trajectories absorbed
through the shortcut---is
\begin{equation}
\pi_{\rm sc}(r)=\frac{2\min(r,u)\,[N-\max(r,u)]}{aN+2u(N-u)},
\label{eq:pisc}
\end{equation}
which for the antipodal case reduces to $\pi_{\rm sc}=\rho/(a+L)$ with $\rho=\min(r,N-r)$ the start's
short-arc distance to the absorbing target. We
emphasize that $\pi_{\rm sc}$ is the eventual splitting probability, \emph{not} the mass under the
first peak; conflating the two is a common error we deliberately avoid. The two absorption channels of
Sec.~\ref{sec:univE} have the explicit generating functions
\begin{align}
\tilde F^{\rm sc}_r(z)&=z\lambda\bra{u}(I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},\notag\\
\tilde F^{\rm diff}_r(z)&=z\tfrac q2\big(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1}\big)(I-zP_\lambda)^{-1}\ket{r},
\label{eq:genchan}
\end{align}
with $\tilde F_r=\tilde F^{\rm sc}_r+\tilde F^{\rm diff}_r$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:genfun}]. Their $t$-domain
inverses $f_{\rm sc}(t)=\lambda\bra{u}P_\lambda^{\,t-1}\ket{r}$ and $f_{\rm diff}(t)=\tfrac
q2(\bra{1}+\bra{N-1})P_\lambda^{\,t-1}\ket{r}$ are the channel densities of Fig.~\ref{fig:sim}(b).

\section{Continuum amplitude from the defect resolvent}\label{app:amp}
We derive Eqs.~\eqref{eq:G}--\eqref{eq:phi} from the resolvent of $H_b=-\tfrac12\partial_x^2
+b\,\delta_\theta$ on $(0,1)$ with Dirichlet ends, continued to $z=-k^2/2$ (so $\mu=k^2/2=2w^2$).

\emph{1. Free resolvent.} With $\varkappa=\sqrt{2z}$ (a spectral variable, not the laziness $q$),
$R_0(x,y;z)=2\sinh(\varkappa x_<)\sinh(\varkappa(1-x_>))/(\varkappa\sinh \varkappa)$; continued to $\varkappa=ik$,
\begin{equation}
r_0(x,y;k)=\frac{2\sin(kx_<)\sin\!\big(k(1-x_>)\big)}{k\sin k}.
\label{eq:r0}
\end{equation}
\emph{2. Defect resolvent (Sherman--Morrison, rank-one $\delta$-sink).}
\begin{equation}
r_b=r_0-\frac{b\,r_0(x,\theta)r_0(\theta,y)}{1+b\,r_0(\theta,\theta)}
=r_0-\frac{4b\,\phi(x)\phi(y)}{k\sin k\;D_\theta},
\label{eq:rb}
\end{equation}
with $\phi=\phi_{k,\theta}$ of \eqref{eq:phi}; the pole condition $1+b\,r_0(\theta,\theta)=0$ is exactly
$D_\theta(k;b)=0$ of \eqref{eq:Dtheta}. \emph{3. Eigenfunction.} The residue eigenfunction is
$\psi_j=\phi_{k_j,\theta}$: it is continuous at $\theta$, satisfies the Dirichlet conditions, and obeys
the $\delta$-sink jump $\psi'(\theta^+)-\psi'(\theta^-)=2b\,\psi(\theta)$, which is algebraically
equivalent to $D_\theta=0$ (verified to $1.8\times10^{-14}$). \emph{4. Residue $=$ FPT amplitude.} The
survival transform is $\bar Q_\xi(z)=\int_0^1 r_b(y,\xi;z)\,dy$ and $\bar f=1-z\bar Q$; the residue at
$z=-\mu_j$ (using $k-k_j=-(z+\mu_j)/k_j$) gives the normalization-independent amplitude
\begin{equation}
G_j=\mu_j\,\psi_j(\xi)\,\frac{\int_0^1\psi_j}{\int_0^1\psi_j^2}
=\mu_j\,\frac{\phi_{k_j,\theta}(\xi)\,I}{J},
\label{eq:Gderiv}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ Eq.~\eqref{eq:G} with $\mu_j=2w_j^2$. \emph{5. Closed forms.} With $A=\sin k\theta$,
$B=\sin k(1-\theta)$,
\begin{align}
I(k,\theta)&=\tfrac1k\big[B\,(1-\cos k\theta)+A\,(1-\cos k(1-\theta))\big],\label{eq:I}\\
J(k,\theta)&=\tfrac12\big[\theta B^2+(1-\theta)A^2\big]-\frac{AB\sin k}{2k},\label{eq:J}
\end{align}
matching direct quadrature to $\sim10^{-9}$. \emph{6. Normalization identity.} At a root of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta},
\begin{equation}
J(k_j,\theta)=\frac{\sin(k_j)\,D_k(k_j;b)}{4b},
\label{eq:JD}
\end{equation}
with $D_k=\sin k+k\cos k+2b[\theta\cos(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))+(1-\theta)\sin(k\theta)
\cos(k(1-\theta))]$; this exact algebraic identity (verified to $2.9\times10^{-15}$) ties the residue
norm to the determinant derivative, and $D_k(k_j)\neq0$ certifies each affected mode as nondegenerate.
\emph{7. Antipodal reduction.} For $\theta=\tfrac12$,
$G_{\xi,1/2}=4w(1-\cos w)\sin(2w\xi^*)/[\sin^2 w\,(1+b(b+2)/4w^2)]$ with $\xi^*=\min(\xi,1-\xi)$,
recovering the previously verified antipodal amplitude. \emph{8. Node modes.} When $\sin(n\pi\theta)=0$
the mode decouples from the sink and is the free Dirichlet mode $\sin(n\pi x)$, with amplitude
$G_n^{\mathrm{node}}=n\pi[1-(-1)^n]\sin(n\pi\xi)$ (even $n$ vanish, and all node modes vanish at
$\xi=\theta$). The reconstructed master curve (affected plus node) matches exact finite-$N$ residues to
$\lesssim6\times10^{-5}$ (worst case in the early-$\tau$ tail; per-mode $\sim2\times10^{-4}$) with
$O(N^{-2})$ convergence.

\emph{The $N\to\infty$ limit.} Convergence of fixed-mode residues $G_{N,j}\to G_j$ follows from
norm-resolvent convergence of the killed generator under standard hypotheses (A) $\xi_N\to\xi$,
$\theta_N\to\theta$; (B) bulk convergence $H_N\to-\tfrac12\partial_x^2$; (C) $\delta$-sink form scaling
$V_{N,\theta}\sim bN$; (D) a symmetrizable transient generator; (E) simple isolated poles. Passing the
fold \eqref{eq:fold} to the limit additionally requires (F) uniform $C^1$ convergence of $S_{1,2,3}$
(including $\partial_b$) on a neighborhood $\tau\in[\tau_c/2,3\tau_c/2]$ of the fold---not down to
$\tau=0$, where the heat-kernel short-time singularity lives---for which a sufficient practical bound is
$\mu_{N,j}\ge cj^2$, $|G_{N,j}|\le Cj^r$ uniformly. Under (F), fold persistence is a consequence of the
implicit-function theorem: the Jacobian $\det DF_c=+S_{1,b}S_3$ is nonzero by nondegeneracy, giving a
unique nearby finite-$N$ fold with $b_{c,N}-b_c=O(\varepsilon_N)$, consistent with the observed
$O(N^{-2})$. We state (F) as a hypothesis, supported by the numerics, rather than a theorem.

\section{Fold criterion: proofs and asymptotics}\label{app:existence}
\emph{Fold criterion.} From $\Phi=\sum_j G_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}$, $\Phi'=-\sum_j G_j\mu_j e^{-\mu_j\tau}
=-S_1$ and $\Phi''=\sum_j G_j\mu_j^2 e^{-\mu_j\tau}=S_2$, so an interior extremum is $S_1=0$ and two
extrema merge at $S_1=S_2=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fold}. The parameter derivative of a root of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta} is $\partial_b k_j=-D_b/D_k$ with $D_b=2\sin(k\theta)\sin(k(1-\theta))$ and $D_k$ as
in \eqref{eq:JD}, which propagates into $\partial_b S_1$ and gives the nondegeneracy condition needed
for a structurally stable fold.

\emph{Minimal-mode theorem.} As in Sec.~\ref{sec:existD}, $S_1=S_2=0$ has no two-mode solution with
$A_j\neq0$; the minimal count is three, with the alternating-sign ratio
$A_1:A_2:A_3=(\mu_3-\mu_2):(\mu_1-\mu_3):(\mu_2-\mu_1)$. This retires an earlier (incorrect) two-mode
heuristic and shows the fold is intrinsically a many-mode cancellation.

\emph{Endpoint law.} As $d=\min(\theta,1-\theta)\to0$ the gate approaches the absorbing boundary.
Rescaling $y=x/d$ and $B=b\,d$ and taking $d\to0$ gives a half-line boundary-layer problem: diffusion
on $(0,\infty)$ with an absorbing end at $0$ and a $\delta$-sink of strength $B$ at $y=1$, started at
$y=1$. Its spectrum is fixed by a transcendental condition of the same $\sinh/\sin$ form as
\eqref{eq:Dtheta}, and imposing the fold $S_1=S_2=0$ in this limit selects a single strength $B^*$ and
reduced time, whence $b_c(\theta)\sim B^*/d$ and $\tau_c\sim c_*\,d^2$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoint}].
Resolving $b_c(\theta)$ \emph{independently} by bisection on the fold count---with the root search
extended to the high-$k$ fold mode ($w\sim1.78/d$), which the naive locator misses---gives
$b_c(\theta)\,\theta=0.789026$ stably for $\theta\in[0.20,0.27]$ and a linear extrapolation
$B^*\approx0.78903$ (with $c_*\approx0.158$); this reproduces the constant to six digits without any
reference table, refuting a circular check. Below $\theta\simeq0.15$ the fold mode is too high-$k$ for
clean resolution, and a closed form for $B^*$ remains open. Near the
antipode a regular perturbation of $\tan w=-2w/b$ yields
$b_c(\tfrac12+\epsilon)\approx3.0764-133\,\epsilon^2$, with the antipodal values \eqref{eq:antip} from
$S_1=S_2=0$ directly ($S_3\approx2.8\times10^5$, $\partial_bS_1\approx22$, nondegenerate). The truncated
thresholds ($K=2$ none, $K=3\to3.046$, $K=4$ none, $K=5\to3.076$) confirm rapid, parity-structured
convergence.

\section{Two-shortcut determinant and cusp}\label{app:cusp}
For two sources the secular condition is $\det[I_2+B\,W(k)]=0$ with $W_{ab}=r_0(k;\theta_a,\theta_b)$
the interval resolvent \eqref{eq:r0}; expanding the $2\times2$ determinant gives $D_{12}(k)$ of
\eqref{eq:D12}, whose cross term $\propto b_1b_2$ is the genuine two-defect interaction (it $\to0$ as
$\alpha\to\eta$, recovering a single defect of strength $b_1+b_2$). The rank-two residue
eigenfunction is $\psi_j(x)=-\sum_a b_a c_a^{(j)}r_0(k_j;x,\theta_a)$ with
$c^{(j)}\in\ker[I_2+W(k_j)B]$, and $G_j=\mu_j\psi_j(\xi)\int\psi_j/\int\psi_j^2$ with
$\int\psi_j=-\sum_a b_a c_a I_{\theta_a}$ and $\int\psi_j^2=\sum_{ab}b_a c_a b_b c_b\,C_{ab}$; the
constituents are $I_\theta$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:I}] and $C_{ab}=\int_0^1 r_0(x,\theta_a)\,r_0(x,\theta_b)\,dx$,
both elementary. The cusp solves
$S_1=S_2=S_3=0$ with $S_4\neq0$ and a nonzero unfolding Jacobian
$\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)$. At the located point \eqref{eq:cusp} an independent
reimplementation gives normalized residuals $\tau S_1/\Phi\approx7\times10^{-12}$,
$\tau^2S_2/\Phi\approx5\times10^{-11}$, $\tau^3S_3/\Phi\approx4\times10^{-10}$,
$\tau^4S_4/\Phi\approx-1.67$, and $\det\partial(S_1,S_2)/\partial(b_1,b_2)\approx-6.9\times10^7$,
confirming a nondegenerate codimension-two cusp.

\section{Numerical methods and reproducibility}\label{app:numerics}
Exact finite-$N$ FPT poles are computed from the symmetric transient block via \texttt{numpy}
\texttt{eigvalsh}, with amplitudes from the closed-form residues; high-precision cross-checks use \texttt{mpmath}. Continuum spectra are the roots of
\eqref{eq:Dtheta}/\eqref{eq:D12}, and amplitudes use \eqref{eq:G} and App.~\ref{app:cusp}. All scripts
are deterministic. Table~\ref{tab:verif} collects the verification tolerances quoted throughout.
The figures are generated by \texttt{dpma\_prr\_figures.py} (results) and \texttt{dpma\_schematic.py}
(model); the existence boundary by \texttt{dpma\_saddle\_node\_bc\_theta.py}; the amplitude/master
curve by \texttt{dpma\_general\_u\_master\_curve.py}; the fold scaling by
\texttt{dpma\_saddle\_node\_certification.py}; the cusp by \texttt{dpma\_cusp\_verify.py}; the rank-two
determinant and triple peak by \texttt{dpma\_multishortcut.py}; the 2D test by
\texttt{dpma\_2d\_universality.py}; the direct-simulation, channel-resolution, and non-antipodal panels
by \texttt{dpma\_channel\_mc.py}; the start-dependence panels by \texttt{dpma\_start\_dependence.py}; the independent Brownian-dynamics
instance by \texttt{dpma\_brownian\_fold.py}; the endpoint-constant resolution by
\texttt{dpma\_endpoint\_law.py}; and the measurability estimate by \texttt{dpma\_measurement.py}.
The 1D boundary $b_c(\theta)$ is obtained by bisection on the valley--peak-pair count over
$\theta\in[0.2,0.5]$, $b\in[0.5,20]$, retaining the affected roots with $k\le40$; over this range the
two-mode set is a single connected interval in $b$ (no re-entrant window). The 2D fold is computed at
$L=31$ by extremum merging (the second interior maximum's prominence $\to0$ near $\beta\approx0.69$),
not the ad-hoc valley heuristic. The Monte-Carlo uses $4\times10^5$ vectorized walkers with a fixed
seed, logarithmic $\tau$-binning, and shortcut-vs-diffusive absorption events recorded per walker; the
quoted $\pi_{\rm sc}$ uncertainty is the binomial standard error. \emph{Code availability.} All scripts
are deterministic and will be released with the paper (repository archive/commit hash to be added).
