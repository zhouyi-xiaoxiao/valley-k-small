I ran the real-z check you suggested. The direct benchmark I used was the finite
stochastic shortcut matrix itself. Starting from the lazy ring transition
matrix, I moved lambda = beta(1-q) from the u -> u self-loop to the directed
edge u -> v. I then computed the resolvent directly and evaluated the
first-passage ratio

F~_{n0}(v,z) = S~_{n0,v}(z) / S~_{v,v}(z)

for real values of z.

The result seems to locate the sign issue already in Eq. (29). If I use the
printed Eq. (29) literally, its ratio agrees with the original Eq. (32), so
Eq. (32) does not seem to be a separate algebraic slip by itself. However, both
the printed Eq. (29) ratio and the original Eq. (32) differ from the direct
stochastic shortcut matrix. So the issue does not seem to be the algebraic step
from Eq. (29) to Eq. (32), but the sign convention already used in Eq. (29).

If I instead use the stochastic shortcut sign in Eq. (29), the ratio agrees
with the direct stochastic matrix to roundoff, and it also gives the corrected
plus/plus version of Eq. (32).

The reason seems to be that the stochastic shortcut matrix removes probability
mass from the u -> u self-loop and adds it to u -> v. In I - zT, this gives the
opposite sign in the rank-one defect. The printed Eq. (29) therefore appears to
correspond to the opposite rank-one perturbation, rather than to moving
probability mass from u -> u to u -> v.

I attached a short PDF showing the benchmark definition and the numerical
comparisons. I also included the two control checks you suggested: Eq. (19) for
W~_{n0}(u,z) and Eq. (28) for W~_u(u,z). Both agree with the absorbing-matrix
calculation, so the discrepancy does not seem to come from those W inputs.

My current interpretation is therefore that the printed Eq. (29) has the
opposite rank-one defect sign for the stochastic u -> v shortcut interpretation.
