I went through the updated version and ran the finite-matrix checks for the
same stochastic shortcut interpretation.

My current reading is that the main resolvent sign has been corrected, but the
correction has not yet been propagated consistently into the first-passage
formula immediately after it. In particular, the equation labelled
first_pass_prob still has the old denominator sign, and the following equation
written using H has the opposite overall sign. Using the direct stochastic
matrix as the benchmark, the plus/plus version agrees to roundoff, while those
two updated displayed forms do not.

For the beta question with N=100 and q=2/3, I checked the fixed shortcut
u=6 -> v=56 with target v=56 and n0=1,...,6. With a clear visual double-peak
criterion, the double peaks occur for n0=1,2,3 up to about beta=0.03; I do not
see clear double peaks at beta_c = 0.04. If one only counts two local maxima,
the boundary is less strict, around beta=0.060--0.062 for n0=1,2,3, but the
valley is already very shallow.

I also checked the proposed condition s_1 = 1-q+q cos(2 pi/N). For the corrected
H expression, this equality does not seem to occur for finite positive beta in
the admissible range. At that gamma_1 value the phi term is zero, so the
denominator is still q/[beta(1-q)] > 0. Numerically s_1 stays above gamma_1
through beta in (0,1].
