In the attached note I followed the original route explicitly: I expanded W,
the no-shortcut first-passage term, and H into modes, performed the convolution,
kept the H(0) contribution separate, and wrote the corrected long Eq.
(41)-style expression using the resulting convolution kernels. I then
recompressed the same expression in the antipodal case and it reduces to the
closed form with denominator

    a T_L(y) + U_{L-1}(y).

This is why I think the closed form is the safer downstream replacement: it is
the simplified form of the original convolution calculation, after the
intermediate alpha/gamma/s terms have been recombined.

The figure in the updated PDF is still meaningful if interpreted narrowly as a
plot of the H-poles, i.e. the intersections with the corrected horizontal line
-q/[beta(1-q)]. But I do not think it can be used to infer a finite transition
at s_1 = 1 - q + q cos(2 pi/N). For the corrected denominator, the dominant
pole satisfies gamma_1 < s_1 < alpha_1 for finite admissible beta, so that
crossing does not occur.

For N=100 and q=2/3, the direct numerical checks are also included in the same
packet. Under the clear visual double-peak criterion, I see clear double peaks
up to about beta = 0.03 for n0=1,2,3, and no clear double peak at beta_c = 0.04.
