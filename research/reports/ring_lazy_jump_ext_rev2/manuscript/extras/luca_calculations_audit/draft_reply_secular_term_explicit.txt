Dear Luca,

Thank you for the message. I worked through the simple algebra for the
coefficient in front of t*alpha^t explicitly, and I have added it to the
corrected notes (main text p. 9 and the end of Appendix A.4), together with
the numerical checks. Let me summarise the few lines that decide it.

I agree that the diagonal convolution of two identical alpha_l modes
produces (t-1)*alpha_l^(t-2) terms - they are visible in my fully expanded
Eq. (58). The question is only the total coefficient that multiplies them.
Exactly two terms of the expansion carry that factor: the H(0)-diagonal term
and the j-summed diagonal term of the positive-time H-poles. Their total
coefficient is

    q f_l(u,v) g_l(n0,u,v) [ sum_j c_j/(s_j - alpha_l) - h0 ],
    h0 = H(0) = beta(1-q)/q .

The bracket is precisely H~(z) evaluated at z = 1/alpha_l. Since the killed
return function W~_u(u,z) has a simple pole at every z = 1/alpha_l (its
residue coefficient there is 2/N, never zero), the feedback denominator
1 + z beta(1-q) W~_u(u,z) diverges at that point, so

    H~(1/alpha_l) = 0    identically in N, q and beta,

equivalently, in partial fractions (s = 1/z):

    H_hat(s) = prod_l (s - alpha_l) / [ a prod_j (s - s_j) ]
             = 1/a + sum_j c_j/(s - s_j),

and setting s = alpha_m makes the left side vanish, giving
sum_j c_j/(s_j - alpha_m) = h0. So the coefficient in front of t*alpha_l^t
is zero for every l and every parameter combination, not only for special
ones: every t*alpha_l^t-type term the convolution can produce comes
multiplied by H~ evaluated exactly where H~ has a structural zero.

The plain alpha_l^(t-1) coefficients cancel in the same explicit way, so the
time-domain argument closes without reading poles off any collected
expression. The W-mode and off-diagonal contributions cancel pairwise by the
same sum rule, and the remaining diagonal piece vanishes by a second,
derivative sum rule,

    sum_j c_j/(s_j - alpha_l)^2 = -L/q        (independent of beta),

which follows from differentiating the partial fraction at s = alpha_m,
where D reduces to U_{L-1}(eta_m) and T_L' = L U_{L-1}. Combined with the
residue identity f_l(n0,v) = (N/2) f_l(u,v) g_l(n0,u,v), the total
coefficient of alpha_l^(t-1) is exactly zero. So every alpha-dependent term
of the expanded route cancels explicitly, and only the s_j^(t-1) modes
survive - exactly the closed form.

I can also say precisely where a nonzero coefficient comes from. If the
positive-time pole expansion H(t) = sum_j c_j s_j^(t-1) is also used at
t'' = 0 - which is what kernels of the form (s_k^t - x^t)/[s_k(s_k - x)]
do - then H(0) = beta(1-q)/q is silently replaced by sum_j c_j/s_j, and the
t*alpha_l^t coefficient becomes proportional to

    h0 - sum_j c_j/s_j = c_inf = T_L(1-1/q)/D(1-1/q)  !=  0 .

For N=10, q=2/3, beta=4/7 one has c_inf = 2/11 exactly: h0 = 2/7 = 0.2857...
while sum_j c_j/s_j = 0.1039... This formula reproduces the apparent
coefficient of the uncollected route to machine precision in my checks. So
the t*alpha^t term measures the H(0) bookkeeping replacement at the time-zero
boundary, not a property of the walk.

There is also a structural reason why no fine-tuning can be involved.
Because the shortcut is directed into the absorbing site, it only changes
one diagonal entry of the transient block: on the transient states the
matrix is symmetric tridiagonal with off-diagonal q/2, with 1-q replaced by
(1-beta)(1-q) at u. A symmetric Jacobi matrix has a complete eigenbasis and
simple spectrum for every parameter choice, so no Jordan block and hence no
t*lambda^t term can occur at all. Its spectrum is exactly {roots s_j of D}
union {gamma_r}; the gamma_r modes are antisymmetric under reflection and
carry zero amplitude in the first-passage probability, and alpha_l is not an
eigenvalue for beta > 0 because D(eta_l) = U_{L-1}(eta_l) != 0. I confirmed
this by exact diagonalization of the finite matrix in high-precision
arithmetic: the spectrum matches to ~1e-60, the exact first-passage
probability obeys F(t+1)/F(t) -> s_1 to 15 digits already by t = 200, while
F(t)/(t alpha_1^(t-1)) decays to zero (4.5e-45 by t = 3000), and the closed
form F(t) = sum_j B_rhoj s_j^(t-1) agrees with the exact finite chain to
1e-52 for all t up to 600.

Two further points are now proved rather than sampled in the revised notes.
First, gamma_1 < s_1 < alpha_1 holds for all 0 < q < 1 and 0 < beta <= 1 by
a sign argument: at the gamma_r locations U_{L-1} vanishes, so
D = a T_L = a(-1)^r != 0, which also shows that a crossing s_j = gamma_r is
impossible for any positive beta. Second, the dominant amplitude is strictly
positive, B_rho1 > 0 for every starting distance and all parameters, from
the compact form B_rhoj = q U_{rho-1}(y_j)[T_L(y_j)-1]/[T_L(y_j) D'(y_j)]
and the bracketing of y_1; so the dominant s_1 mode never drops out.
