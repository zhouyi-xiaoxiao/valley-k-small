Dear Luca,

Thank you - I agree completely that the diagonal convolution of two identical
alpha_l modes produces (t-1) alpha_l^{t-2} terms. Those terms really are present
in the expanded route; that is not where we differ. The question is only the
total coefficient that multiplies them after all diagonal contributions are
collected, and that coefficient can be computed with simple algebra in closed
form. I have now written this out explicitly in the attached revision
(Appendix A.4, "Explicit vanishing of the diagonal secular coefficient").

Collecting the only two sources of (t-1) alpha_l^{t-2} in the expanded
convolution route (the H(0) piece and the positive-time H piece) gives the
total secular coefficient

    K_l = q f_l(u,v) g_l(n0,u,v) [ sum_j c_j/(s_j - alpha_l) - beta(1-q)/q ].

The bracket vanishes identically because of the sum rule

    sum_j c_j/(s_j - alpha_l) = beta(1-q)/q     for every l = 1,...,L,

whose proof is three lines of partial fractions: with s = 1/z,

    H_hat(s) = prod_l (s - alpha_l) / [ a prod_j (s - s_j) ]
             = 1/a + sum_j c_j/(s - s_j),

and setting s = alpha_m makes the left side vanish because alpha_m is a zero
of the numerator. So K_l = 0 for every l and every (N, q, beta) with
beta > 0 - no special combination of parameters is involved. The mechanism is
structural rather than miraculous: H~ = [beta(1-q)/q] / [1 + z beta(1-q)
W~_u(u,z)], and the same W~_u(u,z) that supplies the alpha_l modes of
W_{n0}(u,t) and F_u(v,t) diverges in the denominator of H~, so H~ has a zero
at every z = 1/alpha_l simultaneously. The would-be double pole is reduced to
a simple pole by that zero, and the remaining simple-pole residue cancels
against the no-shortcut baseline via
f_l(n0,v) g_l(u,u,v) = g_l(n0,u,v) f_l(u,v).

I can also say exactly where a nonzero coefficient in front of t alpha_l^t
comes from if one finds one. Kernels of the form (s_k^t - x^t)/[s_k(s_k - x)]
correspond to using the positive-time pole expansion H(t'') = sum_k c_k
s_k^{t''-1} down to t'' = 0. But H(0) = beta(1-q)/q, while
sum_k c_k/s_k = beta(1-q)/q - c_inf with

    c_inf = T_L(1 - 1/q) / D(1 - 1/q)  !=  0.

Using the expansion at t''=0 therefore silently changes H(0) by -c_inf, and
the diagonal secular coefficient then comes out as q f_l g_l c_inf instead of
zero. For N=10, q=2/3, beta=4/7, c_inf = 2/11 exactly. In 50-digit arithmetic
the variant with the (s^t - x^t)/(s(s-x)) kernels deviates from the exact
finite shortcut matrix PMF by q f_1 g_1 c_inf (t-1) alpha_1^{t-2} (1+o(1)),
while the H(0)-separated route and the closed form agree with the matrix to
10^{-51} for all t up to 600. So the apparent log correction measures the
time-zero boundary mismatch of the H expansion, not the tail of F.

There is also a parameter-free way to see that no t lambda^t term can survive:
for t >= 1 the first-passage probability is a linear functional of powers of
the transient submatrix (all sites except v). That submatrix is symmetric -
the shortcut only lowers the diagonal entry at u, since the reallocated mass
exits through the absorbing column - hence diagonalizable with real spectrum
{gamma_r} U {s_j}, and alpha_l is not in the spectrum for any beta > 0 (the
roots s_j strictly interlace the alpha_l). A t lambda^t term would require a
defective eigenvalue, which a symmetric matrix cannot have.
