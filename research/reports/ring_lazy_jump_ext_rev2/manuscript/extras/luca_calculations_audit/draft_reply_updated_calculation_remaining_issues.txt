Before the shortcut section, most of the formulae seem to agree with
finite-matrix or finite-sum checks to roundoff. The only local points I noticed
are the compact generating-function summation range and two displayed

In the shortcut section, my reading is that the simplified absorbing resolvent
now has the corrected sign. However, when this is summed to obtain the
first-passage expression, the sign has not been propagated consistently: the
displayed first-passage formula still has the previous denominator sign, and
in the H notation the corresponding shortcut term should have the plus
prefactor. The direct stochastic-matrix check supports this: the corrected
plus/plus version agrees to roundoff, whereas the updated Eq. (32)- and
Eq. (33)-style forms do not.

For the symmetric antipodal case, if the corrected sign is used, the H(0)
contribution has to be kept separate from the positive-time pole expansion.
After the terms are recombined, the expression reduces cleanly to the closed
form with denominator a T_L(y) + U_{L-1}(y). For this reason I would not read
the final tail as a generic t alpha_1^t term from the updated long expression.

For N=100 and q=2/3, I checked the fixed shortcut u=6 -> v=56 and
n0=1,...,6. Under the clear visual double-peak criterion I used -- two
filtered local maxima, with the intervening valley at least about 20 percent
below the smaller peak -- clear double peaks remain for n0=1,2,3 up to about
beta=0.03. I do not see a clear double peak at beta=0.04. I also checked the
proposed finite crossing s_1 = 1-q+q cos(2 pi/N); in the corrected pole
equation this crossing does not occur for finite positive beta, with
gamma_1 < s_1 < alpha_1 instead. So I would treat the one-peak/two-peak
transition as a finite-time bimodality question, rather than as something
detected just from the asymptotic tail or from an s_1 = gamma_1 crossing.
